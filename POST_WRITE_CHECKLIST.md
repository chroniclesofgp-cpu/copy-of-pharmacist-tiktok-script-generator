# Post-Write Checklist
## RxContent — @pharmdgp
**Version:** 1.0 — Established July 19, 2026  
**Replaces:** The condensed 18-point checklist previously embedded in PRE_SESSION_BRIEF.md  
**Use in:** Every session — brief-only and full-docs alike. The checklist is short enough that it adds negligible token cost regardless of session type.

---

## How This Checklist Works

Every item requires a **specific answer** — not a checkbox tick. The goal is to force a genuine evaluation pass, not a rubber stamp. Where the item says *"quote the line,"* quote the actual line from the script. Where it says *"name the technique,"* name it. If you cannot produce the specific answer, the item has not passed.

Run this checklist on every script before delivery. It applies to every funnel stage and every hook type. Items marked **[REQUIRED]** are non-negotiable. Items marked **[STRONG]** apply unless there is an explicit documented reason to omit.

---

## SECTION 0 — HOOK VALIDATION

This section must be completed **before** the structural rules. A script with a weak or unanchored hook will underperform regardless of how well the rest of the script is written. The hook is the only element the viewer evaluates before deciding whether to watch.

---

### Item 0 — Hook Framework Validation [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every spoken hook must match one of the proven frameworks in HOOK_FRAMEWORKS.md. Cross-creator counts are sample-scoped: use **'Confirmed via direct transcript review in [N] of 5 creators' analyzed top-performing samples (90-day window).'** A single-creator hook is not weaker solely because it lacks breadth; evaluate its source quality, GMV, and structural fit. Generic labels, announcements, or self-introductions do not qualify as hooks.

**Interpretation principle:** Creator breadth and earning power answer different questions. A single-creator hook is not weaker solely because it is single-creator; expert-verdict is the documented rphreviews case study. Use the GMV-share column as context, not as a substitute for structural evidence.

**The two most common failure patterns (from the July 20, 2026 audit of 56 scripts):**

**Failure Pattern 1 — The Label Hook ("Here is my pharmacist verdict on [product]"):**
This announces that a verdict is coming. It does not deliver one. The transcript-verified rphreviews expert-verdict hook opens with the verdict itself; it is not currently a universal 5/5 pattern:
> ✅ *"This is the best [product] for [specific person or condition]."*
> ❌ *"Here is my pharmacist verdict on [product name]."*

The word "verdict" should never appear in the spoken hook. It belongs in the text overlay only, if used at all.

**Failure Pattern 2 — The Equivalence Hook ("same compound as [known product]"):**
Saying "this has the same compound as [Listerine / ibuprofen / a hospital drug]" without immediately establishing superiority positions the product as equivalent to something the viewer already owns or knows. The suppressed-knowledge hook must land as a reason to buy THIS product, not a reason to think it is the same as an alternative.
> ✅ *"Nobody talks about this — but this mouthwash has an ingredient that kills the bacteria Listerine misses."*
> ❌ *"Nobody talks about this — but the thymol in this mouthwash is the exact same compound that Listerine has used for decades."*

**The proven hook frameworks (from HOOK_FRAMEWORKS.md):**

|---|---|---:|---|

| Right-Way | Confirmed via direct transcript review in 3 of 5 creators' analyzed top-performing samples (90-day window); multiple examples retained | ≥10.1% documented lower bound from retained GMV examples | "If you're [doing X] and not seeing results — this is probably why" |

**The check — three questions to answer before marking this item complete:**
1. Which framework does this hook use? Name it.
2. Quote the opening line of the spoken hook. Does it match the proven opening pattern for that framework (verbatim or near-verbatim)?
3. Does the hook create a curiosity gap, call out a specific audience, or deliver a bold claim — or does it merely announce that content is coming?

**Answer required:** *"Hook framework: [name it]. Opening line: [quote it]. Matches proven pattern: [yes / no — if no, rewrite before delivery]. Curiosity gap / audience callout / bold claim present: [yes / no — if no, rewrite]."*

## SECTION 1 — STRUCTURAL RULES

These come from the 5-creator, 84-video synthesis in SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE.md. All are sample-scoped observations from the analyzed top-performing corpus; do not convert a 5/5 sample observation into a universal claim about all creator content.

---

### Item 1 — Product Reveal Timing [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** The specific product (brand name + product name) must not be revealed until 55–88% of the video's runtime has elapsed. Exceptions: Comparison hook (products named at 0:00 as the hook mechanism), transcript-verified symptom-checklist variant (product introduced approximately 65–75%).

**The check:** At what percentage of the script does the product name first appear? Calculate: (line of product reveal ÷ total lines) × 100. Does it fall within 55–88%? If not, is this a Comparison or Symptom Checklist script that qualifies for the exception?

**Answer required:** *"Product revealed at approximately [X]% of runtime. [Within range / Exception applies — hook type: ___]. For symptom-checklist, confirm the transcript-verified 65–75% variant or document the source for any different timing."*

---

### Item 2 — Framework-Specific Authority Before Product [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** The creator's credential must be established before the product is revealed in the way the selected framework requires. A visual badge, white coat, professional setting, credential proof, or framework-specific verbal line may do this work. A quantified verbal credential can strengthen a recommendation, but a generic “I’m a pharmacist” line is **not** a mandatory repeated sentence in every script.

**The check:** Identify the authority signal, its exact location, and why it fits this framework. Has the same verbal credential been repeated later without introducing a stronger claim?

**Answer required:** *"Authority signal: [quote visual and/or verbal signal]. Appears before product reveal or qualifies for the framework exception: [yes/no]. Repeated without a new authority job: [no / quote and cut]."*

---

### Item 3 — Education Occupies 55–75% of Runtime [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** The education section (mechanism + problem framing + proof) must be the majority of the video. The product pitch (reveal + proof stack + CTA) should occupy no more than 30–35% of total runtime.

**The check:** Estimate the line distribution across sections. Does the education section (Hook through Mechanism/Education) occupy the majority of the script?

**Answer required:** *"Education section estimated at [X]% of script. [Within range / Flag — pitch section is too heavy]."*

---

### Item 4 — No Specific Price [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** No dollar amount anywhere in the script. Use supply duration framing instead: "80-day supply," "3-month supply," "they're running a flash sale right now." Specific prices create hesitation and can trigger TikTok policy violations if the price changes after posting.

**The check:** Scan every line for any dollar amount ($X, X dollars, X.XX). Does any line name a specific price?

**Answer required:** *"No specific price found: [yes/no]. If yes, flag the line and replace with supply duration or deal framing]."*

---

### Item 5 — Scarcity in CTA [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every CTA must include at least one scarcity or urgency signal. Types: stock scarcity ("if you still see it in stock"), deal scarcity ("flash sale right now"), personal urgency ("I would not wait on this one"), season/timing ("before summer").

**The check:** Quote the scarcity signal in the CTA. Which type is it?

**Answer required:** *"Scarcity signal: [quote it]. Type: [stock / deal / personal urgency / timing]."*

---

### Item 5a — Approved CTA ID & Filming-Day Check [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every linked product script must select a CTA ID from `PHRASE_BANK.md` Section 8b before delivery. The CTA must create a reason to act now, match the available evidence, be the final spoken section, and land with one physical downward point.

**The check:** Quote the chosen CTA, identify its ID and urgency trigger, and confirm the filming-day check. `U` CTAs require a correctly linked observable cart. `P` CTAs require a live visible sale or bundle. `S` CTAs require documented historical sellout or current restock evidence. `C` CTAs require a genuine comparison decision.

**Answer required:** *"CTA ID: [U/P/S/C-##]. Urgency trigger: [conditional cart / live sale or bundle / historical sellout or restock / comparison decision]. Filming-day check: [quote the listing/cart evidence to verify]. Downward point is final action: [yes/no]."*

---

### Item 6 — Product-Specific Quality and Differentiator Proof [REQUIRED]

**Normalized threshold:** Include at least **2 distinct** quality or differentiator proof anchors. The title, rule, and answer format use this same threshold.

**The rule:** Every product reveal must be followed by at least two **distinct, source-verified reasons to choose this product**. Quality anchors may include made-in-USA, third-party testing, non-GMO, GMP, or free-from claims only when the exact variant/source supports them. Product formula, ingredient transparency, taste/format preference, preparation convenience, supply duration, and a directly verified review theme may also be stronger proof anchors.

**The check:** List each distinct product differentiator, its buyer payoff, and the proof source. Do not count a repeated dose, a repeated safety note, or an unverified product-line claim twice.

**Answer required:** *"Differentiator → buyer payoff → proof source: [list each]. Unique count: [X]. [Passes 2+ threshold / Flag — add a missing verified differentiator]."*

---

### Item 7 — PubMed Study Reference [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** At least 1–2 PubMed or clinical study references must appear during the mechanism section. The reference does not need to be readable on screen — the visual impression of a scientific paper is sufficient.

**The check:** Is at least one study referenced? Is it formatted as a clickable Markdown hyperlink per Rule G?

**Answer required:** *"Study referenced: [yes/no]. Clickable link format: [yes/no]. If no clickable link, flag and fix before delivery."*

---

### Item 8 — Visual Interrupt Cadence [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every 3–5 seconds, a new visual element must appear. The script must include specific on-screen text directions (text overlays, study screenshots, product images, review clusters) at regular intervals throughout.

**The check:** Are there on-screen text/visual directions in the script? Do they appear at regular intervals, not just at the beginning and end?

**Answer required:** *"On-screen visual directions present: [yes/no]. Distribution: [throughout / front-loaded / sparse — flag if sparse]."*

---

### Item 9 — Transition Phrases Between Sections [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every section transition must use a verbal bridge phrase. These phrases are micro-hooks — each one signals that the next section is coming and gives the viewer a reason to stay.

**Standard transitions:**
- Hook → Credential: *"And I know this because I've been a pharmacist for 17 years..."*
- Problem → Mechanism: *"And here's why that happens..."* / *"The reason is..."*
- Mechanism → Product: *"Here's my recommendation..."* / *"So what's the fix?"*
- Product → Proof: *"And the reason I love this one is..."*
- Proof → CTA: *"I'll link it below..."*

**The check:** Does each major section transition use a bridge phrase? Are any transitions abrupt (section ends, next section begins with no bridge)?

**Answer required:** *"Transition phrases present at all major section breaks: [yes/no]. If no, identify which transition is missing and add a bridge phrase]."*

## SECTION 2 — OBJECTION HANDLING

---

### Item 10 — Mechanism Objection Handled [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** The mechanism explanation IS the objection handle for "does this actually work?" By explaining how the ingredient works at a biological level, the creator eliminates the "is this snake oil?" objection before the viewer consciously raises it.

**The check:** Does the education section explain the mechanism at a biological level — not just what the ingredient does, but how it does it (receptor, enzyme, pathway, or process)?

**Answer required:** *"Mechanism explained at biological level: [yes/no]. Key mechanism line: [quote it]."*

---

### Item 11 — Safety/Interaction Note [STRONG — CAMPAIGN COVERAGE]

**The rule:** A real contraindication, interaction, or population-level caution is a genuine differentiator and deserves deliberate coverage in the campaign when the fact record supports it. It does **not** need to appear in every script. Assign it to at least one appropriate asset—usually a dedicated education, objection-handling, or caution-focused video—rather than bolting the same warning onto unrelated assets. Specific cautions signal genuine medical knowledge; generic disclaimers signal legal compliance.

**The check:** First audit the campaign plan: is every material product-level caution assigned to at least one suitable asset? Then audit this script: does it make a claim or give an instruction that specifically requires the caution? If not, do not force the caution into this script merely because it exists elsewhere in the fact record. When relevant here, use a specific, natural note naming the drug, condition, or population rather than a generic disclaimer.

**Answer required:** *"Campaign caution coverage: [asset/script reference]. Relevant to this script: [yes/no]. If yes, specific note: [quote it]. If no, omission is intentional because: [different asset covers it / this script makes no related claim]."*

---

### Item 12 — Empathetic Alternative for Safety Barriers [STRONG — SCRIPT-SPECIFIC]

**The rule (from SCRIPT_24 feedback log):** When **this script** contains a safety requirement that creates a realistic purchase or use barrier—such as SPF, timing, or dietary requirements—add an empathetic alternative that acknowledges real-world behavior. Example: *"Night use is also perfectly fine — and actually what I recommend if you are not consistent with sunscreen."* This preserves the safety message without turning unrelated scripts into walking disclaimers. A separate campaign asset may carry fuller caution education.

**The check:** Does this script contain a safety requirement that could deter purchase or use? If yes, is the barrier explained clearly and is a practical alternative offered? If no, mark N/A. Do not add an empathetic alternative solely because another campaign asset contains a caution.

**Answer required:** *"Script-specific safety barrier present: [yes/no]. Empathetic alternative: [quote it / N/A]. Campaign-level caution is covered separately when applicable: [asset/reference]."*

## SECTION 3 — SENTENCE-LEVEL WRITING STANDARDS

These rules come from SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE.md Part 11. All are [REQUIRED].

---

### Item 13 — Rule E: No Sentence Redundancy [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every sentence must either introduce new information or advance the argument. Restating a point already made — even in different words — is dead weight.

**The test:** *"Does this sentence add something the previous sentence did not already say?"*

**Common violation patterns:**
- Restating the mechanism after already explaining it
- Saying the same instruction multiple ways ("use SPF" → "not moisturizer SPF" → "dedicated SPF" → "SPF 30")
- Repeating the product's ingredient list after already naming them earlier
- Restating the hook premise mid-video after the education section has already made the point
- Repeating the same emotional beat in adjacent sentences using different words

**The check:** Read the GAP section and EDUCATION section line by line. Apply the test to every line. Quote any line that fails.

**Answer required:** *"Redundancy pass completed on GAP and EDUCATION sections: [yes]. Lines flagged: [quote any that fail, or 'none']."*

### Item 13a — Product-Advancement and Package Repetition Pass [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** The script must spend its time advancing the product-specific purchase case. Every spoken beat requires one primary job: recognition/pain, category insight, product differentiator, proof, objection resolution, retention, or CTA. Across a campaign, do not use the same safety note, credential phrase, competitor caveat, or product fact in every asset.

**The check:** Assign a primary job and a unique fact/payoff to each major spoken beat. Then compare every script with the rest of the package. Is a named comparator used only in the video where it actually teaches the decision? Is a safety note included only where it answers a real buyer question? Do multiple assets lead with separate purchase reasons?

**Answer required:** *"Beat ledger: [section → job → new fact/payoff]. Package repetition scan: [terms or concepts checked]. Repetitions removed or intentionally retained: [quote and explain]."*

---

### Item 13b — Sentence-Level Qualification [REQUIRED — LOCAL CLAIM ONLY]

**The rule:** Qualification applies to the **specific claim made in each sentence**, not to every caution in the product fact record. Where the evidence supports an ingredient mechanism or category effect but does not establish an unconditional outcome for the exact product, use natural qualification such as “may help,” “can help,” “helps support,” “is used to help,” or “has been studied for.” Do not import a population-level contraindication or unrelated safety warning into a sentence that makes no claim requiring it. Do not use qualification as defensive narration that removes the viewer payoff, mechanism education, buyer problem, proof, or framework mechanics.

**The check:** Identify each product-level and ingredient-level claim in this script. Flag only absolute outcomes, guarantees, or exact-product clinical implications that exceed the fact record. Separately confirm that campaign-level cautions are assigned to an appropriate asset; that is a campaign-planning check, not a reason to repeat every caution here.

**Answer required:** *“Claims requiring qualification in this script: [list]. Appropriate local wording present: [yes/no]. Unrelated population-level caution intentionally omitted: [yes/no/N/A]. Viewer payoff preserved: [yes/no].”*

### Item 14 — Rule F: Pain-First Mechanism Explanation [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** When explaining a complex ingredient or scientific mechanism, always lead with the viewer's lived experience of the problem before introducing the science. The mechanism must arrive as the answer to a question the viewer is already asking — not as a lecture they have to sit through.

**The test:** *"Does the viewer have a reason to care about this science before I explain it?"*

**The sequence that fails:**
> Ingredient name → mechanism name → what the mechanism does → who it helps

**The sequence that works:**
> Curiosity gap ("has something most products don't") → viewer's lived experience of the problem → mechanism as the answer → familiar analogy to anchor the science → benefit framed as "also does X" (bonus, not replacement)

---

**THE BENCHMARK — SCRIPT_19 COX/Centella Rewrite (June 30, 2026)**

This is the documented standard for what an excellent Rule F pass looks like. Use it as the reference when evaluating any mechanism explanation.

**Original (fails Rule F):**
> *"SKIN1004 is chemical sunscreen with Centella Asiatica. Centella contains natural COX inhibitors. COX is the same enzyme ibuprofen blocks. When the sun hits reactive skin, it triggers an inflammatory cascade. Centella interrupts that inflammation at the enzyme level."*

The ibuprofen bridge is correct and should be retained, but it arrives after the clinical term "COX inhibitors" — the rescue comes after the friction. The viewer who does not know what COX means has already started to disengage.

**Revised (passes Rule F):**
> *"SKIN1004 is chemical, but it has something most sunscreens don't — Centella Asiatica at 9,800 ppm. If your skin gets red or inflamed after sun exposure, that reaction is being driven by an enzyme called COX. It's the same enzyme ibuprofen targets. Centella blocks it naturally — so instead of just blocking UV, this sunscreen is also stopping the inflammatory response that UV triggers in reactive skin."*

**What changed and why:**
- **"But it has something most sunscreens don't"** — creates a curiosity gap before the ingredient name. The viewer leans in before the explanation begins.
- **"If your skin gets red or inflamed after sun exposure"** — the viewer's lived experience comes first. The mechanism is now the answer to their problem.
- **"It's the same enzyme ibuprofen targets"** — same bridge, but now arrives as a relief after the problem statement rather than a definition after a clinical term.
- **"Also stopping the inflammatory response"** — the "also" framing positions the bonus benefit correctly: this sunscreen does what all sunscreens do, plus this.

---

**The check:** Identify every mechanism explanation in the script. For each one: does the viewer's lived experience of the problem appear before the mechanism is introduced? Is there a curiosity gap or problem statement that makes the viewer care before the science arrives?

**Answer required:** *"Mechanism explanation(s) identified: [list them briefly]. Pain-first sequence confirmed: [yes / no — quote the violation and the corrected version]."*

### Item 15 — Rule 11: Viewer-Intelligible Language [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** No clinical jargon in spoken lines. Every mechanism must be explainable to someone with no medical background. Use analogies (ibuprofen, car engine, battery). If a term requires a definition, replace it with the definition.

**The documented example that established this rule (from Dr. Melaxin Multibalm Script 2, June 23, 2026):**
- Original: *"zero skin stimuli index confirmed"*
- Corrected: *"dermatologist-tested, confirmed safe for sensitive skin"*

The original is a clinical trial methodology term — no viewer knows what it means. The corrected version says the same thing in language any viewer understands.

**Additional examples of the violation pattern:**
- "COX inhibitors" → "the enzyme that ibuprofen blocks"
- "500 Dalton rule" → "collagen molecules are too large to absorb through skin"
- "VDR activation" → "activates the receptor that controls how your skin cells renew"
- "fibroblast stimulation" → "signals the cells that make collagen to get to work"
- "keratinocyte differentiation" → "how your skin cells mature and renew"

**The check:** Read every spoken line. Flag any term that requires a definition to be understood by a non-medical viewer. Is there an analogy or plain-English replacement for every clinical term?

**Answer required:** *"Clinical terms found: [list them or 'none']. Plain-English replacements confirmed: [yes / flag any that remain]."*

---

### Item 16 — Colloquial Pain-Point Language [STRONG]

**The rule (from Dr. Melaxin Multibalm Script 2 feedback, June 23, 2026):** Specific, colloquial pain-point phrases outperform clinical equivalents. Viewers comment and share when they recognize themselves in the specific phrase.

**Documented example:** *"turkey neck"* outperforms *"skin laxity on the neck."* The colloquial phrase creates a recognition moment — viewers comment "this is me." The clinical equivalent does not.

**The check:** Are the pain-point phrases in the script specific and colloquial, or clinical and generic? Would a viewer say this phrase to describe their own problem in a text message to a friend?

**Answer required:** *"Pain-point phrases: [quote the key ones]. Register: [colloquial and specific / clinical — flag for rewrite]."*

---

### Item 17 — Motivating Close vs. Timeline Hedge [STRONG]

**The rule (from Dr. Melaxin Multibalm Script 4 feedback, June 23, 2026):** Transformation scripts should end on a motivating close, not a timeline hedge. Both may be true; only one converts.

**The documented example:**
- Hedge close: *"It takes 4–8 weeks."* — ends on a caution.
- Motivating close: *"The people who commit to that timeline are the ones who see their skin actually change. And the hydration? You'll feel that much faster."* — ends on aspiration, creates an in-group, gives an immediate win.

**The check:** How does the script end? Does it close on a motivating statement, an aspiration, or an in-group framing? Or does it close on a caution, a hedge, or a timeline warning?

**Answer required:** *"Closing line: [quote it]. Type: [motivating / hedge — flag for rewrite if hedge]."*

### Item 18 — Rule G: PubMed Links Clickable [REQUIRED]

**The rule:** Every study reference in a script file must use Markdown hyperlink format. Plain text PMIDs are not acceptable.

**Required format:**
```
[PMID 12345678](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12345678/)
[PMC1234567](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1234567/)
```

**Why this matters:** The user screenshots study titles directly from the script file to use as video overlays. A plain text PMID requires a separate browser lookup step. A clickable Markdown link means one click to the PubMed page where the study title, journal name, and abstract are all visible and screenshottable.

**The check:** Scan the entire script for any study reference. Is every reference formatted as a clickable Markdown hyperlink?

**Answer required:** *"All study references clickable: [yes / no — list any plain-text PMIDs and fix before delivery]."*

**Answer required:** *"Declared funnel stage: [TOF/MOF/BOF]. Hook matches stage: [yes/no]. Education section matches stage: [yes/no]. Product reveal timing matches stage: [yes/no]. CTA matches stage: [yes/no]."*

### Item 20 — Verdict-First vs. Credential-First (MOF→BOF Expert Verdict Scripts Only) [STRONG]

**The rule (from SCRIPT_25 feedback, June 22, 2026):** For MOF→BOF expert-verdict hooks, leading with the verdict stops the scroll faster than leading with the credential. The credential belongs in the authority line as a reason, not as the opening statement.

**Documented example:**
- Credential-first (weaker for warm audiences): *"I've been a pharmacist for over a decade — and this is the dark spot toner pad I recommend."*
- Verdict-first (stronger for warm audiences): *"These are the best toner pads for dark spots or acne scars."* Then credential arrives in the authority line as the reason.

**The check:** If this is an expert-verdict script targeting a MOF or BOF audience, does the hook lead with the verdict or the credential?

**Answer required:** *"Script type: [expert-verdict / other — skip if other]. Hook leads with: [verdict / credential]. [Correct for audience stage / Flag — reorder if credential-first for warm audience]."*

## DRAFT-FIRST WORKFLOW — THIS CHECKLIST IS AN AUDIT, NOT A GENERATIVE TEMPLATE

Write the spoken script first in a natural, confident pharmacist voice using the approved framework, product facts, mechanism education, buyer problem, proof, and CTA. Do not write around checklist labels or pre-insert every caution simply to make an item read “yes.” Then run this checklist as a post-write audit. Repair only concrete gaps: unsupported or overbroad claims, missing product proof, a relevant script-specific safety barrier, redundant sentences, a missing campaign coverage assignment, or a structural failure. A caution that is not relevant to the script’s claim should be checked against the campaign plan—not force-fit into the spoken copy. The checklist must preserve decisive delivery and buyer payoff rather than generate self-narrating compliance language.
