# DryWater Complete Daily Electrolyte & Vitamin Powder — Campaign Proposal

**Status:** Draft campaign plan for user approval; **not** a script package.  
**Product:** [DryWater Raspberry Lemon, 12-stick TikTok Shop listing](https://shop.tiktok.com/us/pdp/1732265099914351437)  
**Prepared:** September 2, 2026  
**Required next gate:** User approval of the campaign sequence before any script is written.

> **Campaign thesis:** Do not sell DryWater as a universal replacement for water or as a treatment for dehydration. Build the campaign around a more useful, high-conversion consumer question: **when a person has a real heat, sweat, travel, or activity context for an electrolyte mix, which formula features matter—and which option fits their preference?** DryWater’s defensible product story is a portable, Raspberry Lemon packet with **1,000 mg potassium, 380 mg sodium, 0 g added sugar, and less than 1 g total sugar per stick**—not an unverified claim that the finished product produces “cellular hydration,” recovery, energy, cognition, mood, or a medical outcome.[1]

## 1. Campaign Decision Summary

The campaign should launch with **six decision/education assets** followed by the required **four-part BOF suite**. It uses the existing product-video market signal carefully: three of five directly opened exact-product candidates used a visual comparison treatment, but public views, complete playback, and performance data were unavailable. That supports **testing** comparisons; it does not establish that any product-specific format has already won.[2]

| Decision | Proposed choice | Why this is the best fit now |
|---|---|---|
| Core buyer problem | A person has a hot, active, travel, or outdoor-work day and wants an electrolyte mix that does not taste like a sugary sports drink. | This matches direct shopper language: less-sweet taste, easy mixing, stick-pack convenience, and outdoor/heat use.[1] |
| Core product story | Exact label proof: 1,000 mg potassium, 380 mg sodium, 150 mg chloride, 0 g added sugar, and a 12–16 oz mixing instruction. | This is direct Raspberry Lemon product-page evidence rather than a broad brand promise.[1] |
| Primary campaign engine | **Comparison and decision education.** | The category has obvious, recognizable viewer alternatives; comparison remains a strong MOF choice when each option receives a fair role and a clear decision rule.[3] |
| Expert-verdict scope | **“Best fit” for a narrow buyer:** someone choosing a no-added-sugar, high-potassium stick pack for an active/outdoor routine. | A category-wide “best electrolyte for hydration” claim is not supported by product-specific clinical evidence and would erase valid roles for water, higher-sodium mixes, and medical rehydration products.[1] |
| Ranking choice | Use **both** a numeric Category Scorecard and an F-to-S Tier List, but make their decision questions materially different and release them apart. | The numeric scorecard is a rapid product-choice test; the tier list is the slower category-education / late-reveal test. They are different executions, not duplicate scripts.[4] [5] |
| BOF suite | Include Types 1–4. Type 4 is **Path B label-criteria only** unless a filming-day search captures genuine counterfeit or seller-mismatch evidence. | The initial-suite requirement is met, while no honest competitor is falsely labelled fake or a scam.[6] [7] |

| Sodium is central to extracellular-fluid balance, while potassium has normal roles in cell function, nerve transmission, and muscle contraction. | A pharmacist can explain the reason to look at a formula’s sodium and potassium rather than making a vague “hydration” claim. | Nutrient/category evidence; it does not prove a DryWater-specific effect.[8] [9] |

| Direct buyer reports focus on less-sweet taste, mixability, portable packets, and outdoor use; taste remains variable. | This gives the campaign genuine sensory/convenience proof and an objection to answer without inventing testimonials. | Individual reviews are buyer reports, not efficacy evidence.[1] |

## 5. Why Both Ranking Formats Belong in This Campaign

The formats look similar only at a distance. Their conversion jobs, pacing, and evidence requirements differ.

| Format | Decision question | Delivery | Best use in this DryWater campaign | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Numeric Category Scorecard** | “Which named packet is the better fit for my no-added-sugar / less-sweet preference?” | Off-camera prompt → immediate 0–10 score → one concise reason; rapid, product-focused. | A compact 45–60 second decision video using four named packet alternatives. | A predictable worst-to-best countdown or a clinical-efficacy ranking.[4] |
| **F-to-S Tier List** | “Where do water, sports drinks, high-sodium mixes, and DryWater belong for different goals?” | Longer staged reactions; named options in deliberately non-sequential tiers; S is final. | A 60–90 second category-education asset that earns a final **target-buyer** S-tier recommendation. | “DryWater is best for every person or hydration scenario.” Each prior option must be given its valid role.[5] |

## 6. Comparison Decision Rule and Linking Plan

The strongest first comparison is **DryWater versus Original Liquid I.V.**, with no trashing. It is easy to recognize, the decision is familiar, and the contrast is honest: Original Liquid I.V. is a glucose-containing hydration-mix choice; DryWater’s exact Raspberry Lemon label has 0 g added sugar and 1,000 mg potassium per stick.[1] [12]

| Viewer priority | Decision rule to communicate | Link plan |
|---|---|---|
| Wants an everyday/short-activity drink and no special sweat/heat context | Water remains a reasonable baseline. | No product link required for this branch.[8] |
| Wants a glucose-containing original Liquid I.V. format | Present Original Liquid I.V. as a legitimate alternative, not a losing product. | Add its exact verified TikTok Shop cart **only if** seller, listing, product, and cart are current on filming day. |
| Wants a zero-added-sugar / high-potassium stick packet for active, outdoor, travel, or heat contexts | DryWater is the campaign’s intended recommendation. | DryWater cart only; verify seller, selected Raspberry Lemon variant, and current offer. |
| Wants a deliberately high-sodium, zero-sugar formula | LMNT is a distinct formulation choice, not a product to attack. | Do not co-link in the initial comparison; consider a later separate choice-tree asset only after current listing verification.[14] |
| Has acute fluid-loss or medical-supervision needs | Pedialyte’s need-specific role must be respected; no lifestyle mix should be positioned as a substitute. | No product-link recommendation in this campaign branch.[17] |

> **Recommendation:** Start the comparison with **one DryWater cart**. A second Liquid I.V. link is worthwhile only if the current TikTok Shop listing is live and the user wants a true two-product decision video. The campaign can be credible without a co-link; its strongest version teaches the viewer which profile they prefer, then links the product matching the campaign’s audience.

## 7. Expert Verdict: Recommended Framing

The expert-verdict asset should be strong but specific. The recommended framing is:

> **A pharmacist’s top electrolyte-packet pick for people who want a portable, no-added-sugar formula with 1,000 mg potassium for active or outdoor days.**

This is stronger and more honest than “the best electrolyte mix for hydration.” It directly names the buyer, their pain point, and the exact label difference that makes the recommendation relevant. It also retains room to explain that water, high-sodium formulas, glucose-containing mixes, and medical rehydration products can each have a role. The proof sequence should be **credential → real-use context → category education → exact Raspberry Lemon Nutrition Facts → physical preparation → quality/review proof → current cart**.[1] [3] [8]

### Mandatory filming-day checks

1. Confirm the **DryWater seller**, exact **Raspberry Lemon 12-stick** option, current cart, live price/offer, and visible rating/sold display.
2. Recheck the official Raspberry Lemon Nutrition Facts and capture a label/PDP image used on-screen.
3. For a dual-link Liquid I.V. comparison, verify its **exact formula variant**, seller, cart, price, and current panel. Do not treat Original and Sugar-Free Liquid I.V. as interchangeable.
4. For any Type 4 consumer-protection execution, capture genuine current TikTok Shop search results and official-seller evidence. Without actual counterfeits or seller mismatch, use the **Path B label-criteria** title and do not say “this is a scam” or “this is fake.”[7]
5. Use an appropriate current cart CTA only. Do not mention a sale, coupon, shipping offer, prior sellout, or exact price unless it is visible and current immediately before filming.[6]

## 9. Performance Learning Plan

Each first-wave asset should be logged at 48 hours and 7 days so the next DryWater scripts are selected from evidence rather than preference alone.

| Format group | Primary question | Compare at 48 hours | Decision rule for next batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water comparison vs Do/Don’t | Is context-first education a stronger cold-audience entry than direct product education? | 3-second hold, average watch time, 25% and 50% retention, saves, and comments asking “when should I use this?” | Produce the second version of the winning education structure. |
| Expert Verdict vs Liquid I.V. comparison | Does a narrow one-product recommendation or a side-by-side decision tree create more product intent? | Product-card clicks, attributed orders/GMV where available, comments naming preference/objection, and average watch time. | Expand the higher-intent style into a second use-case angle. |
| Numeric scorecard vs S-tier list | Does rapid verdict pacing or longer category-game anticipation win completion? | Completion rate, average watch time, replays, saves, and comments requesting other brands. | Preserve the winning format and use genuine viewer-requested alternatives next. |
| Type 1 vs Type 2 apology | Does short deal momentum outperform education-plus-proof with warm viewers? | Product-card click rate, orders/GMV, watch time, and drop-off before the offer reveal. | Keep the higher-converting apology length; refine only one variable at a time. |
| Type 3 vs Type 4 | Do practical-use education or formula-check criteria generate more high-intent questions and cart action? | Comments, saves, clicks, orders/GMV, and any wording confusion. | Use the winner for future comment-reply and BOF variants. |

## 10. Approval Decision Required

Before scripts are created, approve or revise the following campaign choices:

1. **Launch all ten assets** in the sequence above, including both the numeric scorecard and S-tier list; or reduce to the core six education/decision assets plus the four BOF assets.
2. Use **DryWater vs Original Liquid I.V.** as the first brand comparison, with a **single DryWater cart by default** and an optional second Liquid I.V. cart only after current listing verification.
3. Use the narrowed expert-verdict thesis—high-potassium / no-added-sugar stick packet for active or outdoor days—rather than a universal “best hydration” superlative.
4. Treat Type 4 as **Path B label criteria** unless live seller-mismatch/counterfeit evidence is captured on filming day.

No script prose, captions, CTA text, price language, or final product grades are authorized by this plan. Those are the next phase and will be drafted only after the user approves this campaign proposal and the live listing checks are repeated.
