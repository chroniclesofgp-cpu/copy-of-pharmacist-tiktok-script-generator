## SECTION 2 — UNIVERSAL HOOK RULES (Apply to Every Script)

1. **Product reveal timing:** TOF = final third. MOF = middle. BOF = within first 15 seconds.
2. **Triple hook required:** Every script needs a visual hook + spoken hook + text hook. Generate 3 text hook variants (question / provocative statement / stakes framing) — pick the best one before filming.
3. **Text hook disappears at 3–8 seconds** — never a persistent banner.
4. **Authority signal from frame 0** — white coat, diplomas, credential badge, or verbal credential in first 10 seconds. Quantified credential outperforms generic: "over a decade" > "I'm a pharmacist."
5. **Hook-first — no preamble.** The hook must be the very first words spoken. No "hey guys," no intro, no greeting before the hook. Start mid-thought.
6. **Education before product reveal** — mechanism/education always precedes the product recommendation.
7. **TOF curiosity loop structure** — product name/brand/visual NEVER appears until the final section. Structure: hook → open curiosity loop → close with education → open second loop → close with solution mechanism → THEN product reveal.
8. **Proactive objection handling** — address 2–4 objections before the viewer forms them.
9. **Approved urgency-first CTA at close** — choose a CTA ID from `PHRASE_BANK.md` Section 8b. Use conditional-cart FOMO by default; use sale/bundle or historical-sellout language only when the filming-day listing evidence supports it.
10. **Supply duration framing** — "4-month supply," "300 capsules." Never a dollar amount.
11. **Quality proof anchor** — "no fillers / third-party tested / made in USA" — verbal or visual.
12. **Physical downward point at CTA** — synchronized with verbal CTA. Non-negotiable. confirmed via direct transcript review in 5 of 5 creators' analyzed top-performing samples (90-day window).
13. **New product scripts include 3–4 PubMed studies.** Any script for a product not previously covered must include 3–4 verified PubMed citations with clickable links. Studies must map to specific claims in the script — mechanism studies for education, RCTs for gap/solution.
14. **Visual interrupt every 3–5 seconds.** The screen must never be static for more than 5 seconds during the education section. New text overlay, study screenshot, product image, or infographic must appear on this cadence.
15. **Transition phrases between every section.** Each section must open with a verbal bridge: Hook→Credential: "And I know this because..." / Credential→Problem: "And in my practice, I see this all the time..." / Problem→Mechanism: "And here's why that happens..." / Mechanism→Product: "Here's my recommendation..." / Product→Proof: "And the reason I love this one is..." / Proof→CTA: "I'll link it below..."

## SECTION 3 — SCRIPT ARCHITECTURE RULES (All Mandatory)

**Rule A — Hook must name the viewer's exact problem in the first 3 seconds.** The viewer must recognize themselves immediately. If they don't see themselves in the first 3 seconds, they scroll.

**Rule B — Authority signal before the mechanism.** Credential must land before any science is explained. The viewer must know why they should trust the explanation before they receive it.

**Rule C — Gap before product.** The gap (why what they're currently doing doesn't work) must be established before the product is revealed. Product is the answer to the gap — not a random recommendation.

**Rule D — Objection stack before CTA.** Address price, skepticism, and "I can get this anywhere" before the CTA. Sequence: social proof → quality proof → scarcity → CTA.

**Rule E — No sentence redundancy (MANDATORY PRE-DELIVERY CHECK).** Every sentence must introduce new information or advance the argument. Most common violation sites: timeline/expectation management in the GAP section, mechanism restatement in the BRAND CLOSE, benefit restatement in the CTA. Before delivering any script, do a final pass on every GAP, EDUCATION, and BRAND CLOSE section and cut any sentence that restates something already said.

**Rule F — Pain-first mechanism explanation (MANDATORY).** Sequence: curiosity gap → viewer's lived experience of the problem → mechanism as the answer → familiar analogy → "also does X" framing. The test: *does the viewer have a reason to care about this science before I explain it?* If no, add one sentence naming their problem before the science begins. Wrong order = viewer tunes out. Example: "COX is the same enzyme ibuprofen blocks" only lands after the viewer has been told their skin gets red and inflamed after sun exposure.

**Rule G — Prescription Feel Technique, applied at the correct scope.** Genuine contraindications, timing instructions, or side-effect warnings are valuable differentiators and should be deliberately covered in at least one appropriate asset within the campaign when supported by the fact record. They do **not** belong in every script by default. In each individual script, qualify the specific product or ingredient claim being made, and include a caution or practical alternative when that script introduces a safety barrier or gives an instruction that requires it. Do not force an unrelated population-level caution into a script merely to make it sound like a medical consultation. This preserves the authority pattern without creating walking-disclaimer delivery.

**Rule H — Bait-and-Switch Trust Architecture (for right-way and instruction-correction hooks).** Introduce the product early (0:07–0:15), then immediately back off — "I'll help you regardless of which brand you bought." Deliver 60–90 seconds of brand-agnostic education. Return to your specific product for the close. Dramatically lowers sales resistance.

**Rule 11 — Viewer-intelligible language.** No clinical jargon in spoken lines. Every mechanism must be explainable to someone with no medical background. Use analogies (ibuprofen, car engine, battery). If a term requires a definition, replace it with the definition.

**Rule 6 — Every specific number must have a source in the product intel doc.** Do not invent percentages, concentrations, or study results. If a number is not in the intel doc, do not use it.

**Rule 7 — Clickable PubMed links in all manually written scripts.** Format: [PMID XXXXXXXX](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/XXXXXXXX/)

## SECTION 5 — BUYER PSYCHOLOGY LEVERS (Condensed)

**TOF levers (cold audience — build awareness):**
- L1 Problem Amplification: name the problem before the solution
- L2 Identity Mirror: "if you have X, this is for you"
- L3 Authority Transfer: credential before mechanism
- L4 Curiosity Gap: open a loop the viewer must close by watching

**MOF levers (warm audience — build desire):**
- L5 Mechanism Revelation: explain why it works at a cellular/molecular level
- L6 Ingredient Superiority: this form > that form, this dose > that dose
- L7 Social Proof Cascade: units sold → reviews → creator testimony
- L8 Objection Neutralization: price, skepticism, availability — address before viewer forms them

**BOF levers (hot audience — convert):**
- L9 Scarcity Activation: stock-based only, never price-based
- L10 Risk Reversal: quality proof, third-party testing, return policy
- L11 Urgency Close: "I wouldn't wait on this one" — soft urgency, not manufactured panic

**Funnel sequencing rule:** Never use a BOF lever on a TOF audience. Never skip MOF levers when moving from TOF to BOF — the mechanism section is what earns the right to sell.

## SECTION 6 — VERBATIM PHRASE BANK (Most-Used Lines)

**Gap openers (use to introduce the problem before the solution):**
- *"Here's what nobody tells you about [ingredient]..."*
- *"The problem is, most [product category] on the market [specific failure]."*
- *"What I see constantly as a pharmacist is people taking [X] and not seeing results — and here's why."*

**Authority transitions:**
- *"I've been a pharmacist for over a decade and I counsel patients on this all the time."*
- *"As a pharmacist, this is what I actually recommend."*
- *"I'm not going to gatekeep this."*

**Quality proof anchors:**
- *"No fillers, third-party tested, made in the USA."*
- *"This is the form that actually absorbs."*
- *"Look at the label — [specific ingredient at specific dose]."*

**Rehook lines (use at 15–20 seconds to retain viewers who are drifting):**
- *"And here's the part most people don't know..."*
- *"But wait — because this is where it gets interesting."*
- *"So which one is actually better? I'll tell you in a second."*

**CTA close rule:** Select `U-01` to `U-04`, `P-01` to `P-03`, `S-01` to `S-02`, or `C-01` to `C-02` from `PHRASE_BANK.md` Section 8b before drafting the final section. Record the CTA ID, urgency trigger, and filming-day check in the script header and post-write checklist. The CTA must be final and synchronize with one downward point.

**Banned phrase:** "zero skin stimuli index confirmed" → replace with "dermatologist-tested, confirmed safe for sensitive skin"

## SECTION 7 — OVERLAY STRATEGY (POST-PRODUCTION NOTES)

**Every script ends with this block:**
```
---
POST-PRODUCTION NOTES

Overlay Strategy: [A / B / C / D / E]
Opening Hook Text: "[exact text, 8–12 words max, disappears 3–8 sec]" [or N/A]
Study Screenshots Needed: [PMID list or "verbal only"]
Checkmark Stack Items: [4–6 benefit lines or N/A]
Condition Images Needed: [describe images or N/A]
Special Graphics: [diagrams, charts, custom graphics or N/A]
A/B Test Variable: [what is being tested or N/A]
```

**Strategy selection (quick):**
- Fear/urgency angle, plain background → **Strategy A** (rphreviews standard — red banner + checkmark stack)
- Mechanism-heavy, science-forward, strong physical setup → **Strategy B** (Drew — PubMed wall)
- Multi-symptom, condition-based, review-rich product → **Strategy C** (Riva — review popups)
- Bundle/comparison, strong physical background → **Strategy D** (Naturo — credential badge + study wall)
- Short video, strong diploma wall + white coat → **Strategy E** (minimal — 1–2 studies + product label)

**Opening hook text formulas:**
- Strategy A: *"If you [have X / want X] — You MUST watch this video ⚠️"*
- Strategy C: *"[INGREDIENT/CONDITION IN CAPS] — [Consequence/Action] ⚠️"*

## SECTION 8 — CAPTION + HASHTAG RULES

**Caption:** Max 2 lines. CTA is acceptable but must be subtle ("definitely worth a try") — never transactional ("tap the orange cart"). Lead with the viewer's pain point or a curiosity gap, not the product name.

**Brand @mention:** Tag the brand account in the caption text when the product has a TikTok Shop presence (e.g., "@Medicube kojic acid deodorant is the one I recommend"). This notifies the brand and can trigger resharing. Confirmed: rphreviews tags the brand in ~80% of captions.

**Pharmacist disclaimer:** End every caption with: *"Just a quick disclaimer: While I'm a licensed pharmacist, this is not medical advice."* This is a trust signal, not just legal protection. Confirmed: rphreviews uses this in 100% of 34 analyzed captions.

**Hashtags:** Always exactly 5.
- 1 = brand name
- 1 = product or key ingredient
- 1–2 = viewer pain points
- 1 = broad reach (#pharmacist, #tiktokshop, #skincare, #supplements)

**Note:** rphreviews uses hashtags in only ~9% of captions; Riva ~27%. Both rely on natural-language SEO in the caption body. 5-hashtag rule is the safe default, but 0–3 is valid when caption body has strong symptom-specific language.

## SECTION 9 — SCRIPT QUALITY CHECKLIST (MANDATORY — BUILT-IN WORKFLOW)

**This checklist runs automatically after every script draft — the user does not need to request it.**

**The full checklist is in `POST_WRITE_CHECKLIST.md`.** That document is the authoritative source. Read it before running any post-write evaluation. The condensed list below has been retired.

The workflow is:
1. Write the spoken script in a natural, confident pharmacist voice using the approved framework and product evidence
2. Run the full 21-item checklist in `POST_WRITE_CHECKLIST.md` as a post-write audit against the draft
3. Each item requires a **specific quoted answer** — not a checkbox tick
4. Apply fixes for any failed items
5. Deliver final draft with the note: **"Quality checklist passed — 21/21"** (or list items flagged and how they were resolved)

The user will never receive a script that has not been audited against the full checklist. This was established September 5, 2026.

**Key rules in the checklist (summary only — full evaluation standards are in `POST_WRITE_CHECKLIST.md`):**
- Product reveal timing: 55–88% of runtime (exceptions: comparison hook, symptom checklist)
- Authority before product — quantified credential (years, certifications)
- No specific price — supply duration framing only
- Scarcity signal in every CTA
- Approved CTA ID, urgency trigger, filming-day check, and final downward point
- 2+ quality proof anchors after product reveal
- PubMed study referenced and formatted as clickable Markdown hyperlink (Rule G)
- Visual interrupt directions throughout script
- Transition phrases at all section breaks
- Rule E: no sentence redundancy — GAP and EDUCATION sections checked line by line
- Rule F: pain-first mechanism — viewer's lived experience before science (SCRIPT_19 COX/Centella rewrite is the benchmark)
- Rule 11: no clinical jargon in spoken lines — every term replaced with its plain-English definition
- Motivating close — not a timeline hedge
- Funnel stage calibration confirmed (TOF/MOF/BOF architecture matches declared stage)

## SECTION 9B — PRE-SCRIPT PRECONDITIONS (Before Writing Begins)

These must be confirmed before writing any script:

1. **Product intel doc exists** — do not write a script without at least a Light Protocol completed. If no intel doc exists, run Light Protocol first.
2. **TikTok handle verified** — the brand's official TikTok handle must be confirmed via direct TikTok profile lookup and stored in the intel doc as `**TikTok Handle:**` before any script is written. Do not guess handles from brand names. If the intel doc does not have a verified handle, verify it now and add it before proceeding. Use the handle in every caption @mention.
3. **Patented ingredient found?** → Full Protocol required before writing.
4. **All factual claims traceable** — every number, percentage, and mechanism claim must be in the intel doc. No claims from memory.
5. **Tiered ingredient naming confirmed:**
   - **Tier 1** (brand-promoted ingredients) → spoken on camera
   - **Tier 2** (patented or clinically studied ingredients) → spoken on camera — this is the pharmacist-only insight
   - **Tier 3** (supporting/barrier-repair ingredients) → text overlay only, not spoken
6. **Funnel stage confirmed** — know whether this is TOF, MOF, or BOF before choosing a hook. Never use a BOF hook for a TOF audience.
7. **Proven angles first** — always write the proven angle before testing open angles. TOF video must exist before MOF/BOF for a new product.

## SECTION 9C — FUNNEL STAGE ARCHITECTURE + RUNTIME TARGETS

| Funnel Stage | Runtime Target | Hook Timing | Education % | Key Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOF | 75–100 seconds | Symptom/problem in first 3s | 55–75% | Full mechanism + curiosity loop structure |
| MOF | 60–80 seconds | Can name ingredient directly | 40–55% | Why most products fail + Trojan Horse objection handle |
| BOF | 30–50 seconds | Deal/urgency focused | Minimal | Product reveal in first 30%, heavy CTA |
| BOF Suite Type 1 | 45–60 seconds | Apology-reveal hook | Minimal | Benefit stack 3 lines, stock scarcity CTA |
| BOF Suite Type 2 | 60–75 seconds | Apology-reveal + science | Low | One education sentence per benefit line |
| BOF Suite Type 3 | 60–80 seconds | Instruction-correction | Moderate | 2–3 protocol rules, pharmacist flag required |

**CTA structure (every funnel stage):** Social proof → quality proof → scarcity → downward point → link. The downward point + arrow graphic are non-negotiable regardless of funnel stage.

## Active Writing Safeguard

Use natural sentence-level qualification where appropriate: “may help,” “can help,” “helps support,” “is used to help,” or “has been studied for.” This safeguard must not replace the viewer payoff, mechanism education, buyer problem, proof, or proven framework mechanics.
