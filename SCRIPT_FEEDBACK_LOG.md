### Tier-List Comparison — Staging Correction — August 16, 2026

**What changed from the written scripts:** The prior Tier-List batch used generic S-tier wording and a predictable C → B → A → S progression. The corrected performance structure requires the off-camera person to name each actual ingredient, product format, or hero product; the on-camera pharmacist reacts and assigns the grade. The full F-through-S scale is available, intermediate grades must jump deliberately, and S is always last as the hero-product transition.

**Why it matters on camera:** Saying “S-tier product” removes the conversational reveal moment. Named prompts make each handoff intelligible and give the pharmacist a natural reaction beat. A scrambled ladder protects the payoff; viewers cannot predict the next grade simply because the sequence is climbing.

**Applied to:** Physician’s Choice Digestive Enzymes and Toplux Magnesium Complex Tier-List scripts. The permanent `tier-list-comparison` framework now contains the staged-reaction and unpredictable-ladder rules.

**Rule status:** Direct user-approved framework correction. Future Tier-List scripts must follow this execution without awaiting an additional performance test.

### cerave-invisible-mineral-miner-all.md — Pre-Film Script Review — May 2026

**Script status:** Reviewed and revised before filming. Changes made during script walkthrough — not yet filmed.

**What changed from the written script:**

- **Added Hook E ("Stop using sunscreen that looks like this") as the primary hook.** Added directly into the script body above the Authority section. This was evaluated against the original 3 hook variations and the year-framing variant ("It's 2026...") and ranked strongest of all five options. Reason: the visual demo (white-cast sunscreen on back of hand) does the work — the viewer sees the problem before you describe it. Shortest setup, widest audience reach, strongest scroll-stop.

- **Reordered the Education section** — moved the zinc oxide concentration range ("most mineral sunscreens sit between 10 and 20% zinc oxide") to the front of the section, before the scattering explanation. Original order: scattering mechanism first, then concentration context. Revised order: concentration context first, then scattering mechanism. Reason: grounding the viewer in a number before explaining the mechanism makes the mechanism more concrete.

- **Rewrote the DESM explanation.** Original: *"They also added DESM — a photostabilizer that prevents the mineral particles from degrading in UV light throughout the day. Most mineral sunscreens do not have this."* Revised: *"They also added a photostabilizer called DESM — most sunscreens don't have this. As zinc oxide absorbs UV, it generates free radicals — and free radicals damage and irritate skin. DESM intercepts those free radicals before they reach your skin."* Reason: the original described a chemistry problem (degradation) with no consequence for the viewer. The revised version names the consequence (free radicals → skin damage and irritation) and makes the mechanism feel personal rather than technical.

Reason noted in file: *"all sunscreens are broad spectrum so this is not unique."* Broad-spectrum is an FDA regulatory requirement — every sunscreen that makes the claim has passed the test. It is table stakes, not a differentiator. The section also repeated the "goes on transparent" point already established in the Miner-ALL explanation. Cut entirely with no replacement.

- **Rewrote the [PRODUCT INTRODUCTION] section to remove redundancy.** Original opened with *"This is the CeraVe Invisible Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50"* — redundant because the product had been named throughout the script. Removed the re-introduction line. Removed the standalone sentence *"This is not just a sunscreen. It is a sunscreen that also actively supports your skin barrier"* — this was a second recap line that followed the value-stack summary. Revised section now flows directly into the ceramides/niacinamide value stack and closes with a single payoff line: *"So you are not just blocking UV — you are getting photostabilization, barrier support, and anti-inflammatory protection in one formula."*

- **Removed the price from the Brand Close.** Original: *"it is $16.99."* Revised: *"it is affordable."* Reason noted in file: *"TikTok gives violations if the price ever goes back up."* Price-specific claims in TikTok Shop content can trigger policy violations if the listed price changes after posting.

- **Product visual timing updated (discussed, not yet reflected in filming notes).** Product should first appear on screen when you say "CeraVe solved this with Miner-ALL technology" — not held back until the Product Introduction section. The white-cast demo (hook) → product reveal at the Miner-ALL line → ingredient panel flip at the value stack is the correct visual sequence.

**What worked in the review:**

- The single payoff line at the end of the value stack lands harder than the two back-to-back recap lines in the original
- DESM rewrite makes a chemistry concept feel like a personal benefit — the free radical framing is accessible without being dumbed down

**What to watch on camera:**
- The "Stop using sunscreen that looks like this" hook requires a second mineral sunscreen with visible white cast for the demo — confirm you have one before filming, or fall back to Variation A
- The Product Introduction section still has the old standalone sentence ("This is not just a sunscreen...") left in the file below the revised value stack — this should be removed before filming or ignored on camera

**Audience reaction:** Not yet filmed — update when metrics are available

**Rule candidates:**
1. **Never include broad-spectrum coverage as a differentiator** — it is an FDA regulatory floor, not a product feature. Every sunscreen that claims broad-spectrum has passed the same test. Only mention it in a comparison context where one product lacks the claim.

## SCRIPT_19 — CeraVe vs SKIN1004 Sunscreen Comparison — Pre-Film Edit Log

**Date:** June 30, 2026
**Status:** Pre-film edit — not yet filmed

### Edit: COX / Centella Asiatica Section Rewrite

**Original version:**

> *"SKIN1004 is chemical sunscreen with Centella Asiatica. Centella contains natural COX inhibitors. COX is the same enzyme ibuprofen blocks. When the sun hits reactive skin, it triggers an inflammatory cascade. Centella interrupts that inflammation at the enzyme level."*

**Revised version:**

> *"SKIN1004 is chemical, but it has something most sunscreens don't — Centella Asiatica at 9,800 ppm. If your skin gets red or inflamed after sun exposure, that reaction is being driven by an enzyme called COX. It's the same enzyme ibuprofen targets. Centella blocks it naturally — so instead of just blocking UV, this sunscreen is also stopping the inflammatory response that UV triggers in reactive skin."*

**Why the original was insufficient:**

The original version introduced the mechanism before the viewer had a reason to care about it. The sequence was: Ingredient → mechanism → what the mechanism does → who it is for. This puts the science before the pain, which means the viewer is receiving a lecture before they feel the problem being solved. Even with the ibuprofen bridge (which was correct and retained), the clinical word "inhibitor" created friction before the rescue arrived.

**Why the rewrite works:**

1. **"But it has something most sunscreens don't"** — creates a curiosity gap before the ingredient name lands. The viewer leans in before the explanation begins.

2. **"If your skin gets red or inflamed after sun exposure, that reaction is being driven by..."** — puts the viewer's lived experience first. The mechanism is now the answer to their problem, not a science lecture. The viewer who has reactive skin hears themselves described and stays.

3. **"It's the same enzyme ibuprofen targets"** — the same bridge from the original, but now it arrives as a relief after the problem statement rather than as a definition after a clinical term.

4. **"Instead of just blocking UV, this sunscreen is also stopping the inflammatory response"** — the "also" framing positions Centella as a bonus benefit on top of SPF. This is the correct value architecture for a comparison script: you are not saying SKIN1004 is better, you are saying it does something additional that CeraVe does not. This keeps the comparison fair and non-salesy.

**Core principle identified (promoted to SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE and MASTER_CONTEXT):**

> **Pain-First Mechanism Explanation:** When explaining a complex ingredient or mechanism, always lead with the viewer's lived experience of the problem before introducing the science. The mechanism becomes the answer to a question the viewer is already asking, not a lecture they have to sit through. Never give the viewer a reason to scroll — curiosity about their own problem is the retention hook.

**Rule candidates:**
1. **Pain-First Mechanism Explanation** — promoted to SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE Rule F and MASTER_CONTEXT Section 9c. See those documents for the full rule definition.
2. **"Also" framing for secondary benefits in comparison scripts** — when one product has a bonus benefit the other does not, frame it as "also does X" rather than "is better because of X." Keeps the comparison credible and non-salesy.
