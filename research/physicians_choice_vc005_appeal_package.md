## Evidence Summary for Manual Review

The video used the linked Physician’s Choice Digestive Enzymes SKU and presented a tier-list comparison. The full visual audit confirms persistent `Results may vary` text from 00:00 through 01:08 and a standard pharmacist/not-medical-advice caption disclaimer. The notice identifies a misleading-functionality/effect concern, but the video contained no altered condition imagery, before/after material, diagnosis, cure, or explicit guarantee.

The official TikTok Shop listing and manufacturer page support the product-specific facts used in the appeal: 16 digestive enzymes, a probiotic/prebiotic blend that lists *Bacillus subtilis* BS50®, one-capsule mealtime directions, vegan/non-GMO positioning, and documented quality/manufacturing facts. [1] [2]

The on-screen study title accurately names a six-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of **BS50**, the ingredient listed in the product—not the complete commercial formula. The study found a statistically significant composite improvement rate for bloating, burping, and flatulence (47.4% vs. 22.2% placebo) and a significant individual burping result. It should be assessed as an ingredient study, not as a full-product efficacy trial. [3] [4]

The original script materials also included a separate BS50 **preclinical safety** paper. It reports genomic, antibiotic-susceptibility, and cell-culture analyses of the BS50 strain. It is included below for completeness and source consistency, but it does **not** establish digestive-symptom efficacy or validate the complete linked product. [5]

## Important Accuracy Boundary

The appeal above deliberately defends only verifiable context and does not claim that every broad statement in the video was independently validated. In particular, the BS50 paper did **not** test the full Physician’s Choice product and did not establish conventional statistical significance for individual bloating or flatulence. The live video’s comparison statements about all probiotics and simethicone were overbroad. Those issues should be corrected in a revised video, not restated in the appeal.

### Do Not Use a Slot for These Purposes

Do **not** upload a generic probiotic page as proof that “a probiotic does not help with immediate symptoms” or “does not help break down food.” Credible sources describe probiotic effects as strain- and context-specific, so they do not establish that categorical statement. Do **not** upload evidence to argue that simethicone “does nothing” for the cause of symptoms; authoritative sources support temporary symptom relief, not the universal negative statement. Those two comparison lines should be treated as overbroad in a future revision, rather than forced into an appeal-evidence theory.

### Reusable Future-Appeal Rule

For every statement challenged in a future appeal, build a source map with four fields before uploading: **exact statement; evidence source; source scope (linked product / ingredient / comparator / general background); and the claim the source does not prove.** Prioritize official live PDP/package images, then primary studies, then authoritative comparator references, then the original video/caption context. More attachments are useful only when they create a clean chain of evidence; unsupported or contradictory attachments weaken that chain.
