# External Viral List-Format Reference Notes

## Reference 1 — Facebook Reel 1241122267803302

- **Creator:** `therealbrianmark`
- **Observed duration:** approximately 28.9 seconds.
- **Visible opening:** large high-contrast title, “7 THINGS TO NEVER SAY IN SALES,” followed immediately by a red-versus-green two-column comparison labelled “Don’t say” and “Instead say.”
- **Caption CTA:** “Comment ‘SALES’ to get access to my free training on how to close more deals.”
- **Observed engagement:** approximately 7.4K reactions, 576 comments, and 658 shares on the public reel page.
- **Visual structure:** the creator speaks directly to camera while the audience can scan the complete red/green comparison grid. The large count, contrast colors, and parallel columns make the value proposition comprehensible before the spoken detail is processed.
- **Early adaptation hypothesis:** this is a **Do / Don’t Contrast List** format, not a standard ranking. For pharmacist content, the counterpart must be a safe educational correction or routine decision—not an instruction to stop prescribed treatment.
- **Verified spoken transcript pattern:** The creator delivers seven rapid paired corrections: “Never say [common phrase]. Instead say [specific replacement].” The exact pairs include “Let me know if you’re interested” → “give two options,” “I haven’t heard from you” → “It’s great to connect again,” and “Just checking in” → “I just had a minute to get back to you.” The structure ends with a follow/comment CTA for a deeper resource.

## Reference 2 — Facebook Reel 1019273693814974

- **Creator:** `therealbrianmark`
- **Observed duration:** approximately 92.8 seconds.
- **Visible opening:** “Rating top entrepreneurs on a scale of 1–10,” paired with a horizontal color-coded scale. The low numbers are green, the high numbers progress through yellow/orange to red, and the current #10 has a prominent marker/photo.
- **Caption prompt:** “Who would you put #1 🤔”
- **Observed engagement:** approximately 684 reactions, 29 comments, and 33 shares on the public reel page.
- **Visual structure:** the speaker remains central while the persistent numbered scale tells viewers that a ranking payoff is coming; rated people appear as small headshots progressively positioned above the scale. The caption, “Who would you put #1,” converts an inherently subjective ranking into a comment prompt.
- **Early adaptation hypothesis:** this is an **Ordered Countdown / Scale Ranking** structure. For pharmacist content, rank routines, ingredients, labels, product formats, or shopper mistakes rather than portraying a medical treatment as universally best.
- **Verified spoken transcript pattern:** The creator names one subject, immediately assigns a number, gives one concise personal rationale, then moves to the next. The closing line explicitly asks viewers to agree/disagree, comment, and name their own #1. The retention engine is not merely the scale; it is the unresolved #1 combined with an invitation to contest the ranking.

## Reference 3 — Facebook Reel 2629687567410812

- **Creator:** `therealbrianmark`
- **Observed duration:** approximately 9.25 seconds.
- **Visible opening:** “5 Viral Content Tools!!” with the caption “#5 is the best one.”
- **Visual structure:** an explicit reverse-list promise, with large vertical numerals 1–4 persistent on the left while the speaker delivers each short item. The viewer is told that the most valuable payoff is reserved for the final item.
- **Early adaptation hypothesis:** this is a **Reverse List / Best-Last** format. For pharmacist content, the last item may be the product only when it naturally delivers the previously named highest-value routine step or combines multiple earlier steps; the list must remain useful even if the viewer never buys.
- **Verified spoken transcript:** “Five content tools to help you go viral and make money. For content research, use ViralFinder. For content ideation, use ChatGPT. For content scripting, use Manus. For analytics, use the Edit app.” The creator states the broader five-item promise, but the recovered nine-second audio contains four named tools. The caption adds the best-last promise: “#5 is the best one.”
