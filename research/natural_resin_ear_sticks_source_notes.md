## Authoritative Safety Boundary

- The American Academy of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery advises against inserting objects into the ear canal because they can push wax deeper and cause injury. It advises medical evaluation for pain, drainage, bleeding, hearing loss, or fullness when the cause is uncertain.
- Mayo Clinic likewise advises against trying to dig out excessive or hardened earwax with objects because this can push wax farther in and damage the canal or eardrum.
- The underlying evidence-based guideline is Schwartz et al., *Clinical Practice Guideline (Update): Earwax (Cerumen Impaction)*, *Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery* (2017), PMID 28045591, DOI 10.1177/0194599816671491. It defines cerumen impaction as wax associated with symptoms or preventing ear assessment, and it identifies cerumenolytic agents, irrigation, or clinician-performed manual removal—not external resin sticks—as management options for impacted cerumen.
