# TikTok Shop Health & Beauty Compliance Preflight
**Status:** Interim control adopted after the August 16, 2026 four-video enforcement audit. This does not claim to identify TikTok’s proprietary automation; it applies the written policy to the recurring patterns actually found in the uploaded videos.

## Use Before Filming Any Linked Health, Supplement, or Beauty Product Video

| Gate | Required evidence | Stop filming when |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact product-claim map** | Copy the exact current PDP/package words supporting every claim, result, ingredient function, duration, certification, origin, and quantity stated in the script. | A claim is supported only by a study, general ingredient biology, another product, a retailer, or the creator’s professional judgment. |
| **No diagnosis or condition treatment** | Reframe to listing-supported appearance, routine, comfort, or general wellness language. | The script says or implies a viewer has a diagnosis, deficiency, cortisol issue, or condition; suggests replacing medication/care; or frames a product as treating/preventing/managing a condition. |
| **No inferred linked-SKU result** | State a true product attribute that matches the PDP/package. | The script turns an ingredient mechanism into “this product repairs/heals/replaces/prevents/blocks/stops/removes” a body outcome not stated by the product’s official materials. |
| **Direct outcome versus support wording** | Use a narrow general-wellness support phrase only when the current PDP/package uses that same support claim and it does not imply a diagnosis, treatment, prevention, symptom reversal, or guaranteed result. | The line says a viewer has a condition; names the cause; promises reversal or return of a function; or uses factual-outcome verbs such as “builds,” “stimulates,” “restores,” “lets you eat,” “fixes,” “stops,” or “corrects.” |
| **Credential separation** | The white coat may remain as established visual identity; a genuine pharmacist credential may be stated briefly and factually as background. | “My patients,” diagnosis/treatment language, or professional endorsement implication is used to make a linked product appear to treat a health condition. The coat itself is not identified by this audit as a standalone trigger. |
| **Visual integrity** | Use actual product, ingredient list, official PDP/package close-ups, and neutral routine shots. | Using a PubMed/article-title screenshot, condition/body-problem image, medical anatomy diagram, before/after implication, filter, AI effect, or altered image to make the linked product look like a treatment or prove a result. |
| **Research boundary** | Keep verified studies as clickable internal links for research and appeals. | Showing a study/article screen in TikTok Shop health/beauty content as apparent proof that the linked product delivers the claim. |
| **Results and timing** | Use only exact PDP/package-supported timing/results where policy permits; include a variation qualifier for genuine results/timing content. | Giving a clinical/result timeline from an ingredient paper, assumption, general category expectation, or different product. A disclaimer alone does not cure unsupported/medical content. |
| **Price and urgency** | Check the official cart immediately before filming; use only conditional availability: “If you still see the official cart, it is available now.” | Saying a price will increase, a product always sells out, it is the lowest/cheapest, or quoting sales/reviews without live confirmation and applicable conditions. |

## Allowed-Style Reframes

| Do not say | Use only if the exact official product materials support it | Prefer this if support is limited |
|---|---|---|
| “This product repairs your skin barrier.” | Exact brand wording that says “supports the skin barrier.” | “This formula includes ceramide NP; the official listing says it is designed with sensitive underarm skin in mind.” |
| “Kojic acid in this product prevents pigmentation.” | Exact product-specific brightening wording. | “The listing positions it for underarms that look dull or uneven.” |
| “Magnesium malate is for energy; taurate is for heart health.” | Exact product-packaging wellness wording, without added disease/clinical scope. | “This is an eight-form magnesium formula; check the label to see the forms included.” |
| “This lets you eat dairy without pain / fixes poor absorption.” | Never for linked supplement content unless the exact PDP/package uses that narrow outcome claim. | “The listing describes this as digestive-enzyme support for mealtime comfort.” |
| “This builds new blood vessels and regrows hair.” | Never infer this from an ingredient paper or mechanism source. | State only exact current PDP/package positioning, without a regrowth or blood-vessel claim. |
| “You have high cortisol / most people just need magnesium.” | Never for linked non-OTC products. | “If stress is affecting your routine, speak with a healthcare professional about what is right for you.” |
| “This is the pick for your bumps/irritation.” | Exact listing-supported consumer/cosmetic language. | “For viewers who prefer an AHA-free deodorant, this is one option to consider.” |

## Three-Level Claim-Language Triage

| Level | What it sounds like | Risk assessment | Required rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Direct factual outcome — highest risk** | “You have high cortisol.” “This makes it difficult for your body to absorb what you eat.” “Builds new blood vessels.” “Stimulates new hair growth.” “Lets you eat dairy without pain.” | States a diagnosis, cause, reversal, mechanism-to-result, or consumer outcome as fact. This is the language pattern most clearly represented in the five violation cases. | Do not use in linked health/beauty content unless the exact current PDP/package makes the same allowed claim; even then, do not pair it with diagnosis, treatment, result, or condition imagery. |
| **2. Qualified support wording — conditional** | “May help support…” “Supports…” “Can help with…” | More restrained, but still a functionality/effect claim about the linked SKU. Adding “may,” “can,” or “supports” does not create support on its own. | Use only when the exact linked PDP/package states an equivalent support claim. Keep it within general wellness or cosmetic scope; do not use it to imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or symptom reversal. |
| **3. Exact listing-supported positioning — lowest risk** | “The official listing describes this as…” “This is an AHA-free deodorant option.” “This formula includes the ingredients shown on its label.” “The listing says kojic acid helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone.” | Describes a verifiable product attribute, format, or brand positioning rather than asserting a consumer health result. | Quote or faithfully paraphrase the current PDP/package. Keep a dated screenshot in the filming package. |

> **Rule of thumb:** Changing “stimulates new hair growth” to “may help stimulate new hair growth” lowers the force of the sentence, but it does **not** make the statement automatically safe. The exact linked product must support that hair-growth claim, and the surrounding visuals cannot turn it into a clinical regrowth promise.

### Strength Rule — Be Precise, Not Vague

Compliance does **not** require replacing a verified benefit with empty language such as “tone care.” If the live PDP/package explicitly says a product “helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone,” the script may use that **same narrow wording** in the product reveal or benefit stack. If it says “promotes brighter, smoother-looking skin,” that exact cosmetic wording may be used. The claim map must show the source word-for-word.

The boundary is the source and scope: do not intensify a supported appearance claim into a pigment, diagnosis, repair, injury, medical-condition, or guaranteed-result claim. For example, a formula that merely lists ceramide NP may be described as containing ceramide NP; “supports the skin barrier” or “replaces the skin barrier lost by shaving” requires that the official product materials themselves support that precise effect. This keeps the script concrete and persuasive while preventing an ingredient paper or professional inference from becoming an unauthorized linked-SKU promise.
