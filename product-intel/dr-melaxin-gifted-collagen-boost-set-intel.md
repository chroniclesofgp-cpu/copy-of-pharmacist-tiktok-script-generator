## Mechanism Deep Dive

**REBORNIC Technology — The Unifying Thread**
All 5 products use REBORNIC technology (Calcium Gluconate + Vitamin D complex). The spicule-containing products (Cyano Pink Spicule Serum) use physical micro-channels to enhance penetration. The non-spicule products (Picotonic Shot, Calcium Cream, Eye Patch) use the calcium ion + VDR pathway without physical micro-channels. The set is designed as a layered protocol: spicule serum opens channels → ampoule delivers brightening actives → cream seals with collagen/elastin → balm provides on-the-go maintenance → eye patches deliver targeted calcium overnight.

**Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12) — The Most Underexplained Ingredient**
Vitamin B12 in skincare is almost never discussed. The mechanism: Cyanocobalamin reduces production of IL-31 (the primary cytokine driving itch signaling in atopic dermatitis and sensitive skin). It also supports mitochondrial function in keratinocytes and has been studied for eczema and psoriasis management. The pink color of the serum comes directly from the Cyanocobalamin — it is not a dye. This is a visual hook: the pink color is the active ingredient.

**Collagen Molecular Weight Story**
- Collagen Extract (intact/large molecule): Cannot penetrate the stratum corneum. Functions as a surface film-former and humectant. Provides amino acid building blocks at the skin surface.
- Hydrolyzed Elastin (small molecule): Penetrates deeper. Provides elastin-derived peptides that signal fibroblasts. Supports the elastic fiber network.
The combination addresses both surface hydration and deeper structural support — but a pharmacist should be honest that "collagen boosting" from topical collagen is primarily through the REBORNIC calcium → fibroblast pathway, not direct collagen absorption.

## Verified PubMed Studies

1. **Bissett DL et al. 2004. International Journal of Cosmetic Science. PMID: 18492135.**
   *Topical niacinamide reduces yellowing, wrinkling, red blotchiness, and hyperpigmented spots in aging facial skin.* Finding: Niacinamide provided significant improvements in fine lines/wrinkles, hyperpigmentation spots, texture, red blotchiness, and skin sallowness.

2. **de Miranda RB et al. 2021. International Journal of Dermatology. PMID: 33742704.**
   *Hydrolyzed collagen supplementation systematic review and meta-analysis.* Finding: Hydrolyzed collagen for 90 days is effective in reducing skin aging — reduces wrinkles and improves elasticity and hydration.

3. **Lee SE, Lee SH. 2018. Annals of Dermatology. PMID: 29853739.**
   *Skin barrier and calcium.* Finding: Calcium ions and their concentration gradient in the epidermis are essential in regulating keratinocyte differentiation, epidermal barrier homeostasis, and formation of the epidermal calcium gradient.

4. **Wawrzyk-Bochenek I et al. 2023. Journal of Clinical Medicine. PMID: 37048793.**
   *Kojic acid preparation significantly reduced hyperpigmentation in clinical measurement.* (Relevant for the Picotonic Shot's brightening mechanism context — niacinamide and tyrosinase inhibition pathway.)

5. **Stücker M et al. 2004. British Journal of Dermatology. PMID: 15149512.**
   *Topical vitamin B12 — a new therapeutic approach in atopic dermatitis.* Finding: Prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase III multicentre RCT. 49 patients, 8 weeks, intraindividual left/right comparison. B12-treated side: SCORAD dropped 55.34 points vs. 28.87 on placebo side — nearly 2x improvement (p < 0.001). Investigators rated B12 cream "good" or "very good" in 58% of cases. Mechanism: B12 is a nitric oxide scavenger; elevated NO drives eczema inflammation and itch signaling. **Use for:** Cyanocobalamin overlay in scripts targeting sensitive skin, eczema, or IL-31/itch mechanism. Script line: *"Published in the British Journal of Dermatology — B12 improved eczema severity nearly twice as much as placebo in a Phase III clinical trial."*

6. **Januchowski R. 2009. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. PMID: 19368512.**
   *Evaluation of topical vitamin B12 for the treatment of childhood eczema.* Finding: Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled intraindividual comparison. 21 children ages 6 months–18 years, 4 weeks. B12-treated skin improved significantly more than placebo at 2 weeks (p=0.02) and 4 weeks (p=0.01). Conclusion: "Topical vitamin B12 should be considered as a treatment option in children with eczema." **Use for:** Parent-audience scripts or comment-reply videos about sensitive/eczema-prone skin in children.

## Safety Flags and Honest Caveats

- **Spicule technology:** Temporary redness, tingling, or stinging on application — especially with the Cyano Pink Spicule Serum. Not for broken or irritated skin. Patch test required.
- **MELAZERO V2 / V-Peptide:** Proprietary brand names. The confirmed brightening mechanism is niacinamide. Do not repeat proprietary ingredient marketing as if it is clinically established.
- **Collagen topical claims:** Topical intact collagen provides surface hydration, not structural collagen replacement. The "collagen boosting" claim is more accurately attributed to the REBORNIC calcium → fibroblast pathway.
- **Eye Patch calcium concentration:** 1,000 ppm (vs. 10,000 ppm in the serum). Appropriate for the delicate eye area but lower concentration than the brand's headline REBORNIC claim.
- **Fragrance:** Picotonic Shot Ampoule contains Fragrance. Sensitive skin users should patch test this product specifically.
