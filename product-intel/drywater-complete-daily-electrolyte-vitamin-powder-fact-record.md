### Relevant scientific context and limits

The beverage RCT by Rodriguez-Sanchez and Galloway randomized **12 young and 11 older euhydrated men** to drink 1 L of water, fruit juice, sports drink, or low-fat milk. At three hours, sodium balance was negative after water and fruit juice but neutral after sports drink and milk; net potassium balance was neutral after milk but negative after water, fruit juice, and sports drink. The study did **not** test DryWater, Raspberry Lemon, the product’s potassium-heavy ratio, a 12–16 oz serving, routine daily use, athletes during exercise, or consumer outcomes. [PMID 37294517](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37294517/)

The exercise-hydration review describes sodium as central to extracellular-fluid balance and identifies water volume, activity type, heat, sweat loss, gastrointestinal tolerance, and individual factors as relevant to hydration planning. It also describes the evidence for extra potassium’s independent effect on fluid retention as equivocal. It is category evidence only. [PMCID PMC10781183](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10781183/)
