## 2. KEY INGREDIENT DEEP DIVE

### 5% AHA Blend (Lactic Acid + Mandelic Acid) — PRIMARY MECHANISM
- **What it is:** Alpha-hydroxy acids that chemically exfoliate the skin surface
- **Mechanism:** AHAs dissolve the bonds between dead skin cells on the surface, accelerating cell turnover. The visible darkening in underarms is largely dead pigmented cells sitting on the surface — AHA removes them.
- **Result:** Faster visible improvement in texture and tone vs. kojic acid (which works upstream on new melanin production)
- **Lactic acid specifically:** Also a humectant — attracts moisture while exfoliating. Gentler than glycolic acid.
- **Mandelic acid specifically:** Larger molecular size than lactic acid — penetrates more slowly, making it gentler and better tolerated. Good for darker skin tones (lower risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation).
- **The AHA + freshly shaved skin issue:** AHA on freshly shaved skin can cause stinging, irritation, and barrier disruption. This is the key pharmacist insight that differentiates Medicube (no AHA) from Truly Beauty.
- **Pharmacist-only angle:** "The 5% AHA exfoliates the dead skin cells where the visible darkening lives — that's why results appear faster. But AHA on freshly shaved skin can sting. This is not the pick if you shave frequently or have sensitive skin."

### Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)
- **Mechanism:** Inhibits melanosome transfer from melanocytes to keratinocytes — reduces visible pigmentation without blocking melanin production entirely. Also reduces inflammation and strengthens the skin barrier.
- **Synergy with AHA:** AHA removes existing pigmented cells; niacinamide reduces the transfer of new pigment. Complementary mechanisms.
- **Clinical evidence:** Well-established. Multiple studies show 4–5% niacinamide reduces hyperpigmentation.

### Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)
- **Mechanism:** Antioxidant that also inhibits tyrosinase (same enzyme as kojic acid, different binding site). Reduces oxidative stress that triggers melanin production.
- **Synergy:** Vitamin C + niacinamide + AHA = three simultaneous approaches to hyperpigmentation. This is a more aggressive multi-mechanism formula than Medicube.

### The AHA + Odor Control Connection
- **Pharmacist-only angle almost nobody covers:** AHAs lower skin pH. Odor-causing bacteria (Corynebacterium, Staphylococcus) thrive at higher pH. By lowering the pH of underarm skin, the AHA creates an environment that is less hospitable to odor bacteria. This is a secondary benefit of the AHA that the brand does not explicitly market.
- **On-camera line:** "The AHA doesn't just brighten — it lowers the pH of your skin, which makes it harder for odor-causing bacteria to survive. It's doing two jobs."

## 7. CONTRAINDICATIONS / INTERACTION FLAGS

- **Freshly shaved skin:** Do not apply immediately after shaving. Wait at least a few hours.
- **Sensitive skin:** AHA can cause irritation, redness, stinging. Medicube is the better choice.
- **Darker skin tones:** Mandelic acid is gentler than glycolic acid and lower PIH risk, but still monitor for irritation.
- **Retinol users:** AHA + retinol can cause over-exfoliation if used in the same area. Not typically an issue for underarms but worth noting.
- **No major drug interactions** at topical application levels.
