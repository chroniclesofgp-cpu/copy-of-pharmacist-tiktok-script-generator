## 5. Mechanism Summary (for script writing)

**How the 3-in-1 architecture works:**

1. **Digestive Enzymes (85 mg):** Secreted in the small intestine to break down macronutrients. When your body doesn't produce enough (stress, age, medications, illness), food sits in the gut partially undigested — causing bloating, gas, and discomfort. The 16 enzymes cover every food category: proteins, carbs, fats, dairy, beans, plant fiber, and sugar.

2. **SBO Probiotics (in 159 mg blend):** Bacillus species form protective endospores — like a shell around the bacteria. This shell survives stomach acid (pH 1.5–3.5) intact. Standard Lactobacillus probiotics are fragile and mostly die before reaching the colon. SBOs reach the colon alive, colonize, and produce short-chain fatty acids that feed the gut lining.

3. **Prebiotics (in 159 mg blend):** Inulin from Jerusalem Artichoke, Chicory, and Acacia fiber are fermentable fibers that feed the probiotic bacteria once they reach the colon. Without prebiotics, probiotics die off within days. This formula feeds them.

**The Beano Connection (pharmacist-exclusive):**
Alpha-galactosidase is the active enzyme in Beano (the OTC gas relief product). It breaks down oligosaccharides — the complex sugars in beans, lentils, broccoli, and cruciferous vegetables that the human body cannot digest on its own. This formula has it built in.

**The Lactase Connection:**
Lactase breaks down lactose (milk sugar). Lactose intolerance is caused by insufficient lactase production. This formula provides exogenous lactase — the same enzyme in Lactaid products.

## 7. Contraindications & Drug Interactions

### CRITICAL — Must include in pharmacist content

**1. Bromelain + Blood Thinners — MAJOR INTERACTION**
- Drugs affected: Warfarin (Coumadin), Eliquis (apixaban), Pradaxa (dabigatran), Xarelto (rivaroxaban), aspirin (daily)
- Mechanism: Bromelain has antiplatelet and anticoagulant effects — potentiates blood thinners
- Risk: Increased bleeding risk
- Pharmacist flag: *"If you are on a blood thinner — Warfarin, Eliquis, Pradaxa, or even daily aspirin — talk to your pharmacist before starting this. Bromelain, one of the 16 enzymes in this formula, can potentiate those medications."*
- Source: ConsumerLab, Healthline, PMC11243481

**2. Amylase/Glucoamylase + Diabetes Medications — MODERATE INTERACTION**
- Drugs affected: Acarbose (Precose), Miglitol (Glyset)
- Mechanism: Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors work by slowing carbohydrate digestion. Digestive enzymes speed up carbohydrate digestion — directly opposing the medication's mechanism
- Pharmacist flag: *"If you are on acarbose or miglitol for diabetes, these enzymes can work against your medication."*
- Source: RxList, MedicineNet

**3. Probiotics + Immunocompromised Patients — SERIOUS**
- Contraindicated in: Active chemotherapy, HIV/AIDS, organ transplant recipients on immunosuppressants
- Risk: Bacteremia/septicemia — documented case reports of Bacillus subtilis septicemia in immunocompromised patients
- Pharmacist flag: *"If you are immunocompromised — on chemotherapy, have HIV, or are on immunosuppressants after a transplant — do not take probiotics without talking to your doctor first."*
- Source: PMC4490230 (Doron 2015), PMC8706842 (Katkowska 2021)

**4. Antibiotics — TIMING INTERACTION**
- Antibiotics can kill the probiotic bacteria in this formula
- Separate by at least 2 hours from antibiotic dose
- Source: Standard pharmacology

### GENERAL SAFETY
- Well-tolerated by most people
- Some users notice mild GI changes in first 3–5 days (adjustment period — normal)
- Not recommended for children under 18
- Not recommended for pregnant/nursing without healthcare provider guidance
- GLP-1 users (Ozempic, Wegovy): No known direct interactions — generally considered safe, may help with GLP-1-related nausea/slow digestion
