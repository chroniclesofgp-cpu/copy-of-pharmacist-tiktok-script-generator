- Spore-forming Bacillus species (SBOs) have protective endospore shells

## Contraindications & Drug Interactions (COMPLETED)

**CRITICAL INTERACTIONS:**

1. **Bromelain + Blood Thinners (Warfarin, aspirin, Pradaxa, Eliquis)** — MAJOR
   - Bromelain has antiplatelet/anticoagulant effects
   - Can potentiate blood thinners — increased bleeding risk
   - Source: ConsumerLab, Healthline, PMC11243481
   - Pharmacist flag: "If you are on a blood thinner — Warfarin, Eliquis, Pradaxa, or even daily aspirin — talk to your pharmacist before starting this. Bromelain, one of the enzymes in this formula, can potentiate those medications."

2. **Amylase/Carbohydrate Enzymes + Diabetes Medications (Acarbose/Miglitol)** — MODERATE
   - Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors (acarbose = Precose, miglitol = Glyset) work by slowing carbohydrate digestion
   - Digestive enzymes with amylase/glucoamylase can counteract this mechanism
   - Source: RxList, MedicineNet
   - Pharmacist flag: "If you are on acarbose or miglitol for diabetes, these enzymes can work against your medication."

3. **Probiotics + Immunocompromised Patients** — SERIOUS
   - Bacillus species probiotics are contraindicated in severely immunocompromised patients (chemotherapy, HIV, organ transplant)
   - Risk of bacteremia/septicemia — documented case reports of Bacillus subtilis septicemia in immunocompromised patients
   - Source: PMC4490230 (Doron 2015), PMC8706842 (Katkowska 2021)
   - Pharmacist flag: "If you are immunocompromised — on chemotherapy, have HIV, or are on immunosuppressants after a transplant — do not take probiotics without talking to your doctor first. This applies to all probiotics, including this one."

4. **Antibiotics** — TIMING INTERACTION
   - Antibiotics can kill the probiotic bacteria
   - Separate by at least 2 hours from antibiotic dose
   - Source: Standard pharmacology

**GENERAL SAFETY:**
- Well-tolerated by most people
- Some users notice mild GI changes in first few days (adjustment period)
- Not recommended for children under 18
- Not recommended for pregnant/nursing without healthcare provider guidance
- GLP-1 users (Ozempic, Wegovy): No known direct interactions, but digestive enzyme use with GLP-1 is an emerging area — generally considered safe

**PHARMACIST CREDIBILITY OPPORTUNITY:**
The blood thinner interaction is the pharmacist-exclusive flag that no lifestyle creator will mention. This is the line that makes the video feel like a pharmacist made it, not a brand partner.
