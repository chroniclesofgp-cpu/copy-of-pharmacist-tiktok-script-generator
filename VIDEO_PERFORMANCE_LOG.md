# Video Performance Log
## RxContent — @pharmdgp

**Purpose:** Track every posted TikTok video with its script, hook type, product, and key metrics. Over time this tells us which hooks convert, which products perform, and which angles are worth doubling down on.

**How to use:** After posting a video, share the TikTok video URL + a screenshot of TikTok Analytics (views, watch time %, average watch time in seconds, saves, shares, comments) + a screenshot of the Creator Center affiliate dashboard showing GMV. The agent will map the numbers to the diagnostic framework, identify the drop-off point, and add the entry to this log with a specific action recommendation.

**Review cadence:** After every 10 posted videos, the agent will review the full log and add a summary note to the Patterns section at the bottom.

**Full framework:** See `VIDEO_PERFORMANCE_ANALYSIS_FRAMEWORK.md` for complete metric definitions, benchmarks, diagnostic patterns, and how performance data feeds back into script writing.

**CTA experiment capture:** For every new linked video, record the CTA ID, urgency trigger, and filming-day check selected from `PHRASE_BANK.md` Section 8b. This makes CTA urgency testable alongside hook type, watch time, saves, followers, and GMV.

**Connection to other documents:**
- Delivery and on-camera feedback → `SCRIPT_FEEDBACK_LOG.md`
- Patterns that affect future scripts → `MASTER_CONTEXT.md` Section 9
- Script iteration and pipeline decisions → `CONTENT_PIPELINE.md`

## Patterns and Insights

**First 22-Video Review — June 9, 2026**

**Pattern 1 — Hook failure is the universal problem across Videos 12–22.**
Every video with available analytics shows watch time under 15% (the "Problem" threshold for 60–120s videos). The average watch time across Videos 12–22 is approximately 6.5 seconds. This is not a delivery problem or a content quality problem — the delivery is strong across the board. This is a hook structure problem. The hooks are not creating enough urgency or emotional connection in the first 3 seconds to hold viewers through the credential section.

**Pattern 2 — The "right way" hook formula is consistently failing.**
Videos 15 ("Here's how to take caffeine the right way") and 20 ("Here's how to take creatine the right way") both produced 7–9% watch time. This generic instruction/correction formula does not create urgency. The specific pain point must be named in the first 3 seconds, not implied.

**Pattern 3 — Product-owner hooks are failing on cold traffic.**
Videos 13 ("If you've tried Medicube's deodorant and are not seeing results") and 21 ("You're probably taking your magnesium supplement all wrong") both failed because they assume the viewer already owns the product. TikTok cold traffic has not heard of these products. The hook must address the pain point, not the product.

**Pattern 4 — The symptom checklist hook drives the most distribution.**
Video 18 (12,159 views) used the most direct symptom checklist in the batch: "Double chin, belly fat, love handles, you're not just eating too much." This drove 8–24x more views than the baseline. The "you're not just eating too much" reframe is the key line — it removes blame and creates hope. This exact structure should be replicated for every symptom-based product.

**Pattern 5 — The comparison hook + conditional close is the proven structure for comparison videos.**
Video 7 (8,675 views) used the comparison hook with an explicit "who should buy which" conditional close. This is the only comparison video with a conditional close, and it is the breakout. Videos 5, 6, 8, and 11 used comparison hooks without the conditional close and likely performed at baseline. The conditional close is the differentiating element.

**Pattern 6 — Watch time is weak despite strong delivery.**
The delivery quality across all 22 videos is high — confident, authoritative, well-paced, strong overlays, good science. The problem is not how the content is delivered. The problem is that the hook is not creating enough urgency to hold viewers through the first 10 seconds. This means the fix is in the script (hook rewrite), not in the filming (delivery coaching).

**Pattern 7 — Instruction/correction and product-owner hooks are not retired — they need sharper executions.**
These hook types are proven converters for other healthcare creators and are used daily by top performers in the niche. The underperformance in this batch (22 videos) is not sufficient evidence to retire them. The specific executions that failed were too generic: "Here's how to take X the right way" does not name the specific mistake, and product-owner hooks assume prior product knowledge that cold TikTok traffic does not have. The fix is precision, not format replacement. More reps will build the pattern recognition needed to execute these correctly.
