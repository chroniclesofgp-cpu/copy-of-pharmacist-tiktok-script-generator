# Cross-Creator Compliance Analysis and Conversion Playbook

## Executive Finding

The audit **does not reveal a hidden phrasing formula that makes medical, outcome, or result-implying content compliant**. Both creators use some genuinely careful practices, especially Riva’s conditional language in the 30-day probiotic video and rphreviews’ non-commercial mechanism-only explainers. But the same 30-video current corpus also contains many direct condition, mechanism, result, time-to-result, before/after, clinical-study, and professional-endorsement patterns that TikTok Shop’s published rules describe as restricted or high-risk when they are attached to a commercial product claim. The fact that a public video is still viewable is not evidence of its policy status or a reliable permission slip for another account. [1] [2] [3] [4]

> **The productive adjustment is not to write vague, weak videos.** It is to preserve specificity at the product-reveal and benefit-stack stage by using the exact, current PDP/package language—and to stop using independent ingredient biology, disease-like diagnosis, or result imagery as the bridge that supposedly proves the linked product will deliver that outcome.

TikTok Shop’s current misleading-content guide requires product claims, attributes, and packaging statements to match the product detail page. Its medical guide separately directs creators to keep non-OTC promotion in general-wellness language, avoid disease/diagnosis/treatment framing, and avoid visuals that imply medical endorsement or treatment. [1] [2] The beauty guide likewise requires claims to match the listing and warns that a disclaimer does not turn a medical or unsupported statement into an allowed one. [4]

## Why Their Videos Can Be Live While Your Account Is Flagged

There are four answers that are **supported by evidence**, and none is “they found a magic safe wording.” First, a public viewer cannot see the exact product detail page, listing version, geo/campaign configuration, product-anchor state, pre-check outcome, enforcement history, appeals, or later moderation decision for every video. Second, TikTok’s published policies impose requirements, but public visibility does not expose whether or how a specific post was reviewed. Third, a creator may have a stronger current PDP/package basis for a particular claim than a different product does. Fourth, enforcement can be inconsistent or delayed; the policy text expressly warns that removal, listing suppression, and account-level consequences are possible. [1] [2] [3]

The six-case update of @dealsbygp is now the controlling cross-case evidence for this account. Its strongest common pattern remains **a linked health/beauty product plus a recommendation/CTA plus a concrete health, appearance, body-function, or symptom outcome stated beyond what was documented on the exact linked PDP/package**. VC-005 adds that short runtime, a white coat/pharmacist text, a persistent `Results may vary` overlay, and a caption disclaimer do not remove this risk; it does not establish a single automated trigger. The creator corpus does not disprove the pattern. It instead shows that large creators may use similar or stronger tactics in public.

## The Conversion-Preserving Play: Precision, Not Timidity

The goal is to use **strong language that belongs to the product**, not strong language that belongs only to an ingredient paper, a generic mechanism, a consumer review, or a creator inference.

| Script moment | Avoid: broad outcome bridge | Use when verified against the exact current PDP/package | Why it preserves conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem hook** | “You have high cortisol.” “Your hair loss is because of poor blood supply.” “This is why your underarms are dark.” | A non-diagnostic lived-experience hook: “If shaving leaves your underarms looking more uneven than you want…” or “If you are tired of covering the look of under-eye darkness…” | The viewer still self-identifies without being diagnosed or assigned a cause. |
| **Ingredient education** | “Kojic acid lightens dark spots.” “Ceramides replace the barrier you lost.” “This ingredient builds blood vessels.” | “Kojic acid is a tone-care ingredient.” Then, at reveal: “This formula helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone.” | The first sentence provides education; the second is a product claim delivered naturally within the authorized source scope. |
| **Skin-barrier discussion** | “Shaving strips your barrier; this replaces it.” | “After shaving, keep the routine gentle and look for barrier-supporting ingredients such as ceramide NP. If the product’s current listing states a barrier-support claim, use that exact claim at reveal.” | This retains an intelligible post-shave action without representing a linked product as restoring an injured condition. |
| **Product reveal** | “This gets rid of dark underarms.” “This is the fix.” | “This is why this formula is a strong fit for that routine: it pairs [exact ingredient] with [exact PDP appearance/wellness claim].” | It sounds decisive while staying within verifiable product language. |
| **Benefit stack** | Generic “tone care” repeated four times, or four inferred ingredient outcomes. | Distinct exact facts: `kojic acid — [PDP tone wording]`; `niacinamide + vitamin C — [PDP wording]`; `low-irritation tested`; `aluminum-free`; `24-hour odor control`; `not an antiperspirant`—but only if each is on the live PDP/package. | The viewer sees multiple reasons to buy; each item remains defensible. |
| **Visual hook / proof** | Altered skin, thinning-hair, “before/after,” result graph, condition photo, or study screenshot that makes ingredient research appear to prove the SKU result. | Product texture/application, bottle/label close-up, exact PDP feature card, routine-relevant neutral objects, format comparison, use-direction demonstration, authentic package/label footage. | Strong visual rhythm without visually promising an appearance/medical transformation. |

### Working Language Bank for Dark-Underarm Content

The following are **stronger than “tone-care ingredients”** but must be selected only after claim-map verification for the precise linked listing. They are not automatic substitutes.

| Claim-map status | Spoken wording that can be used | Do not escalate it to |
|---|---|---|
| Listing says “helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone” | “This formula uses kojic acid for the **look of uneven tone** and helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone.” | “It lightens dark spots,” “it removes hyperpigmentation,” or “it fixes dark underarms.” |
| Listing says “promotes brighter, smoother-looking skin” | “It also pairs niacinamide and vitamin C for **brighter, smoother-looking skin**.” | “It fades pigment,” “it bleaches,” or “it reverses darkening.” |
| Listing states low-irritation/sensitive-underarm testing | “It is low-irritation tested and made with sensitive underarms in mind.” | “It is safe for everyone,” “it cannot irritate,” or “it heals razor burn.” |
| Listing/package names ceramide NP but contains no barrier effect claim | “For post-shave care, I look for formulas that include ceramide NP—and this one lists it.” | “Ceramide NP rebuilds/replaces your skin barrier.” |
| Listing has a verified barrier-support claim | “It [exact barrier-support wording], which is why I would choose it for a gentler post-shave routine.” | A diagnosis or injury/recovery narrative that exceeds the claim. |

## Visual Rules That Keep the Video Competitive

The current corpus confirms that conversion does not require a static talking-head video. Use a visual change every 2–4 seconds, but replace result/condition proof with **claim-map proof and product-use proof**:

1. Open on a direct routine tension or a physical product-context visual, not a diagnosed body condition. A razor, shower routine, ingredient card, product texture, or “DO / DON’T” grid can carry the first-frame contrast.
2. Use a product package close-up, ingredient-panel close-up, or screenshot of the current official PDP precisely when the related exact claim is spoken.
3. Use demonstrative—not transformative—footage: applying a deodorant, showing the applicator, label, texture, instructions, supply count, or product format.
4. Use reviews, rating, units sold, stock, or sale messaging only when they are visible and current on the live official listing at filming. Keep scarcity tied to what is verified that day. [1]
5. Preserve the physical cart point and a short conditional-cart close. Those are conversion mechanics; they are not the root claim issue in the five-case audit.

## What Must Not Be Copied, Even If the Videos Are Still Live

The following are exactly the types of tactics that make the account’s existing five-case pattern harder to defend. They should not be reintroduced merely because Riva or rphreviews currently uses them:

| Do not borrow | Examples visibly found in the 30-video corpus | Reason not to adopt for linked health/beauty content |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis-by-symptom hook** | “That’s high cortisol,” “signs of hormonal imbalance,” “if you have dark undereye circles,” PMDD/PCOS framing. | The current medical guide says not to connect a product with disease/diagnosis outcomes or use vague symptom diagnosis as an indirect substitute. [2] |
| **Ingredient-science-to-SKU-efficacy leap** | Hair-density studies; tyrosinase/melanin explanation; nitric oxide/circulation; collagen/fat-cell mechanisms; strain-specific outcomes. | Ingredient research alone does not establish that the linked SKU delivers the stated viewer result; TikTok requires the content claim to match the PDP/package. [1] |
| **Result or altered-condition imagery as sales proof** | Wrinkled/aged skin, dark-circle image, neck-line images, hair study outcome slides, PDRN before/after, clinical percentage-result panels. | The current guides flag before/after, result implication, edited effects, and medical/condition visuals in product promotion. [1] [2] [4] |
| **Prescription/clinical-procedure equivalence** | “Clinical-level” device framing; clinic/professional LDM comparison; “wound healing” anti-aging bridge; topical-filler implication. | Beauty products may not be positioned as procedure-level or medical-treatment equivalents. [1] [4] |
| **Weight-loss / fat-reduction bridge** | “Help you lose weight,” appetite suppression, belly/fat-cell claims, fasted product benefit. | Current health guidance treats high-risk weight-management statements as restricted, including linked products and visual/wording implications. [3] |

## Immediate Script-Writing Rule Change

For linked health or beauty scripts, do **not** automatically downgrade a verified, current PDP/package benefit into a vague phrase such as “tone care.” Instead, use the **strongest exact listing-supported wording** in the reveal and benefit stack, quoted or materially identical to the source. The pre-reveal education must remain general, non-diagnostic, and non-transformative; the claimed product payoff must then be narrow enough to match the source exactly.

This gives the creator a clear speaking rule:

> **Be direct about what the listing directly says. Be qualified about what the listing qualifies. Do not ask ingredient science, a study overlay, or a condition image to say more than the linked product is authorized to say.**
