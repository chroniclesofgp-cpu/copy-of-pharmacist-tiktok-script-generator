## Dominant Hook Frameworks (Top 3)

### 1. "If you have [symptom/condition]…" — MOST USED
The single most repeated opening. Targets a specific audience by their symptom or condition, then pivots to a product.

**Verbatim examples:**
- "If you have stinky feet…try @Lume Deodorant whole body deoderant plus sweat control."
- "If you have high stress, you must watch this video."
- "If you have hot flashes you need this product by @Snap_Supplements Perimeno Health…"
- "If you have a bad back or bad knees. Dont take ibuprofen long term."
- "If you're a #womanover50 look into taking a good phytoestrogen…"
- "If you need support with your hair, @Hims & Hers hair bundle…"

**Pattern structure:**
> "If you have [SYMPTOM/CONDITION]… [PRODUCT] [BENEFIT]."

---

### 2. "Stop [doing X]! Instead [do Y]" — INSTRUCTION CORRECTION
Challenges a common behavior, positions the product as the smarter alternative.

**Verbatim examples:**
- "Stop taking ibuprofen long term! It's not good instead look for an alternative like turmeric."
- "Stop taking ibuprofen long term! Instead talk to your doctor about supplementing with curcumin the active ingredient from turmeric."
- "Stop getting the Visine that doesn't work. Instead get Lumify…"

**Pattern structure:**
> "Stop [COMMON BEHAVIOR]! [NEGATIVE CONSEQUENCE]. Instead [PRODUCT/INGREDIENT] [BENEFIT]."

---

### 3. Credibility Anchor — "I've been a pharmacist for X years…"
Used as an authority hook, especially on high-view videos. Establishes trust before the recommendation.

**Verbatim examples:**

### NEW HOOK 2: "How Do You Know If You Have [Condition]?"
Diagnostic hook — makes the viewer self-identify with a condition, then positions a product as the solution.

**Verbatim template:**
> "How do you know if you have [CONDITION]? [PRODUCT] is a really good formula to help support and boost your [METRIC]."

**Structure:** Diagnostic question → Symptom list → Product as solution

### NEW HOOK 3: "[X] vs [Y] — Which One Is Better?"
Comparison hook. Creates immediate curiosity and positions the creator as an unbiased expert.

**Structure:** Comparison frame → Ingredient breakdown → Recommendation

## Nuanced Techniques Observed

1. **Ingredient-before-brand naming**: He always names the active ingredient first, then the brand. This builds credibility and educates before selling. Example: "curcumin the active ingredient from turmeric" before mentioning @Micro Ingredients.

2. **Compliance disclaimer as trust signal**: His standard disclaimer ("Just a quick disclaimer: While I'm a licensed pharmacist, this is not medical advice.") appears in every caption. This is not just legal protection — it actually reinforces his pharmacist authority.

3. **Reply-to-comment format**: ~20% of his videos are replies to specific user comments. This creates the impression of personalized advice and drives comment engagement on other videos (viewers ask questions hoping for a reply video).

4. **"You must watch this video" sticker text**: Several videos use the sticker overlay "You MUST watch this video ⚠️" — this is a scroll-stopping urgency technique used on top of the thumbnail.

5. **Condition-specific targeting for women 40-50**: Multiple videos target perimenopause symptoms (hot flashes, night sweats, hormones). This is a high-converting demographic for supplements.
