## Overview

Dr. Faith operates as a medical doctor promoting TikTok Shop affiliate products, primarily supplements. Her catalog is heavily concentrated in NMN and Astaxanthin (6 of 10 videos, 60%), with secondary coverage in Medicube skincare sticks (2 of 10) and Magnesium Complex (1 of 10). Despite this concentration, her structural patterns are consistent enough to extract universal rules.

Her dominant conversion engine is the **NMN + Astaxanthin bundle** ($279K top GMV, $60–66 GPM range). Her highest-GPM videos are not her highest-view videos — the Medicube video at Rank 2 has 7.5M views but only $22.96 GPM, while her NMN bundle videos convert at $60–66 GPM on far fewer views. This is a MOF → BOF creator: she converts warm audiences at high efficiency rather than building cold awareness at scale.

## Dimension 1: Visual Hook Architecture (0–3 seconds)

Dr. Faith uses three primary visual hook formats:

**Format A — Product-in-Hand from Frame 0 (7/10 videos):** The product is held up and visible before she speaks. This functions as a visual filter — it self-selects viewers already interested in the product category. The product is never introduced cold.

**Format B — Dual-Product Comparison Hold (2/10 videos — Medicube):** Both competing products are held up simultaneously from second 0:00, one in each hand. The comparison is established visually before she speaks. This is the visual hook for the Expert Comparison format.

**Format C — Laptop/Slide Screen (1/10 videos — Rank 4):** Opens with a laptop screen showing a symptom-to-supplement mapping slide. Creator's face is not shown. This is the only video in her top 10 that does not use a product-in-hand or face-visible opening.

**Key finding:** Dr. Faith's visual hook is almost always the product itself or the comparison setup. She does not use pattern interrupts, jump cuts, or dramatic visual setups. The product-in-hand format is her default.

**Persistent visual element:** "Doctor Explains" red badge text overlay appears in the first 3 seconds in 9 of 10 videos. It is a credential badge, not a title card — authority and product are introduced simultaneously.

## Dimension 2: Text Hook System

Dr. Faith's text hooks follow two primary patterns:

**Pattern A — "Doctor Explains" credential badge (9/10 videos):** A red text overlay reading "Doctor Explains" or "Dr. Faith Explains" appears in the first 3 seconds. This is not a hook in the traditional sense — it is an authority signal that frames everything that follows as medical advice.

**Pattern B — Symptom or question overlay (4/10 videos):** A secondary text overlay names the symptom or question being addressed. Examples: "Going grey? → NMN" / "Fine lines? → Astaxanthin" / "Which NMN is better?"

**Key structural difference from rphreviews:** Dr. Faith's text overlays are front-loaded (first 5 seconds) and then disappear. rphreviews' red banner stays on screen for 3–8 seconds (overlapping with his credential delivery) before disappearing — Dr. Faith does not replicate this.

**Text hook placement:** Upper third of screen in 8/10 videos. Red text on white background for the credential badge. White text on dark background for symptom/question overlays.

## Dimension 3: Spoken Hook Formula

Dr. Faith's spoken hooks fall into three patterns:

**Pattern A — Specific Audience Situation (5/10 videos):** Names a relatable, specific situation the viewer is likely in. This is her highest-GMV pattern.
- "So NMN was shoved down your throat by all the celebrities and you got yours in the mail and now you don't know how to take it." (Rank 1 — $279K)
- "Anti-aging NMN from Micro Ingredients or CataKor. Who did it better?" (Rank 6 — $30K)

**Pattern B — Expert Comparison Question (3/10 videos):** Opens with a direct A vs. B question.
- "Dr. Melaxin's Calcium Bomb Stick or Medicube's new PDRN and Pink Collagen Stick? Which one is better?" (Rank 2 — $172K)

**Pattern C — Comment Reply / "You asked" (2/10 videos):** Opens by acknowledging a viewer question, establishing parasocial connection.
- "So many of you have been asking me about NMN..." (Rank 7 — $29K)

**Cross-creator validation:** The "specific audience situation" hook (Pattern A) maps to the Right-Way hook framework. The Expert Comparison Question (Pattern B) maps to the Comparison framework. Both are confirmed across multiple creators.

## Dimension 4: Credential Delivery System

Dr. Faith uses a three-layer credential stack in every video:

**Layer 1 — Visual credential (0:00):** Medical scrubs (pink or purple) with embroidered name "Dr. Faith Coats" visible on chest in every video.

**Layer 2 — Text credential (0:00–0:03):** "Doctor Explains" red badge overlay present from frame 0 in 9/10 videos.

**Layer 3 — Verbal credential (0:05–0:10):** "I'm Dr. Faith Coats" or "as a doctor" stated within first 10 seconds in 8/10 videos.

**Credential sequence:** Hook → Credential → Education → Product. The credential never opens the video — the hook does. This is consistent with rphreviews, Riva, Drew, and naturo — universal rule confirmed.

**Exception (Rank 4 — laptop video):** Delivers authority only via text overlay and verbal claim ("As a doctor, I do recommend...") — no scrubs, no face shown. Still converted $99K, suggesting that for a warm audience, the verbal credential alone is sufficient.

**"I don't gatekeep" verbal marker:** Appears in 4/10 videos, always within first 10 seconds after the credential statement. Functions as a trust accelerant — signals insider information is about to be shared.

## Dimension 5: Problem/Mechanism Architecture

**Education occupies 55–70% of runtime in 8/10 videos.** The product is not pitched until the viewer has received substantial free value.

**Visual education toolkit (used in every video):**
- Chemical pathway diagrams (NMN → NAD+, or ingredient → mechanism)
- Age-decline bar charts (NAD+ dropping from age 20 to 90)
- Before/after aging photos (skin, wrinkles, hair)
- Scientific study screenshots (PubMed or ScienceDirect, 3–5 per video)

**"Wrong way → right way" structure:** Dr. Faith uses this educational architecture even in videos not explicitly framed as correction hooks. The education section almost always includes a "most people don't know this" beat that positions her as an insider.

**Dosage specificity as trust-builder:** In 6/10 videos, Dr. Faith gives exact dosage instructions — specific mg amounts by age bracket (Under 35: X mg, Over 35: Y mg, 60+: Z mg), timing relative to meals, and specific interactions to avoid. This level of specificity makes the video feel like a genuine medical consultation.

**NMN-specific:** The NAD+ age-decline chart appears in 5 of 6 NMN videos. It is the single most-used visual in her catalog — it makes an abstract concept (cellular energy decline) viscerally visible.

## Dimension 7: Proof and Social Proof Architecture

Dr. Faith uses a consistent 4-layer proof stack in every video:

1. **Authority proof** — Medical scrubs + "Doctor Explains" badge + verbal credential
2. **Scientific proof** — PubMed study screenshots (3–5 per video, shown as green-screen overlays)
3. **Quality certifications** — "No weird fillers, third-party tested, made in the United States" — nearly verbatim across all 10 videos
4. **Social proof** — Used selectively: review counts ("20,000+ reviews"), celebrity name-drops (David Sinclair, Bryan Johnson), or "over X people have bought this"

**Proof anchor phrase:** *"no weird fillers, third-party tested, made in the United States"* — memorized phrase that appears in the same position (just before or just after product reveal) in every video.

**Supplement facts label:** Turning the bottle around to show the label appears in 4/6 NMN videos. Supplement-category trust tactic specific to ingredient transparency.

## Dimension 9: CTA Architecture

Dr. Faith's CTA formula is consistent across all 10 videos:

```
[Discount/Deal mention] → [Link placement instruction] → [Scarcity/Urgency] → [Optional: engagement ask]
```

**Verbatim CTA components (recurring):**
- *"I'm gonna put it in the little orange shopping cart"* — appears in 8/10 videos
- *"it does sell out so incredibly fast"* / *"stock is limited"* / *"snag it while you can"* — appears in 9/10 videos
- Coupon mention ($2–$5 off) — appears in 4/10 videos
- Finger pointing downward gesture — appears in 10/10 videos

**Scarcity type:** Always stock-based, never time-based. "Sells out fast" or "stock is limited" — never "sale ends Sunday." More credible for supplement products where stock genuinely fluctuates.

**Physical gesture:** The downward-pointing gesture is synchronized with the verbal CTA in every video. It trains the viewer's eye to look at the TikTok Shop cart icon at the exact moment they are being asked to buy.

## Dimension 10: Rehook and Mid-Video Retention

**Primary rehook technique:** The "Bait-and-Switch" — introduces the product early, then backs off to deliver brand-agnostic education. The viewer stays to learn the general advice, then receives the product recommendation at the end. This is a retention architecture built into the video's structure rather than a discrete mid-video hook.

**Secondary rehook technique:** Dosage specificity. Exact mg amounts, timing instructions, and interaction warnings create a "I need to write this down" moment that keeps viewers watching and pausing.

**Open loop technique (comparison videos):** Holds both products from frame 0 but withholds the verdict until 65–87% of runtime. "Which one is better?" is the open loop that drives retention.

**Missing element:** Dr. Faith does not use rphreviews' opening red banner (3–8 seconds) or his sequential checkmark benefit list retention system. Her retention is driven by content value (dosage instructions, brand-agnostic advice) rather than visual pattern interrupts.

## Dimension 12: Funnel Stage Distribution

**9 of 10 videos operate at MOF → BOF.** Dr. Faith's content assumes the viewer already knows the product category exists. She does not introduce supplements to cold audiences — she educates warm audiences and closes them.

**Exception:** The Magnesium video (Rank 8) operates TOF → BOF. Magnesium deficiency is a broad enough problem that the hook ("most people are deficient and don't know it") captures cold audiences.

**Implication:** Dr. Faith's high GPM ($33–$65 range) is explained by her MOF → BOF architecture. She is not trying to reach everyone — she is converting viewers who are already in the consideration phase. Lower reach, higher conversion rate.

## Unexpected Findings

**Finding 1: GPM inversely correlated with view count in Dr. Faith's catalog.** Her highest-view video (7.5M views, Medicube) has the lowest GPM ($22.96). Her highest-GPM videos (NMN bundle, $60–66) have 400K–4.6M views. This confirms she is a MOF → BOF creator — her content is optimized for conversion efficiency, not reach.

**Finding 2: The "Prescription Feel" as the most important trust device.** By giving contraindications, timing instructions, and side effect warnings, Dr. Faith makes her videos feel like genuine medical consultations. This is not just a tone choice — it is the structural reason her authority converts at high GPM. The prescription feel is the mechanism behind her Authority + Reciprocity psychological combination.

**Finding 3: Parasocial warmth in clinical content.** The "pudding pop," "sweet baby angels," "bad boys" language appears in 6/10 videos. This is Dr. Faith's signature tone device — it makes clinical content feel personal and lowers the viewer's sales defense. The underlying principle (inject warmth into clinical authority) is replicable for any pharmacist creator.

## Implications for Our Scripts

1. **Lead with the specific situation, not the benefit.** Dr. Faith's highest-GMV hooks name a specific, relatable audience situation in the first sentence — not a generic benefit claim. "NMN was shoved down your throat by all the celebrities and you got yours in the mail and now you don't know how to take it" is more powerful than "NMN is an anti-aging supplement."

2. **Give exact dosage instructions.** Specific mg amounts by age bracket, timing relative to meals, and interaction warnings make the video feel like a medical consultation. This is a conversion driver, not just an educational nicety.

3. **Use the Bait-and-Switch Trust Architecture for high-ticket products.** Introduce the product early, back off to deliver brand-agnostic education, then return for the close. This lowers sales resistance and increases conversion on products where the viewer has multiple brand options.

4. **Memorize the proof anchor phrase.** "No weird fillers, third-party tested, made in the United States" — deliver it in the same position every time (just before or just after product reveal). Consistency makes it feel like a professional standard, not a sales claim.

5. **Inject warmth into clinical authority.** The "I don't gatekeep" marker and the parasocial language are not decoration — they are trust-building devices that make clinical content feel personal. A pharmacist who sounds like a friend is more persuasive than one who sounds like a textbook.
