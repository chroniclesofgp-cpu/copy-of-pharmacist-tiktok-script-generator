## Overview

Drew operates two TikTok Shop accounts simultaneously, both using the same creator persona, the same visual system, and the same conversion architecture. The accounts are not differentiated by product category or audience — they appear to be a volume strategy: more content, more placements, more chances to catch a viewer. The combined GMV across both accounts is approximately $1.15M, making Drew one of the highest-earning creators in this dataset.

Her dominant products are **astaxanthin** ($420K+ across both accounts), **collagen** ($242K), and **magnesium** ($223K) — three products that also appear in rphreviews' and Riva's top catalogs. This cross-creator validation is significant: these three products are not just Drew's best sellers, they are the category's best sellers.

Her persona is distinct from rphreviews and Riva: she presents as a **cancer researcher** (not a pharmacist), wears a lab coat and ID badge, and leans harder into scientific literature than any other creator in this dataset. The PubMed study screenshot volume in her videos is 3–5x higher than rphreviews and Riva.

## Dimension 1: Visual Hook Architecture (0–3 seconds)

Drew uses three primary visual hook formats:

**Format A — Product-in-Hand Reveal (9/20 videos):** Both hands hold up the product(s) at frame center from the first frame. No face, no background — product is the entire visual. This is the most common format in her top-tier videos (Ranks 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8 across both accounts). The product-first visual signals "this is about a specific product" immediately, which is a MOF signal — it self-selects viewers who are already interested in this category.

**Format B — Talking Head with Immediate Text Overlay (7/20 videos):** Face visible, lab coat and ID badge visible, text hook appears within 1 second. The credential visual (lab coat + badge) does the authority work before any words are spoken.

**Format C — Story/News Stitch Hook (4/20 videos):** Opens with an external clip (news footage, viral animal video, historical photo) before Drew appears. The berberine video ($55K) opens with footage of a wild orangutan applying a medicinal leaf paste to a wound — 25 seconds of nature documentary content before any supplement mention. This is the most creative hook format in her catalog and the only one that could work for a cold TOF audience.

**Key finding:** Drew's visual hook is almost always the product itself or her credentialed appearance. She does not use pattern interrupts, jump cuts, or dramatic visual setups. The product-in-hand format is her default because it immediately communicates "this is a recommendation from an expert" — which is the entire premise of her channel.

## Dimension 2: Text Hook System

Drew's text hooks follow a consistent formula:

**Primary format:** `"[Benefit/Action] + [Specific Product/Ingredient]"`

Examples:
- "Here's how to take NMN and astaxanthin together correctly..." (Acct2 Rank 10)
- "The best supplements for [condition]" (multiple videos)
- "If you're taking [ingredient], you NEED to know this" (Acct1 Rank 5)

**Key structural difference from rphreviews:** Drew does not use the red banner + "MUST watch this video ⚠️" formula. Her text hooks are informational rather than urgency-based. They promise value (knowledge, correct usage, best option) rather than warning of risk. This is consistent with her researcher persona — a researcher informs, a pharmacist warns.

**Text hook placement:** Upper third of screen in 14/20 videos. White text on dark background or yellow text on dark background. No consistent color system — less systematized than rphreviews.

**Missing element:** Drew does not use a persistent text hook that stays on screen throughout the video. rphreviews' persistent red banner is a retention tool that Drew does not replicate.

## Dimension 3: Spoken Hook Formula

Drew's spoken hooks fall into three patterns:

**Pattern A — "How to take X correctly" (6/20 videos):** Opens with the correct usage of a supplement, implying the viewer is currently doing it wrong. Identical to the Right-Way hook (Framework 24). Examples:
- "Here's how to take NMN and astaxanthin together correctly..."
- "Here's how to take magnesium correctly..."
- "Here's how to take collagen correctly..."

**Pattern B — Expert Verdict (8/20 videos):** Opens with the conclusion. "This is the best [product] for [condition/person]." Identical to Framework 25. Examples:
- "This is the best supplement for gut health..."
- "This is the best collagen for women over 40..."

**Pattern C — Curiosity/Story Hook (4/20 videos):** Opens with a question or story that has nothing to do with supplements. The berberine video opens: "Did you know that wild orangutans in Borneo have been observed applying medicinal plants to their wounds?" The H. pylori video opens with a symptom question. These are TOF hooks.

**Cross-creator validation:** Drew uses the same Right-Way and Expert Verdict hooks as rphreviews and Riva. This is strong evidence these two formats are the dominant hook types in this product category, not creator-specific preferences.

## Dimension 4: Credential Delivery System

Drew's authority system is the most elaborate in this dataset:

**Layer 1 — Visual credential (0:00):** Lab coat visible in every video. ID badge clipped to coat visible in 18/20 videos.

**Layer 2 — Verbal credential (0:05–0:10):** "As a cancer researcher..." or "As a pharmacist and cancer researcher..." in 16/20 videos. The cancer researcher title is more authoritative than pharmacist for supplement science claims because it implies research methodology expertise, not just dispensing expertise.

**Layer 3 — Scientific literature wall (0:10–1:00):** 8–15 PubMed study screenshots displayed in rapid succession during the mechanism section. This is Drew's signature and her most distinctive element. No other creator in this dataset uses scientific literature at this volume or frequency.

**Layer 4 — Ingredient label proof (1:00–1:15):** Shows the actual supplement facts panel on screen to prove the product contains the ingredients she just discussed. Appears in 12/20 videos.

**Layer 5 — Manufacturing quality proof (1:10–1:20):** Text box or verbal statement: "Made in USA / Third-party tested / Non-GMO / Gluten-free." Appears in 17/20 videos.

**Key insight:** Drew's authority system is layered and sequential — each layer builds on the previous one. By the time she reveals the product, the viewer has seen: her appearance (lab coat), her title (cancer researcher), 10+ scientific studies, the actual ingredient label, and quality certifications. The product reveal is not a pitch — it is a prescription from someone who has already proven they know what they are talking about.

## Dimension 5: Problem/Mechanism Architecture

Drew's mechanism section is the longest and most detailed of any creator in this dataset. Average mechanism section: 45–75 seconds (vs. rphreviews' 20–35 seconds and Riva's 25–40 seconds).

**The PubMed Wall:** Drew's mechanism section is structured as a rapid-fire presentation of scientific evidence. Each claim is immediately followed by a PubMed screenshot. The pattern repeats 8–15 times per video. This creates a "wall of proof" effect — the viewer cannot process each study individually, but the cumulative visual impression is overwhelming scientific validation.

**Mechanism types by product:**
- **Astaxanthin:** Antioxidant potency comparison (6000x stronger than Vitamin C), eye health, skin collagen, mitochondrial protection
- **Magnesium:** Cortisol regulation, sleep architecture, muscle function, 300+ enzymatic reactions
- **Collagen:** Skin elasticity, joint cartilage, gut lining, hair/nail structure
- **NMN:** NAD+ precursor, cellular energy, DNA repair, anti-aging at cellular level
- **Berberine:** Blood sugar regulation, AMPK activation, anti-inflammatory, gut microbiome

**Key structural note:** Drew always names the mechanism *before* naming the product. The viewer learns why the ingredient works before they know what product she is recommending. This is the same architecture as rphreviews and Riva — mechanism first, product second.

## Dimension 6: Product Reveal Timing and Method

**Timing:** Product reveal occurs at 55–70% through the video in 16/20 videos. This is identical to rphreviews and Riva — cross-creator validation of the universal architecture.

**Method:** Drew holds the product up with both hands at chest height, label facing camera. In 8/20 videos she holds up two products simultaneously (bundle or stack). In 4/20 videos she shows the actual pills/capsules in her palm before showing the bottle.

**Reveal language patterns:**
- "So if you want to try [ingredient] for yourself and you want an American-made supplement, this right here is top of the line." (berberine video)
- "Now targeting all of these pathways separately can be expensive, but if you'd like a recommendation, there's a [X]-in-1 formula from [Brand] that has everything I just mentioned plus more." (testosterone video)
- "I'm going to leave the link on the bottom of the screen..." (universal CTA bridge)

**The "expensive to buy separately" objection pivot:** Drew uses this reveal bridge in 7/20 videos. It is her most distinctive reveal technique — she names the cost objection at the exact moment of product reveal, then immediately resolves it with the bundle/formula. This is a pre-emptive objection handle that converts the product reveal into a value proposition.

## Dimension 7: Proof and Social Proof Architecture

Drew's proof system has three distinct layers used in sequence:

**Layer 1 — Scientific proof (mechanism section):** PubMed screenshots. Primary proof mechanism. Establishes that the *ingredient* works.

**Layer 2 — Product quality proof (post-reveal):** Ingredient label screenshot + "Made in USA / Third-party tested" text box. Establishes that *this specific product* contains the right ingredients at the right quality.

**Layer 3 — Social proof (CTA section):** TikTok Shop product card popups showing star ratings and units sold. Appears in 9/20 videos. Examples: "4.6 stars, 36.6K sold" (NMN video). Verbal redirect: "I'll leave the link so you can see all their reviews for yourself."

**Key finding:** Drew's social proof is the weakest of the three layers — she uses it last and least. Her primary proof mechanism is scientific, not social. This is the opposite of many TikTok Shop creators who lead with reviews. Drew's approach works because her authority is so established by the time social proof appears that it is confirmatory rather than persuasive.

## Dimension 8: Objection Handling

Drew handles objections more systematically than any other creator in this dataset. She addresses 3–5 distinct objections per video, compared to rphreviews' 1–2 and Riva's 2–3.

**Standard objection sequence (appears in 14/20 videos):**
1. **"Does it work?"** — Addressed in mechanism section via PubMed wall
2. **"Is it safe/quality?"** — Addressed post-reveal: "Made in USA, third-party tested"
3. **"Is it too expensive?"** — Addressed at product reveal: "Targeting these pathways separately can be expensive, but..."
4. **"How do I take it?"** — Addressed after product reveal: exact dosage, timing, food requirements
5. **"Are there interactions?"** — Addressed near CTA: "Consult your doctor if you're on [specific medication]"

**The medical disclaimer as trust builder:** Drew's "consult your doctor if you're on blood thinners" (or similar) appears in 11/20 videos and is consistently placed near the CTA. This is counterintuitive — most creators avoid medical disclaimers because they fear it will reduce conversions. Drew uses them as a trust signal: a responsible researcher who warns you about interactions is not trying to just sell you something.

**The "expensive to buy separately" objection bridge:** Unique to Drew. Appears at the product reveal moment in 7/20 videos. Converts the price objection into the product's value proposition.

## Dimension 9: CTA Architecture

Drew's CTA is the most formulaic element of her videos — nearly identical across all 20 videos:

**Standard CTA formula:**
> "And if you want to check this out, [scarcity element]. I will leave the link right [here/down here/on the bottom of the screen] [so you can see the reviews / next to the orange shopping cart]. [Optional: If you have any questions, let me know in the comments.]"

**Scarcity elements used:**
- "They're having a flash sale right now" (13/20 videos)
- "Very limited in stock" (4/20 videos)
- "Amazing price right now" (6/20 videos)
- "Save an extra $X plus free shipping" (5/20 videos)

**Physical gesture:** Points index finger downward toward TikTok Shop link in 18/20 videos. In 6/20 videos, a red arrow graphic appears on screen simultaneously pointing to the same location.

**CTA length:** 15–25 seconds in 17/20 videos. Shorter than rphreviews (20–35 seconds) and Riva (20–40 seconds).

**Key finding:** Drew's CTA is almost entirely scarcity-based ("flash sale right now," "limited in stock"). She does not use benefit-based CTAs ("your skin will thank you") or social proof CTAs ("join 36,000 people who already tried this"). The scarcity-only CTA is her consistent system.

## Dimension 10: Rehook and Mid-Video Retention

Drew's primary retention mechanism is **visual density** — the screen is never static. PubMed screenshots pop up every 3–5 seconds during the mechanism section, creating constant visual movement that prevents scroll-away.

**Secondary retention mechanisms:**
- **Transition phrases:** "Let me tell you the science behind this..." (pivots from hook to mechanism), "Now if you want to try this for yourself..." (pivots from mechanism to product)
- **The "smart like the orangutan" callback:** In the berberine video, the CTA opens with "So if you want to be smart like the orangutan..." — bringing the opening story full circle. This is a sophisticated narrative loop that rewards viewers who watched from the beginning.
- **Pill reveal close-up:** In 6/20 videos, Drew shows the actual pills in her palm mid-video. This is a pattern interrupt that breaks the talking-head format and grounds the abstract science in a tangible product.

**What Drew does NOT do:**
- No explicit retention plea ("stay until the end")
- No mid-video question ("have you ever wondered why...")
- No comment bait mid-video

Her retention is entirely passive — the visual density keeps viewers watching without asking them to stay.

## Dimension 13: Buyer Psychology Levers

**Dominant levers (appear in 18+ of 20 videos):**
- **Authority** — Lab coat, ID badge, cancer researcher title, PubMed wall. The single most important lever in Drew's system. Everything else depends on this.
- **Scarcity** — Flash sale, limited stock. Appears in every CTA.

**Secondary levers (appear in 10–15 of 20 videos):**
- **Liking/Trust** — Medical disclaimers ("consult your doctor"), responsible framing, educational tone
- **Convenience** — "15-in-1 formula," "80-day supply," bundle pricing
- **Social Proof** — TikTok Shop product cards, review redirects

**Desire channels activated:**
- **Health/Longevity** — Primary channel in all 20 videos
- **Appearance** — Secondary in skin/hair/nail products (collagen, astaxanthin)
- **Financial/Value** — Bundle pricing, supply duration framing, "expensive to buy separately" bridge

**Missing levers:** Reciprocity (no free value/gift framing), Commitment/Consistency (no "you already care about your health, so..."), Fear (used minimally — Drew prefers positive desire framing over fear framing, unlike rphreviews who uses fear heavily)

## New Framework Candidates — Evaluation

**Candidate 1: "The PubMed Wall" (Scientific Proof Barrage)**
Drew's rapid-fire PubMed screenshot technique is unique in its volume and density. No other creator uses 8–15 study screenshots in a single video.

*Check against existing frameworks:* This is not a hook — it is a mid-video proof technique. It belongs in SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE.md as a proof mechanism, not in HOOK_FRAMEWORKS.md. **Not a new hook framework.**

**Candidate 2: "The Expensive to Buy Separately" Objection Bridge**
Drew's technique of naming the cost objection at the exact moment of product reveal, then resolving it with a bundle/formula, is a distinct and replicable technique.

*Check against existing frameworks:* This is not a hook — it is a product reveal technique and objection handle. Belongs in SCRIPT_ARCHITECTURE_GUIDE.md under "Product Reveal Mechanics." **Not a new hook framework.**

**Candidate 3: "The Orangutan Story Hook" / Viral Story Hook**
Opening with a viral/surprising story from nature, history, or news that metaphorically connects to the supplement's mechanism. The berberine video is the clearest example.

*Check against existing frameworks:* Checking Framework 16 (Storytime Hook) and Framework 25 (Fear + External Threat)... The Storytime Hook (Framework 16) covers personal stories. The Fear + External Threat Hook (Framework 25) covers news stitches with threat framing. The Orangutan/Viral Story Hook is different: it uses a *third-party viral story* (not personal, not threatening) as a metaphorical bridge to the product. The story is surprising and entertaining, not scary. The connection to the product is conceptual (animal uses plant medicine → humans should use plant medicine).

*Verdict:* This is a genuinely distinct format. It is TOF-capable (cold audience can engage with a viral animal story), it bypasses TikTok Shop fatigue (first 25 seconds look like a nature documentary), and it creates a narrative loop that rewards full watch-through. **Adding as new framework: Viral Metaphor Hook.**

## Unexpected Findings

**1. The two-account volume strategy works.** Drew's combined GMV across both accounts is approximately $1.15M. The same products appear on both accounts — astaxanthin, collagen, magnesium — but with different video angles. This is a deliberate strategy: more videos on the same product = more chances to reach buyers at different stages of awareness. The accounts are not competing with each other; they are covering different audience segments with different hook types.

**2. The medical disclaimer increases conversions.** Drew's "consult your doctor if you're on blood thinners" appears in 11/20 videos and is always placed near the CTA. This is counterintuitive but consistent. The disclaimer signals that Drew is a responsible practitioner, which increases trust, which increases conversions. This is a replicable technique that most creators avoid.

**3. The "80-day supply" / "4-month supply" framing is more powerful than price.** Drew never mentions a specific price in any of the 20 videos. Instead she frames value through supply duration: "80-day supply," "3-month supply," "4-month supply." This is consistent with rphreviews and Riva. The pattern is universal — price creates hesitation, supply duration creates value perception without triggering price resistance.

**4. The pill reveal close-up is an underused retention and conversion tool.** Showing the actual pills in the palm of the hand appears in 6/20 videos and serves two functions: (1) it is a pattern interrupt that breaks the talking-head format, and (2) it makes the abstract supplement concrete and tangible. The viewer can see the size, color, and quantity of what they are buying. This reduces a hidden objection ("what am I actually getting?") without explicitly addressing it.

**5. Drew's astaxanthin dominance is a product-market fit signal.** Astaxanthin appears in 5 of Drew's 20 deep-analysis videos and generates $420K+ combined. The same product is Riva's Rank 2 ($133K) and naturopathicapothecary1's top product ($216K). Astaxanthin is not just Drew's best product — it is the category's best product. Any creator entering this space should have an astaxanthin video as a priority.

## Implications for Our Scripts

**Immediate applications:**

1. **Adopt the "expensive to buy separately" objection bridge** for any bundle or multi-ingredient product. Name the cost objection at the product reveal moment, then resolve it with the bundle value.

2. **Use the medical disclaimer as a trust signal**, not just a legal requirement. "If you're on blood thinners, consult your doctor first" placed near the CTA increases trust without reducing conversions.

3. **Show the pills in your hand** mid-video for any capsule/tablet product. It is a pattern interrupt and a hidden objection resolver.

4. **Never mention a specific price.** Frame value through supply duration ("3-month supply," "80-day supply") and savings framing ("saving $15 plus free shipping").

5. **The PubMed wall is replicable** — even 3–4 study screenshots displayed in rapid succession during the mechanism section creates a "wall of proof" effect. Does not require 15 studies. 3–4 is sufficient for the visual impression.

**New framework to add to HOOK_FRAMEWORKS.md:**
- Viral Metaphor Hook (Framework 27) — opens with a surprising third-party viral story that metaphorically connects to the supplement's mechanism
