# Proposed Conversion-Preserving, Evidence-Ready TikTok Shop Operating Model

**Status:** Proposed for creator approval; not yet a permanent script or filming control.  
**Date:** August 18, 2026  
**Purpose:** Preserve the authority, speed, pain-point relevance, visual density, study literacy, and urgency that make leading pharmacist creators convert—without knowingly turning unsupported ingredient research, competitor criticism, or medical-result imagery into a promise about the linked SKU.

## 1. Strategic Conclusion

The correct response to the enforcement history is **not** to make every video timid or generic. The Riva and rphreviews corpus shows that strong healthcare content uses direct tensions, fast visual changes, authority signals, formula comparisons, study cards, professional explanation, product choice, and cart urgency. However, the creator audit does not show a hidden phrase that makes any direct medical or result claim safe.

The working model is therefore:

> **Be aggressive about the problem, the product-selection standard, the visual proof, and the exact product facts. Do not be aggressive about a linked product outcome that the live PDP/package and source record cannot support.**

VC-004 and VC-005 show that a focused claim-mapped evidence submission can materially improve an appeal outcome. They do **not** establish that repeated enforcement is harmless, that evidence authorizes any original wording, or that an account with prior enforcement should deliberately accumulate violations.

## 2. Three Content Lanes

| Lane | Purpose and conversion role | What remains strong | Non-negotiable boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Direct Commerce** | Default linked-product conversion video. | A sharp lived-experience hook; white coat; fast cuts; real product comparison; formula architecture; label/PDP proof cards; exact benefit stack; conditional cart urgency. | Every linked-product payoff, result, direction, certification, quantity, and comparison must match the current live PDP/package. No diagnosis, universal competitor claim, ingredient-to-SKU leap, or study card presented as product proof. |
| **B. Evidence-Backed Product Proof** | Higher-authority linked-product video for products with unusually strong support. | Full-formula clinical evidence, exact listed claims, study-design explanations, formula/label close-ups, pharmacist interpretation, and a decisive product selection. | Use a study visual only when it evaluates the actual linked formulation or the live PDP/package makes materially the same claim. Label the source precisely; never call an ingredient study a product trial. |
| **C. General Education / Trust Building** | Noncommercial content that builds authority, search discovery, follows, and a reusable education library. | Deep study cards, anatomy/mechanism diagrams, condition discussion, category comparisons, medication/OTC education, and long-form pharmacist explanation. | No product cart, code, shopping CTA, selection verdict, or indirect commercial bridge in the same video. This is genuine education—not a way to disguise an advertisement. |

The account should use all three lanes. Direct Commerce should remain the purchase engine; General Education keeps the pharmacist differentiation and gives the audience the deeper study content they value; Evidence-Backed Product Proof is reserved for the minority of products with true full-formula or exact-PDP support.

## 3. What “Boundary Testing” Should Mean

Boundary testing should mean **testing persuasive execution**, not testing whether an unsupported health claim can survive an appeal.

Appropriate variables to test include:

1. Pain-first hook versus category-confusion hook.
2. Formula-architecture visual versus label close-up.
3. Product reveal at the middle versus final third, within the required funnel stage.
4. Exact PDP claim spoken as a verdict versus displayed as a claim card.
5. One decisive category comparison versus a multi-option tier list.
6. Product-use demonstration versus package/PDP proof sequence.
7. Study **design literacy** in Lane C versus formula evidence in Lane B.

Do not test direct diagnosis, broad symptom relief, “real cause,” “does nothing,” condition-result imagery, unsupported time-to-result, or ingredient-study-to-SKU outcome language as variables in a linked-product video. Those are the recurring risk amplifiers in the six-case record, not productive experimentation.

## 4. Two-Track Proof System

Every linked-product video should deliberately assign every proof visual to one of two tracks.

| Track | What it can prove | Best visual forms | Required spoken framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product-proof track** | What the exact linked SKU says it contains, is, or is positioned to do. | Live PDP screenshot, product label, directions panel, supplement facts, certification, package close-up, application/use footage. | Natural, exact-source benefit language: “This is a dual-action formula for meal-time discomfort and temporary bloating relief.” The PDP/label card proves the source silently. |
| **Education track** | What a study, ingredient, category, or mechanism actually establishes. | Study-design card, source title, neutral mechanism diagram, category explainer. | “This ingredient/strain/category research found…” plus the true scope. It must not be presented as proof that the linked SKU delivers the same consumer outcome. |

If a study does not evaluate the linked product or an exact listed claim, it belongs in Lane C or in the appeal package—not as sales proof in Lane A.

## 5. Pre-Posting Controls for Any Linked Product

Before filming, create a short **Evidence-Ready Filming Packet**:

1. Dated screenshots of the exact live TikTok PDP and package for the product variant being linked.
2. One-line source map for every spoken claim, text card, visual implication, comparison, direction, and urgency statement.
3. A visual map: identify whether every on-screen proof card is Product Proof or Education; remove any card that visually overstates the linked SKU.
4. Final script, caption, hashtags, product-cart screenshot, and rendered pre-post version saved together.
5. A ten-slot appeal manifest prepared before posting for any medically adjacent, supplement, or beauty content.

For Lane B, add a second review that confirms the study is either full-formula evidence or is represented only at its true ingredient/category scope.

## 6. Account-Protection Rules

The account already has a meaningful enforcement history. Two approved appeals are positive, but they are not a reason to normalize serial flags.

1. Do not post several high-risk linked videos with the same claim pattern or product in a short window.
2. If a video is flagged, pause materially similar linked-content variations until the appeal outcome and evidence gap are recorded.
3. Preserve actual enforcement evidence and appeal outcomes; do not infer that an approval cleared wording that TikTok did not explicitly address.
4. Keep the backup account education-first while it grows. Do not use it to recreate known high-risk linked-product patterns or to evade an enforcement action.
5. Treat live creator videos as inspiration for **structure and conversion mechanics**, not permission to copy their most aggressive claim language.

## 7. Practical Next Step

For the next campaign, choose the lane before writing the hook. Then write one high-conversion linked-product video in Lane A and one genuinely noncommercial, study-forward Lane C education video around the same audience problem. This preserves the content depth that differentiates a pharmacist account while avoiding the forced jump from a general study to a linked-product result.
