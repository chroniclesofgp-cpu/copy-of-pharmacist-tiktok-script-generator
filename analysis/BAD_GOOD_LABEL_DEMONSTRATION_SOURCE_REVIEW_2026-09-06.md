# Bad/Good Label Demonstration — Source Review

**Status:** Discovery complete for this research pass. **No exact live two-tier Bad/Good Label Demonstration source was located or treated as a framework match.** This is not a revalidation card, active framework, campaign plan, or script.

## 1. Proposed Test Format

The proposed test is a deliberately simplified **two-tier** decision format. The creator points to a product or its label, gives one short and specific reason it is a less suitable option for a defined goal, then points to a better-fit product or label and gives one short reason why. It has two proposed executions:

| Execution | Structure | Intended close |
|---|---|---|
| Single-product pair | One less-suitable label → one better-fit label | Single-product TikTok Shop cart CTA |
| Category rapid-fire | Several discrete less-suitable → better-fit pairs | Multi-product CTA linking the distinct better-fit options |

The desired test is **not** a three-tier “bad / good / excellent” ranking. Three-tier sources are included below only as contextual evidence.

## 2. Search Result: Exact Match Not Found

Public web, TikTok search, Instagram-indexed, YouTube-indexed, and direct candidate checks did **not** yield a retrievable video matching all of these conditions together:

## 5. Implications for a Future Test Card

The future card should identify this honestly as a **user-approved synthesized test format**, not as a proven hook copied from one source. It may borrow the confirmed pacing, physical-item handling, immediate viewer-language reason, label-proof visual, and decision-map constraints listed above.

The word **“bad”** must be operationalized as a narrow, evidence-supported limitation for a defined viewer goal, not a blanket verdict that the alternative is unsafe, useless, or universally inferior. For example, a fair contrast identifies the alternative’s real role before showing why another label may be a better fit for the stated goal. The new card must specify exactly what counts as a valid label criterion, what source proves it, and when an alternative remains appropriate.

For the category rapid-fire version, a generic line such as “I’ll link the good ones below” is eligible only after a current Competitive Decision Map verifies the alternatives, their roles and limitations, and which distinct 8/10+ items can actually be linked. The script cannot call a form “good” and link an unrelated product merely because both belong to the same category.

## 6. Candidate Category Fit — Research Needed Before Use

| Candidate | Why it fits the label device | Research boundary before a test |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium | Form is visibly stated on labels; viewer decision sets are familiar. | Preserve magnesium oxide’s narrow roles and do not reduce the comparison to a universal absorption claim. |
| Probiotics | Strain designation, per-strain CFUs, and proprietary blends are label-visible. | Requires strain-specific evidence and careful correction of the adjacent source’s broad CFU rule before any script. |
| Fish oil | Form and EPA/DHA disclosure can be label-based. | Form/bioavailability claims require nuanced, current evidence; do not treat triglyceride form as universally superior without a defined use context. |
| Melatonin | Dose is label-visible and creates a clear decision tension. | Requires current, population-specific dose research and no blanket medical-direction claim. |
| Iron | Form is label-visible and buyer trade-offs are familiar. | Requires use-context, tolerability, interaction, and diagnostic boundaries. |
| Electrolyte powders | Added sugar and disclosed electrolytes are directly label-visible. | Requires current product-specific panels and a fair use case for each option; no universal “sugar is bad” framing. |
| Sunscreen | Visible white-cast demonstration offers a fast contrast. | This is more a live-application/texture test than a label demonstration; it should not be treated as direct evidence for the label-led version. |
