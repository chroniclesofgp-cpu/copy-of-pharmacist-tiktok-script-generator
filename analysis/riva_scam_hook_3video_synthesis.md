## KEY STRUCTURAL INSIGHT — WHAT MAKES THIS HOOK WORK

This is NOT primarily a product education video. It is a **consumer protection PSA** that happens to sell a product. The structure is:
1. Fear trigger (fake products exist → you could be scammed)
2. Authority anchor (I'm a pharmacist — I can tell you what's real)
3. Verification tutorial (here is exactly how to buy the real one)
4. Soft product benefit mention (brief — enough to remind you why you wanted it)
5. CTA (the link is safe — I've verified it for you)

The product benefit section is intentionally SHORT in this hook type. The conversion mechanism is TRUST + FEAR OF BEING SCAMMED, not product education. This is the opposite of the TOF/MOF education-heavy approach.

**Why the pharmacist credential is uniquely powerful here:** A lifestyle creator saying "watch out for scams" is just a warning. A pharmacist saying "supplements are not well-regulated and I've verified this one" is a professional endorsement. The credential transforms a generic scam warning into a clinical quality assurance statement.

**Riva's version:** Consumer protection framing — "buy from the verified seller"
**Our current Type 4 template:** Label-reading criteria — "here are 3 things I check on the label"

**Recommendation:** The Type 4 template should offer BOTH execution paths. Path A = Riva's consumer protection approach (use when real knockoffs exist). Path B = our label-reading criteria approach (use when strong ingredient differentiators exist). Path B is the pharmacist-exclusive version and is stronger for conversion — but requires confirmed differentiators.
