## 2. User-Confirmed Execution Detail

The project creator previously identified the key performance mechanic of this source format: an **off-camera person calls out the product/category**, and the on-camera pharmacist immediately reacts with the numerical score. This user-confirmed execution detail is retained as an approved adaptation requirement even though the accessible silent source frames cannot independently recover every spoken cue.

## 3. Framework Purpose

Use this framework when viewers need help choosing among **real options in a familiar product category** or navigating different roles in a routine. It earns attention through the viewer’s unresolved question: *“What will the expert give the thing I use—or am considering—a score?”*

It is not a full comparison lecture, a generic tier grid, or a #5-to-#1 countdown. It is an **expert verdict sequence**.

## 4. Required Beat Order

| Beat | Visual | Spoken action | Viewer job |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Persistent title: “RATING [category] on a scale of 0–10” | No preamble. | Establish the decision promise. |
| 1 | Off-camera prompt names a real product/category; pharmacist displays it or a truthful proxy | Immediate score and one concise reason. | Create the first contrast. |
| 2–3 | New real prompt + new physical/category visual each beat | Score immediately; one pain-to-payoff reason specific to that option’s role or limitation. | Maintain uncertainty and category recognition. |
| Final | Highest-priority / linked option appears last with physical product in hand | Final score, pharmacist reason-to-care, direct benefit, then late cart close. | Earn the recommendation after contrast. |

### Score Order Rule

The final linked option receives the last major verdict so the product reveal resolves the category question. Earlier scores should **not** progress predictably from low to high. Use a meaningful order based on familiar viewer choices and their trade-offs; include a genuinely limited or low-value option only where its limitation is factual and category-relevant.

## 5. Pharmacist Adaptation Rules

1. **Every score needs a viewer-facing reason.** “8/10” is not a complete beat. The reason must name the desired outcome, pain point, practical limitation, or product role in ordinary language.
2. **Use product-role contrast, not blanket attacks.** Example pattern: “Great for [narrow role]; not my first pick if your goal is [viewer goal].”
3. **The final product earns the score through a pharmacist breakdown.** Explain the useful ingredient, mechanism, routine role, or formulation distinction in one direct reason-to-care sentence.
4. **Use one evidence card when the final reason makes an ingredient or clinical claim.** The card must state what it supports and distinguish ingredient evidence from product-specific evidence. Do not replace visible proof with backstage narration.
5. **At least three buyer-psychology levers must be designed deliberately:** recognition (a familiar option), contrast (why one choice has a limitation), authority (pharmacist rationale), convenience or value (where real), and conditional urgency (current cart context).
6. **Use sentence-level qualification when it is necessary for accuracy.** Qualification scopes a claim; it does not erase the mechanism, direct benefit, pain point, proof card, or expert verdict.

## 6. Visual System

| Required asset | Execution |
|---|---|
| Persistent header | Category + 0–10 scale stays in a consistent top position. |
| One current item focus | One named product/category prompt at a time; physical product, truthful package image, or neutral category visual. |
| Score graphic | Large numeral shown immediately with an intentional color treatment. Avoid a multi-row board that competes with the product focus. |
| Reason text | Short viewer-language summary, not ingredient jargon alone. |
| Proof card | Timed after the claim it supports; plain-language takeaway plus a citation/identifier. |
| Final product proof | Real package, label, or exact current listing visual—never a generic stand-in. |

### Competitive Decision Map — Required Before Ranking

The scorecard must be built from a current **Competitive Decision Map** (`COMPETITIVE_DECISION_MAP_STANDARD_2026-09-01.md`), not a list of generic stand-ins. Each prompt must be a real, recognizable buyer alternative: a competitive product, ingredient/form, procedure, professional treatment, alternative therapy, or routine viewers genuinely use or consider.

For each option, research its useful role and the specific limitation or frustration viewers encounter. A fair score identifies that narrow role before explaining why the option falls short of the central viewer goal. The final recommendation solves an earned gap; it is not placed last merely because it is linked.

Two or more products may earn an 8/10+ recommendation and be linked when their exact current TikTok Shop listings are available and their use cases differ clearly. Explain the choice: **“Choose [Option A] if your priority is [role]; choose [Option B] if you need [different role].”**

## 7. Eligibility and Preflight

| Check | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Category familiarity | Can the viewer recognize each option and the decision they are making? |
| Competition research | Has a current Competitive Decision Map established that the named alternatives are real, recognizable, fairly scoped to a narrow role, and paired with source-supported limitation/frustration evidence? |
| Evidence | Is every final-product benefit supported by the fact-only product record and a correctly scoped proof card where needed? |
| Product placement | Is the final item genuinely the clearest fit for the viewer goal rather than forced into the format? |
| Cart context | Does the filming-day listing support any availability, bundle, price, or urgency statement? |

## 8. Exclusions

- Do not call the final recommendation “S-tier” unless the selected source format explicitly uses that grading system; this framework is **0–10**.
- Do not use generic placeholders such as “a purple oral-care product” or “generic energy drink” when a real recognizable choice is required.
- Do not make all early options low-scoring simply to manufacture a product reveal.
- Do not turn the video into a predictable worst-to-best countdown.
- Do not describe a product claim as “the listing says” in spoken copy. State verified benefits naturally and use the product visual/proof card as support.

## 9. Fresh-Rebuild Use Gate

Before a new product campaign uses this format, create or reopen:

1. a fact-only product record with current listing/label/ingredient sources;
2. category-competition research containing real viewer-recognizable alternatives and fair limitations;
3. a campaign brief that identifies the viewer goal and why the final item earns the last verdict; and
4. a script-specific proof-card plan.

No script is included in this card. Script drafting remains a later phase of the clean-slate rebuild.
