## 2. Format Purpose

This format helps a buyer make one narrow, label-visible choice without requiring a full tier list. It works when the relevant distinction is plain enough to show in a few seconds—for example, an ingredient form, dose, strain disclosure, per-strain CFU disclosure, added-sugar line, or confirmed formula attribute.

The format is **not** a device for declaring that a familiar product, treatment, or brand is broadly bad. It is a controlled decision demonstration:

> **This option can fit [its truthful narrow role]. But if your stated goal is [specific goal], this verified label/formulation limitation matters. This other verified option is the better fit for that goal because [specific label-visible reason].**

## 3. Non-Negotiable “Bad” Safeguard

The word **“Bad”** is an on-screen shorthand only. It must always mean **less suitable for one named viewer goal because of one evidence-supported limitation**. It must never mean that the alternative is universally inferior, unsafe, useless, fraudulent, or wrong for every buyer.

| Required part of every less-suitable beat | Minimum execution |
|---|---|
| Stated viewer goal | Name the goal before or within the pair: “If you want [goal] …” |
| Alternative’s real role | One short fair statement: “This can still make sense for [narrow role].” |
| Narrow limitation | One current, source-supported limitation tied to that same goal—not a vague insult or unproven clinical claim. |
| Better-fit condition | State why the second exact label/formulation fits the named goal better. |
| Proof | Show the label fact and use the fact record / evidence source appropriate to the mechanism claim. |

**Preferred visual wording:** `LESS FIT FOR [GOAL] → BETTER FIT FOR [GOAL]`.

If the visual uses the requested shorter `BAD → GOOD` language, the spoken line and/or adjacent small text must immediately define the goal, such as: `BAD FOR [SPECIFIC GOAL]` and `GOOD FOR [SPECIFIC GOAL]`. Do not leave a blanket product verdict on screen.

## 4. Verdict Delivery Options

Each Bad/Good beat must choose one of the following spoken verdict styles. Both are legitimate; **Option B is the default preference** because its personal-opinion framing makes the narrow, goal-specific judgment especially clear. The reason must follow the verdict immediately in the same spoken beat. A bare “bad,” “good,” “yes,” or “no” is not a complete line.

| Option | Less-suitable beat | Better-fit beat | Default use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Direct verdict + reason** | “This is bad — [specific reason].” | “This is good — [specific reason].” | Use when the goal has already been named clearly and a concise direct label improves pacing. |
| **B — Personal-opinion framing + reason** | “This one’s a no for me — [specific reason].” | “This one’s a yes — [specific reason].” | **Preferred by default.** Use when personal-opinion framing makes the narrow judgment unmistakably a fit decision rather than a blanket factual verdict. |

The bracketed reason must be **specific, label-visible or evidence-supported, and tied to the named viewer goal**. Do not use a generic reason such as “it is worse,” “it is better,” or “I do not like it.” The alternative’s legitimate role still comes first when the beat requires it: for example, “This one’s a no for me if your goal is zero added sugar — it has eleven grams per stick,” while preserving that the product may fit a different goal.

### Spoken verdict and overlay must stay synchronized

The spoken verdict and the on-screen verdict are one communication unit. Whichever option is used, the spoken line must match or be an obvious paraphrase of the visible overlay. They must never drift independently.

| On-screen overlay | Compatible spoken wording | Incompatible drift |
|---|---|---|
| `BAD` | “This is bad — [reason]” or “This one’s a no for me — [reason]” | Overlay says `BAD`, but spoken line avoids the verdict and only describes a limitation. |
| `GOOD` | “This is good — [reason]” or “This one’s a yes — [reason]” | Overlay says `GOOD`, but spoken line gives only a neutral product description. |
| `LESS FIT FOR [GOAL]` | “This one’s a no for me for [goal] — [reason]” or an obvious equivalent. | Overlay narrows the goal but spoken wording sounds like a universal product rejection. |
| `BETTER FIT FOR [GOAL]` | “This one’s a yes for [goal] — [reason]” or an obvious equivalent. | Overlay claims a goal-specific fit while spoken wording makes an unsupported universal superiority claim. |

If the shorter `BAD/GOOD` overlay is used, the adjacent small text or immediately preceding spoken line must name the narrow goal. If the creator wants maximum precision, use `LESS FIT/BETTER FIT` overlays and make the same goal explicit in the spoken verdict. **Do not display a verdict word that the spoken line deliberately avoids.**

## 5. Core Two-Tier Beat Order

| Beat | Single-pair execution | Category rapid-fire execution |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Frame-one text names the category and exact viewer goal: `FOR [GOAL]: BAD / GOOD LABELS`. | Persistent title names the category and goal; do not explain the system at length. |
| 1 | Physically point to or hold the first exact product/label. | First real alternative enters frame. |
| 2 | Give its legitimate narrow role, then one label/formulation reason it is less suitable **for the named goal**. | Repeat the same one-item pattern for each real decision-set alternative. Each pair must stand alone. |
| 3 | Point to or hold the exact better-fit product/label. | Better-fit counterpart follows each less-suitable item; do not delay every good option until the end. |
| 4 | Give one concise, label-visible reason why it better fits the goal. | Give one concise reason per better-fit option. Avoid stacked claims. |
| 5 | Show the relevant label line / proof card immediately after the claim it supports. | Keep proof cards brief and correctly matched to each pair. |
| Final | Name the one linked product, direct benefit, and current-cart CTA. | Summarize the distinct use-case split, then link only the qualifying, current TikTok Shop winners. |

### Pacing

- **Single-product pair:** approximately 20–35 seconds; one real less-suitable label and one better-fit label only.
- **Category rapid-fire:** approximately 35–60 seconds; typically two to four discrete pairs. One item, one narrow role, one limitation, one better-fit reason; then move on.
- **No third “excellent” tier:** The core test remains two-tier by design. Three-tier ranking is separate contextual territory, not an automatic expansion of this card.

## 5. Single-Product Version

Use this execution when a single current TikTok Shop product clearly earns the better-fit position for one precise, familiar buyer goal.

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Comparison set | One recognizable alternative/formulation and one exact linked product. |
| Product identity | Both products/forms must be current and visually/label verified. |
| Viewer goal | Narrow enough that the label difference actually matters. |
| Less-suitable statement | Includes legitimate role first, then the limitation. Example pattern: “This still has a role for [role], but for [goal], [specific label/form] is not my first pick because [evidence-supported reason].” |
| Better-fit statement | Explains the verified form/label distinction and viewer benefit in one plain-English line. |
| Close | Single-product cart CTA only after the comparison conclusion. Cart/offering language remains filming-day verified. |

## 6. Category Rapid-Fire Version and Linking Gate

The category version may use the line **“I’ll link the good ones below”** only after the following conditions are complete. A shared label trait is never enough to justify a linked recommendation.

| Required gate | What must be preserved in the current Competitive Decision Map |
|---|---|
| Real alternatives | Every compared option is a recognizable product, ingredient/form, routine, or other genuine buyer alternative—not filler. |
| Fair role | Each less-suitable option has a truthful, narrow role before its limitation is explained. |
| Source-supported limitation | The limitation is current, relevant to the named goal, and tied to a cited label, product, or category source. |
| Exact current listings | Every product called “good” and linked is a currently available TikTok Shop listing at the time of filming. |
| 8/10+ threshold | Each linked recommendation has earned an 8/10+ verdict through the map. |
| Distinct use cases | Multiple linked items must serve clearly different viewer priorities; the final line states the choice split instead of pretending one is universally best. |

**Permitted category close pattern:** “I’ll link the good ones below—choose [Option A] if your priority is [role], or [Option B] if you need [different role].”

**Not permitted:** “I’ll link the good ones below” when the creator has not mapped which exact products are current, high-scoring, distinct-use-case winners.

## 7. Sunscreen Is a Separate Candidate Sub-Type

White cast versus blends-in demonstrations are worth retaining as a candidate because they offer immediate visual contrast. They are **not** the core label-reading version of this card.

| Sunscreen live-application / texture sub-type | Boundary |
|---|---|
| Primary proof | Real-time application, blend-out, finish, and current product texture—not Supplement Facts/ingredient-label comparison. |
| What it may borrow | Two-tier, goal-specific fairness: a product may be less suitable for a white-cast-sensitive buyer while still fitting another narrow role. |
| What it must not claim | That application appearance proves SPF performance, universal cosmetic preference, or medical suitability. |
| Card status | Separate candidate sub-type; it requires its own source review and approval before being used as an active extension. |

## 9. Eligibility and Script Preflight

| Check | Required decision before a script is drafted |
|---|---|
| Exact label distinction | Is the product/form/dose/strain/sugar or other difference physically label-visible and current? |
| Viewer goal | Can the choice be stated in a narrow, ordinary-language goal rather than a disease claim or broad product attack? |
| Fairness | Does every less-suitable option receive a real legitimate role before the goal-specific limitation? |
| Fact record | Does a current fact-only product record support each hero-product claim, label line, and benefit? |
| Competitive Decision Map | Is there a current map for every comparison, ranking, or multi-link close? |
| Proof cards | Does each ingredient/mechanism claim have a correctly scoped source card, separate from the exact label proof? |
| Category linking | If more than one product will be linked, are current listings, 8/10+ verdicts, and distinct use-case splits documented? |
| Filming-day check | Are the exact products, labels, seller/listing, and cart conditions still current? |

## 10. Exclusions

- Do not call a product, form, treatment, brand, or familiar alternative broadly bad, inferior, unsafe, useless, fake, or a scam because it is less suitable for the selected goal.
- Do not omit the alternative’s legitimate role.
- Do not use unnamed, generic, or invented alternatives.
- Do not use a comparison fact from one product to justify a conclusion about another product.
- Do not convert a label fact into a product-specific clinical-outcome promise.
- Do not introduce a third tier without a separately approved format decision.
- Do not use sunscreen application visuals as if they prove the core label-reading structure.
- Do not rely on Food Pharmer as structural evidence for this format.
