# Bundle Routine Walkthrough — Fresh Framework Revalidation Card

**Status:** Rebuilt active framework foundation  
**Rebuild date:** September 1, 2026  
**Source basis:** Three original user-supplied TikTok references, directly re-opened and visually inspected; detailed observations are preserved in `BUNDLE_ROUTINE_WALKTHROUGH_SOURCE_LOG_2026-09-01.md`.  
**Scope:** A reusable routine-order format for genuine multi-product bundles. It is **not** a product campaign, product-intelligence record, or script.

## 1. What the Original Sources Reliably Show

The shared, source-observed promise is practical: a viewer who owns or is considering several products is shown **what each item is, where it fits, and what to do with it**. This is the source of the format’s value. It is not a generic product list and it is not a broad product review.

| Observed source mechanic | Evidence across the three fresh references | Reusable framework rule |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate physical bundle or first-step proof | Reference 1 opens on the product-filled box; Reference 2 opens on texture/product; Reference 3 opens on an opened pad container plus the first pad. | Show the actual bundle or the actual first routine item at 0:00. Do not open on a talking head or a product-name slide alone. |
| Clear routine-use promise | “Step by Step Guide,” “How to use…Bundle,” and “RN explains…routine” are visible in the source openings. | Make the text and spoken hook answer a practical question: *How do I use this routine?* Add the audience problem only if it is true for the exact bundle. |
| One item at a time | Reference 1 proceeds through individual toner, serum, and treatment-stage visual cards; References 2–3 keep the active product/texture in hand. | Use a repeated **Step → show → explain role → demonstrate** micro-loop for every product. |
| A single job per product | Source cards pair an ingredient/product with one concise reason to care, while the visual shows the product or use area. | Give each product one plain-language buyer payoff and one use instruction. Do not turn a routine video into a dense ingredient lecture. |
| Routine order is visually persistent | Reference 1 keeps a visible Step 1–4 board; source openings identify a first physical step. | Number every step on screen. The viewer should be able to save the video and reproduce the order without replaying the narration. |
| Texture/application proof carries the explanation | Product on hand, fingertip texture, toner pad use, dropper handling, and face application recur in the sources. | Demonstrate texture, amount, or application on every step where it is useful and truthful. Product proof must be in motion. |
| End-state restores the system | Reference 1 returns to the full lineup/step board; Reference 3 returns to the product pair near close. | End with a simple routine recap and the whole bundle before the cart action. |

## 2. Framework Purpose and Viewer Problem

> **Viewer tension:** “I keep seeing this bundle, but I do not know what each product does, which one comes first, or how I would realistically fit all of it into my routine.”

The format solves confusion and friction. It creates a credible reason to buy a bundle only when the video proves that the bundle is a **coherent, usable system**, rather than several products placed in a cart together.

## 3. Source-Faithful Structure

| Beat | Target timing | Visual requirement | Spoken job | Text requirement |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **1. Practical opening** | 0–3s | Hold the bundle or first product/texture immediately against the lens. | State the routine-use promise and, when true, the audience problem. A credential may follow, but must not delay the promise. | A practical question or promise, such as “How to use the [bundle] in order.” |
| **2. Routine frame** | 3–7s | Lay out the complete set briefly or show a numbered routine board. | Set the payoff: “Here is exactly what each one is for and where it goes.” | Persistent Step 1–N markers. |
| **3. Repeated step loop** | 7–75% of runtime | For each item: physical product + texture/application/area demonstration. | Explain the item’s **one job**, why that job matters to the audience, and how/when to use it. | “Step [n]”; product name; one short role/payoff phrase. |
| **4. Evidence moment(s)** | Only where a clinical claim is made | Use a claim-matched label, ingredient card, or scoped study visual beside the active product/action. | Give the pharmacist breakdown in ordinary language. Qualify ingredient evidence naturally; do not narrate the source. | A short scoped proof heading, not a dense screenshot or product-listing narration. |
| **5. Whole-routine payoff** | Final 15–20% | Reassemble/hold the full set; point back to the step order. | Resolve the initial confusion and name the practical benefit of the system: a routine the viewer can actually follow. | “Your [morning/evening] order” or a compact Step 1–N recap. |
| **6. Current-cart close** | Final 3–5s | Keep the real full bundle in frame. | Direct the viewer to the current cart only when the bundle is actually attached and the spoken offer is true that day. | Current offer/review/cart information only when verified at filming. |

## 4. Pharmacist Adaptation Rules

The creator’s value is not simply listing steps. A pharmacist-led version must add **one understandable why** at the point it matters, without interrupting product use:

1. Start from the audience’s real friction or desired outcome, not the product name.
2. For each step, connect the product role to the viewer’s reason to care in one sentence.
3. When an ingredient is central, explain its general mechanism in normal language and place claim-matched proof visually; do not imply that an ingredient study is a clinical trial of the exact bundle.
4. Keep the routine authentic. Do not invent an order, frequency, or complementary benefit not supported by the exact product directions and product research.
5. Use natural sentence-level qualification where it is needed; qualification scopes the claim and must not replace the viewer payoff.

## 5. Core Visual System

| Component | Required execution |
|---|---|
| **First frame** | Product-in-hand, texture, opened item, pad, applicator, or complete bundle. No visual preamble. |
| **Routine board** | A clean 1–N board stays visible or reappears at every step; each number populates as the creator reaches it. |
| **Active-step proof** | The step number, product/product texture, and one role/payoff are visible together. |
| **Proof card** | Use only a source-matched ingredient, label, or study card for the exact claim being spoken. If a step makes no clinical claim, do not force a study image. |
| **Recap** | Return to the complete bundle and completed routine order before the close. |

## 6. Allowed Source Variations

The sources support three opening treatments. Keep the underlying routine-order structure unchanged.

| Variation | Source precedent | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| **Unbox → routine** | Product-filled box and “Step by Step Guide” in Reference 1 | A new, visually complete bundle where the unboxing itself provides scale/novelty. |
| **Texture-first routine** | Close texture/application opening in Reference 2 | A tactile skincare, body-care, or personal-care set. |
| **Credential + first step + pain point** | “RN explains…” plus toner pad and dark-spot routine opening in Reference 3 | A healthcare-led bundle whose audience problem is verified and specific. |

## 7. Exclusions and Evidence Boundary

The fresh source review supports the **visual routine-walkthrough mechanics** above. It does not establish a universal verbatim spoken script, a universal CTA phrase, or a universal clinical claim set. Those must be built from the exact product’s newly researched fact sheet and campaign approval.

Do not use this format when products are unrelated, when the claimed routine order is unsupported, or when the bundle lacks a real shared use case. Do not reuse the original creators’ product claims as generic claims for a new product.

## 8. Fresh-Source Use Gate

Before this format may be used in a campaign, complete the following for the specific bundle:

| Requirement | Gate |
|---|---|
| Exact product set and directions | Fact-only product record with each item, order, directions, and source links. |
| Audience problem and one role per item | Approved campaign plan that maps every step to a real buyer question. |
| Clinical statement/proof card | Claim-specific source and visual provenance. |
| Cart close | Current filming-day bundle/cart and offer verification. |
