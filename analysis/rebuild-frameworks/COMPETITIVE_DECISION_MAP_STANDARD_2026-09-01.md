# Competitive Decision Map — Active Rebuild Standard

**Status:** Active clean-slate decision-framework requirement  
**Created:** September 1, 2026  
**Source:** Creator-approved strategic requirement recorded during the clean-slate framework review.  
**Scope:** Category Scorecard, Numeric Scorecard, Tier List after fresh revalidation, Best-Last List, and any comparison-led product campaign. This is not a script or product-intelligence record.

> **Core rule:** A ranked or compared option must be a real buyer alternative—not filler. It should be a recognizable product, treatment, procedure, therapy, routine, or commonly recommended approach that a viewer has used, considered, or been frustrated by.

## 1. Required Research Before Selecting Ranked Options

Build a **Competitive Decision Map** from fresh source research before a decision framework is approved for a product. The map must include the genuine ways buyers attempt to solve the same problem, not only directly competing TikTok Shop products.

| Required field | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Real buyer alternative** | Use a recognized product, ingredient/form, procedure, professional treatment, routine, or common therapy. Never use an unnamed “generic product” as filler. |
| **Recognition evidence** | Record why the choice belongs in the viewer’s real decision set: current market presence, patient/question frequency, review language, creator-content frequency, or first-party/category sources. |
| **Narrow useful role** | State the truthful job it can do or the user it may fit. A fair ranking does not dismiss every alternative. |
| **Recurring drawback or frustration** | Identify a real, source-supported limitation such as cost, inconvenience, narrow applicability, side-effect concern, slow timeline, maintenance burden, coverage gap, or frequently reported buyer frustration. Do not invent a flaw. |
| **Final-product gap** | Explain the specific viewer problem or routine gap that the linked option addresses more directly. The conclusion must be earned, not forced. |
| **Proof and scope** | Preserve the exact source for every product fact, clinical/ingredient claim, and limitation. Ingredient studies do not become product-trial claims. |

## 2. Decision-Set Examples

| Viewer goal | Potential real decision set | What makes the set useful |
|---|---|---|
| Hair growth | Minoxidil, finasteride, hair transplantation, low-level-light devices, and a researched topical support product | These are real approaches viewers know; each has a distinct role, burden, limitation, or decision barrier. |
| Daily energy | Coffee, sugary energy drinks, B-vitamin supplements, IV/NAD-style infusion services, and researched functional-drink/gum formats | The viewer recognizes the trade-offs: taste/sugar, caffeine routine, pills, needles/cost, or portability. |
| Brighter-looking teeth | Surface-stain toothpaste, purple colour corrector, peroxide/PAP strip course, professional whitening, and a researched daily oral-care product | Each solves a different part of the decision: appearance, surface stains, treatment course, professional care, or daily routine. |

These examples are **research prompts**, not preapproved claims, ranks, or script copy. Every real product campaign must rebuild its own map from current evidence.

## 3. Ranking and Linking Rules

1. Use low scores only for a **truthful, relevant limitation**. A low-scored option still receives its narrow fair use case.
2. Rank order must follow meaningful viewer familiarity and decision tension—not a manufactured worst-to-best staircase.
3. A final linked product must earn its verdict through a direct viewer benefit, pharmacist explanation, and claim-matched proof card where an ingredient/mechanism claim is made.
4. More than one item may be recommended and linked when **two or more products score 8/10 or higher**, are currently available in TikTok Shop, and serve clearly distinct use cases. Explain the choice rather than pretending one is universally best.
5. Procedures, professional treatments, and non-linkable alternatives may appear as fair category context when they are part of the real buyer journey. Their purpose is insight and contrast, not to create a fake product rivalry.

## 4. Required Campaign Record

Before a decision-format script, the fact-only product record or campaign plan must preserve this table:

| Alternative | Type | Why viewers know/use it | Narrow role | Source-supported limitation/frustration | Score rationale | Link status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Option] | Product / procedure / therapy / routine | [Evidence] | [Role] | [Evidence] | [Fair reason] | Linked / unlinked / not applicable |

## 5. Exclusions

- Do not use anonymous, generic, invented, or obscure options only to make the hero product look better.
- Do not state that a common treatment “does not work” when it works for a defined use case.
- Do not call a procedure, competitor, or alternative unsafe, inferior, or a scam without source-specific evidence.
- Do not co-link two high-scoring products without explaining which viewer goal makes one a better fit than the other.

## 6. Fresh-Source Gate

This map is a prerequisite for every fresh decision-format campaign. If current sources cannot establish recognizable alternatives and honest limitations, select a different framework rather than force a ranking.
