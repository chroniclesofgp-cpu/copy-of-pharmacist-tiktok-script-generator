## 1. Source-Verified Core

| Observed source mechanism | Reusable framework rule |
|---|---|
| Firm “never” title with a fixed list promise | The first frame must name a real problem and promise concise correction—not a product description. |
| Explicit red **“Don’t say”** left column versus green **“Instead say”** right column | Preserve a two-sided visual contrast. The right side must replace the exact error on the left. |
| Short paired substitutions | Each beat is a complete correction: **never [specific wrong action] → always [specific better action]**. Do not place unrelated tips on opposite sides. |
| On-camera creator behind the grid | The pharmacist is the authority and moves/gestures through each contrast; the grid makes the point instantly understandable. |
| More visible row slots than are initially resolved | Keep the next correction visually unresolved until it is spoken. Do not reveal every answer at time zero. |

## 2. Project-Approved Healthcare Adaptation

The project creator has specified that the health version should use the language **“Never”** on the red side and **“Always”** on the green side. The visual board must use an **equal number of rows per side**—normally a compact 2×2 or 3×3 grid—rather than an uneven list. The “always” action must solve the same problem created by the paired “never” behavior.

Use the framework only where the viewer can immediately recognize the mistake and benefit from a practical replacement. For example, if the topic is dark underarms, “never over-exfoliate or scrub too hard” must pair with an action that reduces the same irritation/barrier problem; it cannot pair with a disconnected product claim.

## 3. Required Beat Order

| Beat | Visual execution | Spoken execution | Viewer payoff |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Title card: **“[N] things to NEVER / ALWAYS do for [plain-language problem]”** with a red/green board | Hook starts immediately; no credential preamble. | Recognition and a clear correction promise. |
| 1 | First red row fills; paired green row remains visually incomplete until the turn | “Never [mistake]. Instead, always [replacement] because [plain-language reason].” | Immediate useful action. |
| 2–3 | One new paired row is revealed per beat; pharmacist gestures to the active row | Repeat an exact error → replacement relationship; use a brief pharmacist reason where needed. | Curiosity and practical routine building. |
| Late product bridge | The board resolves; physical product enters after the education earned it | “That is why I look for [product role / relevant ingredient] when [viewer goal].” | Turns education into a reason to care. |
| Product + close | Exact product/label proof, one scoped evidence card, cart appears only at close | Direct benefit, then concise current-cart CTA. | Trust, solution clarity, and action. |

## 4. Pharmacy-Quality Writing Requirements

1. **Pain before product.** The title and first correction must name what viewers actually call the problem, such as “dark underarms,” “bloating after meals,” or “teeth that look dull,” not euphemistic clinical jargon.
2. **One correction, one causal explanation.** Keep each reason short and intelligible. A mechanism should explain why the substitute action matters; it must not become an ingredient list.
3. **Direct product benefit, never label narration.** Say why the product’s relevant ingredient/formulation could help the viewer’s goal in natural language. The physical package, exact listing, and proof card support the claim; they are not spoken as “the label says.”
4. **Evidence is visible, not substituted away.** If a beat uses an ingredient, efficacy, or clinical claim, attach an accurately scoped proof card. Ingredient evidence does not become proof of a product-specific result.
5. **Framework fidelity is non-negotiable.** Do not turn the grid into a generic lecture, an unequally sided list, a product roundup, or a predictable countdown.
6. **Use at least three deliberate buyer-psychology levers:** recognition, relief from a familiar mistake, pharmacist authority, simplicity/consistency, hope grounded in a realistic routine, or filming-day conditional urgency.
7. **Sentence-level qualification only.** Use “may help,” “can help,” “helps support,” or equivalent only where accuracy requires it. Do not delete the mechanism, pain point, direct benefit, study proof, or conversion sequence.

## 5. Visual and Proof-Card Plan

| Visual asset | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Header | Visible for the opening 3–8 seconds. It defines the exact plain-language problem and count. |
| Board | Red Never column / green Always column with the same number of readable rows. Reveal each pair on cue. |
| Pharmacist performance | On camera and actively interacting with the current row, not standing behind static text. |
| Pain/behavior illustration | Show only what the spoken beat is solving; do not use exaggerated or unrelated imagery. |
| Mechanism/study proof | Use a simple diagram or study card after the claim it supports. Include a plain-language takeaway and source identifier. |
| Product proof | Physical product, truthful label/ingredient image, or exact live listing timed at the late product bridge. |
| CTA visual | Current cart and short arrow/availability prompt only after the benefit and proof. |

## 6. Product-Fit Gate

Before a campaign uses this format, confirm that the product can naturally enter as the answer to the education. The product must solve at least one of the board’s **Always** actions better or more simply than a generic alternative. If the board exists only to force a product reveal, choose a different source-validated framework.

## 7. Known Source Limits

The source confirms a 28.86-second, dual-column correction format with a seven-item title and three directly observed substitution pairs. Facebook re-authentication prevented reliable review of later rows and the close. Therefore:

- preserve the verified **two-sided substitution** architecture;
- do not claim that every source row used the same timing, exact spoken formula, or closing CTA; and
- use project-approved 2×2 or 3×3 adaptation only where the topic has enough genuinely paired corrections.
