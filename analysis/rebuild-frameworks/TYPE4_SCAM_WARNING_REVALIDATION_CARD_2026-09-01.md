# Type 4 Scam-Warning Framework — Fresh Riva Revalidation Card

**Status:** Validated within Riva’s account; situational until independently replicated by additional healthcare creators  
**Scope:** Framework mechanics only; this is not a product campaign or script  
**Primary source record:** `analysis/riva_scam_hook_3video_synthesis.md`, based on Whisper-verified and visual analysis of three Riva videos, with the pre-trial Hook 29 record in `MASTER_CONTEXT.md`.

## What the Source Actually Establishes

Riva’s format is a **consumer-protection conversion mechanism**, not a long ingredient breakdown. The viewer is stopped by a four-beat fake/scam accusation and moving product images; the real product appears immediately; the video explains why the product became popular and how to identify the verified listing; then it ends with an orange-cart and comments close.

> “This is fake, this is fake, this is fake, and this is fake.”

The four-beat spoken rhythm, visual pointing, dual title boxes, product reveal within five seconds, “gone crazy viral” framing, blue-check verification instruction, and orange-cart/comment close were observed across the source set. Sales-volume contrast and the supplement-regulation authority line appeared repeatedly but not in every reference.

## Approved Framework Mechanics

| Beat | Timing | Job | Required execution |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **1. Four-beat hook** | 0:00–0:03 | Stop the scroll with fear and visual movement. | Point to four real suspicious, counterfeit, or directly comparable listing images in sync with “This is fake…” / “Fake…” repetition. Keep “Watch out for SCAMS” and “From a Pharmacist” title boxes visible. |
| **2. Real-product pivot** | 0:03–0:05 | Resolve the visual chaos and establish the authentic reference. | Both title boxes disappear as the real product is physically held up. |
| **3. Why it went viral** | 0:05–0:20 | Remind the viewer why they cared about the product. | Use “gone crazy viral” or equivalent and give one concise, direct product benefit in normal language. The length remains short because the primary value is protection. |
| **4. Protection explanation** | 0:20–0:35 | Explain why the buyer should care about the seller/listing distinction. | State the real reason verified sourcing matters for this category. A pharmacist credential or license flash belongs here as a trust anchor. |
| **5. Verification tutorial** | 0:35–0:55 | Tell the viewer exactly how to avoid the problem. | Show the official cart/store, seller name and blue checkmark, and sales-volume/reference signal if genuinely visible. |
| **6. Cart + comment close** | 0:55–1:05 | Convert trust to a safe buying action. | Point to the orange cart and invite specific questions. |

## Two Valid Paths

| Path | When it is eligible | Middle execution | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Consumer-protection path** | Current, genuine counterfeit or seller-mismatch evidence exists. | Explain why the genuine product’s popularity produced confusing or fraudulent listings; teach the exact official-seller check. | Preserve the four actual listings, official-store proof, current blue-check screenshot, and any sales-volume comparison used. |
| **B. Label-criteria path** | Literal scam evidence is absent but a category has three real, differentiating purchase criteria. | The hook pivots to “what I check” and explains the criteria in plain language before showing the verified product. | The displayed alternatives, criteria, product label, and any study card must be current and source-traceable. Do not call an honest competitor fake. |

## Proof Strategy

The hook itself is proof-led: the listing screenshots, real product, official-store screenshot, blue-check circle, and sales signal do the primary work. A product-benefit study card may appear only in the short “why it went viral” section or Path B criteria section, where it directly supports an ingredient education claim. It must not delay the real-product pivot or crowd out the buyer-protection tutorial.

## Literal-Language Boundary

Do not call a listing “fake,” “counterfeit,” or “a scam” merely because it is a low-sales, competing, non-brand, or unfamiliar listing. Use Path A only with preserved current evidence. When that evidence is not present, use Path B or choose a different framework; visual tension never justifies a false accusation.

## Fresh-Rebuild Use Rule

Before writing a Type 4 video for a product, reopen the current TikTok Shop search results and official store, capture the four opening images, verify the seller marker shown on the linked cart, confirm the product’s concise benefit from a fact-only product record, and select Path A or Path B. No deleted post-trial Type 4 script may be reused as a model.
