> This is a fresh source-bounded list variation. Its performance device is not simply “put a product last”; it is a visible list that leaves the final, most useful answer unresolved until the viewer has processed the earlier choices.

## Source-Verified Core

| Observed source mechanic | Approved adaptation |
|---|---|
| Opening promise: “#5 is the best one….” | The first visual/spoken beat must create a credible reason to wait for the final answer. |
| Fixed five-item category title | Use 3–5 real actions, options, or routine steps for a plain-language problem. The count must match the actual video. |
| Vertical numbered list with early slots shown and final answer unresolved | Keep the final slot visually blank, covered, or otherwise unresolved until the closing product/answer beat. |
| Each item gets a concise practical role | Each health-item beat must identify who it helps, what it is for, or its limitation in direct viewer language. |
| Creator remains on camera behind the list | Maintain visible pharmacist authority and movement; do not use a static slideshow as a substitute. |

## Required Execution

| Beat | Visual | Spoken purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Header: **“[N] things to do/use for [plain-language goal]—but # [final] matters most.”** | Establish the goal and a real unresolved question. |
| 1–3 | One numbered entry becomes active each beat; final slot stays unresolved | Give a concise role, benefit, or limitation. Do not pad with arbitrary options. |
| Bridge | Final blank slot remains visible | Explain the overlooked gap in the first choices or the principle tying the routine together. |
| Final item | Reveal the final answer/product physically and on the list | Deliver the pharmacist mechanism/benefit explanation, supported by a scoped proof card when a clinical/ingredient claim is made. |
| Close | Final list fully resolved, current cart visual appears | Concise cart CTA after—not before—the reason to care. |

## Health-Content Rules

1. The first 3–4 entries must come from a current **Competitive Decision Map**: real products, procedures, treatments, therapies, routines, or commonly recommended approaches viewers actually use or consider. Each gets its narrow fair role and one source-supported limitation or recurring frustration. The final product/ingredient must solve an earned central gap; it cannot be forced into an unrelated list.
2. Use direct product benefits and pharmacist education. Do not narrate product labels, reduce the final benefit to jargon, or hide all proof in internal notes.
3. Use a source-scoped study/diagram proof card for ingredient or efficacy education. Keep product-specific claims tied to the verified label/listing/physical product.
4. Name at least three buyer-psychology levers: recognition through familiar earlier choices, curiosity through the unresolved final slot, pharmacist authority, a direct payoff, and only current/conditional urgency at the close.
5. Use ordinary viewer language. “Dark underarms,” “bloating after meals,” or “teeth that look dull” is clearer than an unexplained technical substitute.
6. More than one high-scoring TikTok Shop product may be linked only when each serves a distinct role that is plainly explained. The list must not pretend a single winner is universal when the decision map supports more than one 8/10+ fit.

## Source Limits and Classification

The source confirms a 9.25-second “#5 is the best” promise, five-item vertical-list layout, partial list reveal, on-camera presentation, and short item-role labels. Facebook re-authentication blocked full playback. Therefore:

- retain the **best-last unresolved-final-slot** mechanic;
- do not copy a spoken line sequence or claim the original used a specific product-reveal/CTA formula;
- treat this as a **source-validated variation**, not a free pass to add any arbitrary numbered list; and
- build a standalone campaign/script only after the relevant product/campaign research confirms the list has real viewer logic.
