> This is not a duplicate hook. It is the numeric visual execution of an expert Category Scorecard, where a continuous 1–10 scale replaces a letter grade or unmarked verdict.

## Source-Verified Core

| Observed source mechanic | Approved adaptation |
|---|---|
| Persistent “Rating [familiar category] on a scale of 1–10” title | Place category + scale in the visual first frame; do not use spoken time to explain the scale. |
| Continuous green-to-red 1–10 number scale | Retain an immediately understandable numeric scale for each verdict. A health adaptation may use 0–10 or 1–10 consistently. |
| Familiar items represented on or beside the scale | Use real products, recognizable formats, or familiar routine choices—not generic placeholders. |
| On-camera expert commentary on each named item | Deliver one short pharmacist reason-to-care, limitation, or product-role explanation per score. |
| “Who would you put #1?” engagement idea | End with an earned final product verdict and, if appropriate, a real viewer-choice question; do not present an unsupported countdown claim. |

## Required Execution

1. An off-camera prompt names a real familiar item; the pharmacist immediately gives the numerical score and visible reaction.
2. The title/scale remains visually consistent while items change.
3. Each score gets one direct viewer-facing reason. The line must explain a benefit, limitation, routine role, or relevant pain point—not state an ingredient alone.
4. The final linked product is introduced only after the preceding category contrast has earned it; its pharmacist breakdown includes a direct benefit and evidence-mapped proof card where required.
5. Earlier score order must follow meaningful viewer choices rather than a mechanically predictable climb. The original source title supports a continuous scale; its inaccessible later sequence does **not** prove an exact worst-to-best countdown.
6. Inherit the parent framework’s required **Competitive Decision Map**. Every scored choice must be a real, recognizable buyer alternative with a fair narrow role and source-supported limitation or frustration. Multiple 8/10+ linked winners are permitted only when their current listings are available and their use cases are explicitly different.

## Health-Content Guardrails

- Use Category Scorecard eligibility, competition research, product-proof, buyer-psychology, and CTA controls from the parent framework.
- Ingredient or clinical claims require a correctly scoped study/diagram card. An ingredient study must not be presented as a product-specific trial.
- Sentence-level qualification may be used where needed, but cannot replace direct benefits, pain points, pharmacist reasoning, proof visuals, or the immediate-score mechanic.

## Exclusions

- Do not call the form a “Tier List” unless the source/creative specifically uses tiers. This is numeric scoring.
- Do not force a #5-to-#1 order from the source; that is a distinct best-last-list variation with its own evidence card.
- Do not use generic score reasons such as “good option” or “it has a good formula.”
