# Medicube vs. Truly Beauty Deodorant — Remake & Iteration Plan

**Source video:** `export_1786852866162.mov`  
**Product pair:** Medicube Body Brightening Deodorant + Truly Beauty Soft Serve Serum Deodorant  
**Original post date:** May 23, 2026, 2:46 PM  
**Source duration:** 95.64 seconds in TikTok Studio; 95.7 seconds in the source-video transcript  
**Status:** **Analysis complete. Do not write or film the improved iteration until the iteration plan below is approved.**

---

## 1. Executive Verdict

This is a **search breakout, not a retention breakout**. The video reached **33.9K views**, but **96.1% of its traffic came from Search** and the average viewer watched only **15.5 seconds / 16.2%** of a 95.64-second video. In other words, its “Medicube vs. Truly Beauty” comparison framing successfully captured high-intent searchers, but its opening did not give a cold viewer enough reason to stay.

The core comparison is worth preserving. The iteration should **not** replace the comparison with a different hook family. It should preserve the same pair, same neutral pharmacist verdict, and same search language while changing three variables only:

1. **Hook the problem and decision before naming brands.**
2. **Compress the body to roughly 55–65 seconds.**
3. **Move the situational verdict from 1:14 to approximately 0:40–0:48.**

The faithful remake tests whether the original comparison can earn another search wave. The improved iteration tests whether the same product pair can win **more For You distribution, stronger early retention, and more followers**.

## 3. What the Video Did Well

### A. It matched a real search intent

The title-card language, spoken product names, and direct comparison all reinforced the same query: **“Medicube vs. Truly Beauty deodorant.”** The 96.1% Search share is the clearest reason to keep the pair and comparison architecture.

### B. It maintained an earned pharmacist verdict

The body did not force a winner. It distinguished:

- **Medicube** for sensitive, frequently shaved, irritated, or bumpy underarms; and
- **Truly** for a non-reactive viewer prioritizing exfoliation, texture, and dullness.

That conditional close is the structural asset. It makes the comparison feel like counseling rather than a product pitch.

### C. It created saveable decision value

The final visual grid turns a complex ingredient discussion into a practical self-selection guide. The **1.51% save rate** shows viewers valued the answer even when they did not finish the full video.

### D. It used a dense first minute of visuals

The visual review confirms frequent new stimuli through 0:58: products in hand, condition images, a license graphic, ingredient labels, a tyrosinase diagram, and short benefit overlays. This is better than a static talking-head comparison.

---

## 4. Why Retention Still Failed

### A. The first-second hook is a search label, not a cold-audience promise

The first 4.2 seconds are:

> “Medicube versus Truly Beauty deodorant. Which one actually works for dark underarms?”

This is excellent for someone already searching those two brands. It is less compelling for a cold viewer who does not recognize either bottle. The viewer has to wait until 1:14 to hear the immediate decision that matters: **which one fits their own skin and shaving routine.**

### B. The answer arrives too late

The 14-second comparison verdict at **1:14–1:28** is the most decision-useful part of the video, but only 1.48% of viewers reached the end. The original spends about 58 seconds on ingredient-by-ingredient explanation before the “freshly shaved skin” distinction, then another 14 seconds before the practical choice grid.

### C. The first product creates a long one-sided section

From 0:06–0:31, the viewer gets only Truly’s ingredient explanation. The contrast is delayed until 0:32. In a comparison video, every 5–8 seconds should make the viewer feel the **comparison**, not a product-by-product lecture.

### D. There is a visual lull from 0:59–1:13

The visual analysis identifies this as a long static sequence with only one “AHA can irritate skin” overlay. It follows a visually dense first minute, so the drop in novelty is especially noticeable. This is also where the “what matters for me?” answer should begin, not where the pace slows.

### E. Two end overlays create credibility friction

- **“Can be user together 🤯”** is a visible typo and is unclear on whether the products can be layered the same day.
- The current project protocol is to **alternate** the AHA-based Truly product and Medicube rather than layer both on the same day. The remake must not imply same-day use.

## 6. Improved Iteration Plan — Approve This Before Script Writing

### Iteration objective

Increase first-five-second retention and For You distribution **without sacrificing the product-pair search intent or the conditional pharmacist verdict**.

### What stays fixed

| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| Same two products | The comparison has demonstrated search demand. |
| Same neutral pharmacist conclusion | This is the trust and save-rate driver. |
| Same pink-vs-yellow visual coding | Reduces cognitive load and keeps the comparison scannable. |
| Both products visible in the opening | Comparison format permits early product visibility. |
| Searchable names in speech/caption | Search is currently the distribution engine. |

### What changes

| Change | Exact implementation | Why it addresses the metric |
|---|---|---|
| Cold-audience hook | Start with the shaving/dark-underarm decision, then name the products within 2–3 seconds. | Solves the 0:01 drop-off by giving non-brand-aware viewers immediate stakes. |
| Runtime | Cut from 95.6 seconds to **55–65 seconds**. | A 15.5-second average watch then carries the viewer near the verdict rather than through only one product section. |
| Comparison cadence | Alternate products sooner; never spend more than 8–10 seconds on one without returning to the choice. | Keeps the viewer in a comparison, not a lecture. |
| Verdict timing | Put a one-line conditional answer around **0:08–0:12**, then fully prove it; restate the complete grid at 0:42–0:50. | Gives an early payoff while preserving curiosity about the “why.” |
| Key caveat timing | Bring the post-shave AHA caveat forward to roughly **0:24–0:32** with a razor/underarm visual. | Turns the most practical differentiation into a mid-video rehook. |
| CTA | One line only, after a truthful alternate-days use note. | Eliminates the low-value closing section that viewers currently skip. |
| Follow conversion | Add a non-transactional end line before the point-down: “Follow for pharmacist comparisons that actually tell you who each product is for.” | Gives a reason to follow beyond this one cart decision. |

### Proposed iteration timing map — no final wording yet

| Timing | Section | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Pain + decision hook | “Dark underarms + shaving” problem appears before brand knowledge is required. |
| 0:03–0:06 | Products + pharmacist authority | Both bottles remain visible; product-pair SEO is retained. |
| 0:06–0:12 | Early answer / open loop | State that one is better after shaving and one is better for texture, then promise the reason. |
| 0:12–0:23 | Truly in plain English | One AHA sentence + one consequence sentence; no ingredient list recital. |
| 0:23–0:34 | Medicube in plain English | One kojic-acid sentence + one barrier-support sentence. |
| 0:34–0:42 | Razor rehook / safety boundary | Freshly shaved skin and AHA caveat; explain that different days are safer than layering. |
| 0:42–0:52 | Full “who should buy which” grid | The saveable decision is reached before the average 15.5-second viewer-equivalent progression of the old video. |
| 0:52–1:00 | Link + follow close | Both links + downward point + one repeatable reason to follow. |

### Visual iteration assignment

**Overlay strategy:** Comparison adaptation of Strategy D, but with Strategy A’s strong first-three-second stakes visual.

1. **0:00:** one close-up underarm/shaving-sensitivity visual only; not two competing condition images.
2. **0:00–0:03:** text hook must express the decision, not simply name the brands.
3. **0:03:** license popup while “pharmacist for over a decade” is spoken.
4. **0:12–0:34:** one visual change every 2–3 seconds: product close-up → ingredient label/one benefit overlay → switch product → second benefit overlay.
5. **0:34:** razor icon + “AHA right after shaving can sting” text; this is the rehook.
6. **0:42:** full side-by-side grid with only two criteria per column; the original four-item lists were visually dense.
7. **0:52:** current, product-matched cart/review screenshot if available; then an arrow plus physical downward point.

### Hypothesis and success criteria

| Variable | Current | Iteration target / readout |
|---|---:|---|
| Largest drop-off | 0:01 | Aim for a visibly flatter first 3–5 seconds. |
| Average watch | 15.5s / 16.2% | Aim for at least **20–25%** on the shorter version. |
| Completion | 1.48% | Aim for **3%+**; the exact target depends on final runtime. |
| For You traffic | 2.0% | Increase from the current search-only distribution mix. |
| Save rate | 1.51% | Protect or improve it; the conditional grid remains the save mechanism. |
| Share rate | 0.33% | Reach the stronger end of the 0.3–0.8% project benchmark by making the grid easier to send. |
| Follower rate | 0.09% | Improve with a clear “pharmacist comparisons” follow reason. |
