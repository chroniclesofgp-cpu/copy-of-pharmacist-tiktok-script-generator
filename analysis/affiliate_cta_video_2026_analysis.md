## What the Video Is

The video is a rapid-fire crowd interview built around the persistent on-screen prompt **“pov: what’s your cta.”** Nine speakers deliver one short CTA each, while the microphone moves immediately to the next person. Every speaker finishes with a deliberate downward point; the video does not show a cart, product, or checkout screen.

> **Reusable execution pattern:** direct eye contact → one short, self-contained CTA → physical downward point. The CTA must be the final line, not followed by another product feature or explanation.

## Visual Delivery Rules Observed

| Element | Observed execution | Bank rule proposed |
|---|---|---|
| On-screen text | “pov: what’s your cta” persisted throughout | Do not copy this text into a product script. Use product-specific opening text only in the hook window, then remove it. |
| Spoken length | One short sentence per speaker | Keep an individual script CTA to one or two sentences maximum. |
| Delivery | Direct eye contact, no post-CTA feature recap | The CTA is the final spoken section. |
| Physical action | Every speaker points down | Every product CTA retains a physical downward point at the final link reference. |
| Product/cart visual | No cart shown | In product scripts, pair the downward point with the actual relevant cart/product visual when available. |

## Key Finding

The video validates the **delivery architecture** more strongly than it validates every scarcity statement. Its strongest safe contribution to this project is the short conditional-cart closer:

> “If you want to check it out, I’ll leave it in the orange link down below. But if you can’t see it anymore, that means they’re all gone.”

The next CTA bank should preserve exact creator wording and label each line as **verified stock only**, **conditional cart availability**, **social proof**, **situational comparison**, or **follow/save**. It should never apply a scarcity line by default simply because it performed in an affiliate video.
