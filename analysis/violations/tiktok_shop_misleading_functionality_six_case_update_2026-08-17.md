**Status:** Supersedes the five-case audit for cross-case pattern analysis only. It does not replace individual notice, appeal, or evidence records in the casebook.  
**Evidence boundary:** TikTok’s notices identify quoted content and a policy category; they do not expose the platform’s model features, reviewer notes, or a single trigger phrase. Every conclusion below is therefore evidence-ranked, not a claim about TikTok’s proprietary enforcement system.

## 2. What VC-005 Adds

VC-005 is a materially useful data point because it is a **shorter, list-style linked-product video** that received an enforcement notice within approximately six and a half minutes of posting. It used a persistent on-screen `Results may vary` disclaimer and the standard caption disclaimer. It had no before/after image, altered-condition image, diagnosis, or guarantee. It nevertheless paired a linked product and purchase CTA with broad competitor statements and an ingredient-study-to-product bridge.

> **Immediate implication:** A caption disclaimer, a continuous `Results may vary` overlay, a short runtime, white coat, or the mere absence of before/after imagery is not sufficient to explain or prevent this enforcement category.

## 4. VC-005 Claim Analysis

The notice quoted the following categories of statements. The table separates factual support from the part that should **not** be defended as a universal product effect.

| VC-005 statement category | What is supportable | What exceeds the source boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Standard probiotic does not help break down food or immediate symptoms | Probiotics and digestive enzymes are different product categories. | A categorical claim about what all probiotics can or cannot do, particularly for immediate symptoms. |
| Simethicone only treats symptoms and does nothing for the cause | Simethicone is used for temporary gas-bubble symptom relief. | “Does nothing” for cause in every context; a universal negative comparison. |
| Digestive enzyme complex covers proteins, carbs, fats, dairy, and plant foods | The official formula lists 16 digestive enzymes and formats intended to support the breakdown of multiple meal components. | An implied guarantee that the linked product will solve every viewer’s meal-related symptom or absorption issue. |
| BS50 significantly improved burping, bloating, and gas | The BS50 RCT found a significant **composite** result and individual statistical significance for burping. | Presenting the ingredient result as a complete-product trial or saying every individual endpoint was significant. |
| One capsule, vegan, non-GMO, third-party tested, Made in USA | These are official product/package facts when currently shown on the linked PDP/package. | Using quality facts to imply clinical efficacy. |

## 5. Revised Explanation of the Pattern

The new case **strengthens**, rather than changes, the prior conclusion. The most defensible explanation is a **semantic-risk bundle**: a linked health product is ranked/recommended with a commercial CTA; the video gives the viewer a concrete symptom/body-function payoff; and the product proof is broadened beyond the current listing or the true scope of the cited evidence. VC-005 demonstrates that this bundle can be present in a short format with disclaimers and no condition-result visual.

It does **not** establish that one trigger phrase, a white coat, the word “pharmacist,” the study-title card, or the `Results may vary` text independently caused action. The instant timing makes automated detection plausible but does not reveal which feature(s) were detected. Account history, exact live cart/PDP configuration, the full notice-quotation system, and unobservable platform classifiers remain confounders.

## 6. Operational Changes Supported by the Sixth Case

1. **Treat ingredient-study overlays as commercial proof risk, not as an automatic credibility win.** Do not show an ingredient-only paper in a linked-product video when the spoken line makes it appear to validate the full SKU or every symptom outcome.
2. **Keep Tier-List comparisons category-specific and non-universal.** A lactase can be described as dairy-specific; simethicone can be described as short-term gas-bubble relief. Avoid “does nothing,” “only treats symptoms,” “cannot help,” and “the real cause” language.
3. **Use the strongest exact live listing words for the final product.** Strong, listing-supported language remains appropriate; weak generic phrasing is not required. The constraint is scope—not persuasion.
4. **Do not use disclaimer presence as a filming green light.** Retain it where appropriate, but complete the claim map and visual-claim map independently.
5. **For the next linked digestive-enzyme test, remove ingredient-study screenshots and symptom-result claims from the video itself.** Use physical label/PDP evidence, formula architecture, exact directions, and accurate comparator limits. Preserve study papers for the appeal package unless the live PDP itself authorizes a materially identical study claim.

## 7. Research Status and Next Data Needed

VC-005’s appeal is user-confirmed **approved** after the 491-character appeal and ten-slot claim-mapped screenshot package were submitted. The platform’s stated rationale and exact outcome-screen wording are not preserved, so the approval cannot identify a decisive phrase, visual, or attachment.

Together, VC-004 and VC-005 show that two evidence-backed submissions were approved. By contrast, the preserved in-app appeals for VC-001 and VC-002 state that no supporting documents were attached and were denied. This is a useful operational correlation only; it does not prove causation, clear every original claim, or create a reusable safe template. The highest-value next data point remains any platform-supplied reasoning attached to the VC-005 decision.
