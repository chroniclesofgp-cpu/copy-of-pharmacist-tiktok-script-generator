## Overview: The Four-Type BOF Suite

Every product that has a TOF/MOF education video should have a full BOF suite to convert the warm audience. These four formats work together as a campaign, and all four are templated directly in this document:

| Script Type | Format Name | Funnel Stage | Runtime | Primary Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | BOF Short-Form: Negative Apology Hook | BOF | 30–45 sec | Fast conversion — pain points only, no education, price reveal |
| Type 2 | MOF→BOF Longer-Form: "It's Not Because" | MOF→BOF | 55–75 sec | Benefit stack with one education line per pain point + price reveal |
| Type 3 | Instruction-Correction: "They Shoved This Down Your Throat" | MOF→BOF | 60–90 sec | Fills the how-to-use gap — builds authority + comment engagement |
| Type 4 | Scam-Warning / Criteria-Led BOF ("This Is Fake") | BOF | Varies by path | Path A: literal scam/fake-listing warning (requires real, current counterfeit/seller-mismatch evidence). Path B: criteria-led label-reading education when that evidence doesn't exist. Skip entirely if neither condition is met. |

**A/B Test Rule:** Always run Type 1 and Type 2 as an A/B test. Whichever drives more purchases per view becomes the primary BOF asset.

**Sequencing Rule:** Type 3 (instruction-correction) can run at any point after the first TOF video — it does not require the full campaign to have run first. Types 1 and 2 work best after the audience has seen the mechanism education video. Type 4, when used, typically runs after Types 1–3 have established warm-audience familiarity, since its criteria-led education (Path B) or scam-warning framing (Path A) lands better once the viewer already recognizes the product.

## TYPE 1 — BOF Short-Form: Negative Apology Hook

**Hook Framework:** `apology-reveal` (BOF short-form)  
**Funnel Stage:** BOF  
**Runtime Target:** 30–45 seconds  
**Origin:** First developed for HiSmile Mouthwash (HISMILE_campaign.md) and Toplux Magnesium (SCRIPT_26). Product examples: Medicube Deodorant (SCRIPT_35), Medicube PDRN Multibalm (SCRIPT_38), Dr. Melaxin Calcium Multibalm (SCRIPT_41).

---

### Audit-Required Truthfulness Caveat — Types 1 and 2

The apology and patient-return framing is a **format**, not permission to invent patient history, returns, purchases, or clinical outcomes. Use literal patient-return language only when the underlying event is true and supportable for the creator. If that premise is not true, convert the hook to a clearly hypothetical or audience-facing version while preserving the curiosity mechanism; do not imply that named patients actually returned the product merely to reveal a lower price.

The same rule applies to the benefit stack: use product facts and source-supported ingredient benefits, but do not turn a template placeholder into an unsupported patient result or universal outcome. The price reveal must reflect the current offer and must not claim a lowest price, sellout, or discount that has not been verified on the filming day.

### What This Script Does

The viewer has already seen the TOF video and knows the product. This script does one thing: delivers a price signal through a pharmacist apology. The three "It's not because" lines remind the viewer of the reasons they already know they should buy — framed as **pain points and desired outcomes only**. No mechanism education. No ingredient explanations. Fast. Clean. Ends on CTA.

---

### Structure

```
[HOOK — 0:00–0:06]
One of the five approved negative apology hooks (see below). Each hook now ends on
the bridge phrase "...and the reason might surprise you" — this replaces the old
standalone SETUP beat. Authority ("I'm a pharmacist... recommending to my patients")
is established inline as part of the hook itself, not as a separate line.

[BENEFIT STACK — 0:06–0:28]
"It's not because [PAIN POINT / DESIRED OUTCOME — who is finally getting relief or confidence]."

"It's not because [PAIN POINT / DESIRED OUTCOME — what problem it solves that others don't]."

"And it's not because [PAIN POINT / DESIRED OUTCOME — unique differentiator framed as viewer benefit]."

"All of that is true and it still stands."

[REVEAL — 0:28–0:36]
"They are all returning it because they were buying it full price — and now you can get it for way cheaper on TikTok Shop. I'm going to drop the link below. You won't believe what they're getting it for."

[CTA — 0:36–0:44]
"The only problem is they have been selling out. If you still see that orange cart and you want it, I would grab one before they sell out again." *(point down)*
```

### The Five Approved Negative Apology Hooks (Ranked Strongest to Weakest)

**Hook 1 — Patients Are Returning It (STRONGEST):**
> *"I'm a pharmacist and I have to set the record straight — my patients are starting to return [product] after I recommended it, and the reason might surprise you."*

Why it's strongest: Borrows the highest-converting hook mechanic on TikTok Shop ("I bought this and now I have to return it"). The viewer expects a product failure story and waits for it. Instead they get a full benefit stack. The reveal — that patients are returning it to buy it cheaper — is a complete misdirection payoff. Stakes feel higher coming from a pharmacist recommendation than from a random buyer. This is the one hook in the list that makes a literal "they're returning it" claim, so it's the one the Audit-Required Truthfulness Caveat above applies to most directly — only use it when that premise is genuinely supportable.

**Special reveal language required for Hook 1:**
The reveal must explicitly close the loop on why they're returning it — price, not product failure:
> *"The reason my patients are returning it — TikTok Shop has it at the lowest price I've ever seen. They bought it full price somewhere else on my recommendation. I'm sorry about that."*

---

**Hook 2 — I Really Messed Up:**
> *"I'm a pharmacist and I've been recommending [product] to all of my patients, and I realized I really messed up — but the reason might surprise you."*

Why it's second: Blunter and more casual than "bad news," "apology," or "come clean" — reads as a confession rather than an accusation or a formality, which gives it real tension without asserting a literal return event (safer under the Truthfulness Caveat than Hook 1). Sits just below Hook 1 because it doesn't borrow the specific "return" mechanic, but the "I messed up" framing still creates strong stakes coming from a pharmacist.

---

**Hook 3 — Break Bad News:**
> *"I hate to break the bad news to all of my patients I recommended [product] to — and the reason might surprise you."*

Why it's third: Strong curiosity gap. "Bad news" implies something went wrong without specifying what. The viewer is invested in finding out. The relief reveal (it's just the price) lands well.

---

**Hook 4 — Owe an Apology:**
> *"I owe all of my patients an apology for recommending [product] to them — and the reason might surprise you."*

Why it's fourth: Solid accountability framing. Slightly less tension than "bad news" because "apology" is softer and more expected from a pharmacist. Still converts well.

---

**Hook 5 — Need to Come Clean (WEAKEST of the five):**
> *"I need to come clean to all of my patients I recommended [product] to — and the reason might surprise you."*

Why it's fifth: "Come clean" implies a confession, which creates curiosity — but it's slightly more passive than the others. Still approved and effective, especially when the other four have been used recently on the same feed.

---

**Additional hooks with the same feel (acceptable variations):**
- *"I feel terrible about this — I've been recommending [product] to my patients for years and I just found out something — and the reason might surprise you."*
- *"My patients are going to be upset with me when they see this."*
- *"I've been giving my patients bad advice about where to buy [product]."*

**Rule:** Any hook used must have the same emotional register — pharmacist accountability, not outrage or drama. The reveal is always a price gap, not a product problem. For Hook 1 specifically, the reveal language must explicitly state that the return reason is price — not product quality.

---

### Pain Point vs. Feature — The Critical Distinction

**Pain point / desired outcome (PREFERRED):**
Describes what the viewer is experiencing or wants to experience. Speaks directly to anyone dealing with that problem.

> *"It's not because my patients who have been embarrassed to wear sleeveless shirts for years are finally feeling confident enough to show their arms again."*

**Feature (acceptable when pain point is too difficult or less compelling):**
Names what the product does or contains. Less emotionally resonant but still valid.

> *"It's not because it's the only brightening deodorant I've found that has no AHA."*

**Rule:** Pain point is preferred when it paints a clear picture. Feature is acceptable when the pain point version would be confusing, too long, or less compelling than the feature itself. **Never use mechanism language** (enzyme names, receptor names, clinical terms) in the Type 1 benefit stack — those belong in Type 2.

### 5 Benefit Stack Options — Required Section in Every BOF Script

Every BOF short-form and longer-form script must include 5 fully written benefit stack options. Option 1 is the default (used in the script as written). Options 2–5 are ready-to-swap alternates for A/B testing, targeting different patient populations, or refreshing a script that is no longer converting.

**Why 5 options:**
- Different pain points resonate with different viewers — age, gender, lifestyle, and health history all affect which line lands
- If one angle stops converting, swap in another without rewriting the full script
- Allows systematic A/B testing across the same product
- Covers the full range of reasons someone buys the product

**How to use them:**
- Option 1 is always the strongest opening angle — the most emotionally resonant pain point for the broadest audience
- Options 2–5 are ranked by specificity — broader pain points first, more niche/targeted last
- To swap: replace all three lines in the benefit stack with the three lines from the alternate option
- The setup, reveal, and CTA never change — only the benefit stack lines swap

**Format for each option:**
```
**Option [N] — [Descriptive Label]:**
Line 1: It's not because [pain point / desired outcome].
Line 2: It's not because [pain point / desired outcome].
Line 3: And it's not because [pain point / desired outcome].
[For Type 2 only: include one education sentence after each line]
```

## TYPE 2 — MOF→BOF Longer-Form: "It's Not Because" with Education Lines

**Hook Framework:** `apology-reveal` + one-line mechanism education per benefit  
**Funnel Stage:** MOF→BOF  
**Runtime Target:** 55–75 seconds  
**Origin:** First developed for HiSmile Mouthwash (HISMILE_campaign.md longer-form) and Toplux Magnesium (SCRIPT_26 Longer-Form Script 6). Product examples: Medicube Deodorant (SCRIPT_36), Medicube PDRN Multibalm (SCRIPT_39), Dr. Melaxin Calcium Multibalm (SCRIPT_42).

---

### What This Script Does

Same structure as Type 1 — same negative apology hook, same "It's not because" benefit stack, same price reveal close. The only difference is that each "It's not because" line gets **one additional education sentence** after the pain point. This is the pharmacist-exclusive mechanism explanation that a lifestyle creator would not say. Longer runtime, higher credibility, same close.

### The Education Line — What It Should Do

The education line is one sentence. It should be the pharmacist-exclusive insight that makes the benefit feel real and credible — something a lifestyle creator would not say.

**Strong education lines:**
- Name the mechanism at a biological level: *"— kojic acid blocks the tyrosinase enzyme, which is the enzyme that produces melanin, stopping new darkening before it even starts."*
- Cite clinical data: *"— Volufiline is a patented ingredient that actually stimulates your own fat cells to grow in the areas you apply it, which is how it creates real, lasting volume from beneath the skin."*
- Name a receptor or pathway: *"— PDRN activates the A2A adenosine receptor in your skin, which triggers your fibroblasts to produce new collagen and elastin."*
- Explain why the mechanism matters: *"— ceramide NP is the specific lipid that makes up your skin barrier, and shaving strips it away every single time. This formula actively replaces it while you wear it."*

**Rule:** The education line must be intelligible to a non-pharmacist viewer. If you use a clinical term, explain it in the same sentence. No unexplained jargon.

## TYPE 3 — Instruction-Correction: "They Shoved This Down Your Throat"

**Hook Framework:** `instruction-correction` (MOF)  
**Funnel Stage:** MOF → BOF  
**Runtime Target:** 60–90 seconds  
**Origin:** First developed for Toplux Magnesium (SCRIPT_33). Product examples: Medicube Deodorant (SCRIPT_37), Medicube PDRN Multibalm (SCRIPT_40), Dr. Melaxin Calcium Multibalm (SCRIPT_43).

---

### What This Script Does

This script runs as a response to the saturation of creator videos pushing a product — the viewer has seen the product everywhere but nobody told them how to use it correctly. The pharmacist steps in to fill the how-to-use gap. This builds authority, drives comment engagement (people ask follow-up questions), and converts viewers who bought the product but aren't getting results.

---

### Structure

```
[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]
"So they shoved [product] down your throat and told you [main claim] — but then they didn't tell you
how to use it. Well, I'm a pharmacist, and I'm going to fix that."

[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:13]
"I've been counseling patients on [category] for over a decade. And the number one question I get
after someone buys this is: [QUESTION 1] and [QUESTION 2]."

[ANSWER 1 — 0:13–0:30]
Answer to the first question (timing / when / how often).
Must include the pharmacist-exclusive rationale — not just the instruction, but WHY.

[ANSWER 2 — 0:30–0:45]
Answer to the second question (application / how to use / what to do).
Must include a pharmacist-exclusive insight that no lifestyle creator would think to address.

[PHARMACIST FLAG — 0:45–0:55]
One interaction, contraindication, or layering rule that commonly gets missed.
Must be something no lifestyle creator would know to mention. Generates comments.
Always offer the empathetic alternative if one exists.

[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:55–1:08]
Name the product. Summarize the correct protocol in 2–3 sentences.
Include the realistic timeline for results.

[CTA — 1:08–1:16]
"I'll leave the link in the orange cart below. If you have questions about [specific topic],
drop them in the comments — I read them." *(point down)*
```

---

### Hook Template

> *"So they shoved [product name] down your throat and told you [the main claim everyone is making] — but then they didn't tell you how to use it. Well, I'm a pharmacist, and I'm going to fix that."*

**Rule:** The hook must use the exact phrase "shoved [product] down your throat." Do not soften it. The viewer has seen 20 videos about this product and nobody gave them the protocol. That frustration is the hook.

---

### Choosing the Two Answers

The two answers should be the two questions that every buyer of this product has but no creator answers. Good candidates:

- **Timing:** When in the day / when in a routine should this be used?
- **Application:** Where exactly should it be applied? Most people only use it on the obvious area.
- **Frequency:** Once a day vs. twice a day — and what does the clinical study protocol say?
- **Layering:** What should not be used at the same time or in the same routine?
- **Duration:** How long before results? What does the clinical data say?
- **Dose:** How much is enough? What is the studied dose?

**Rule:** Each answer must have a "why" — the mechanism or clinical data that explains the rule. Without the why, it is just instructions. The why is what makes it a pharmacist video.

---

### Pharmacist Flag — What Belongs Here

The pharmacist flag is the one thing that only a pharmacist would know to mention. It is not a scare tactic — it is a genuine safety note that protects the viewer and builds trust.

Good pharmacist flags:
- Ingredient-specific allergy risk (e.g., PDRN derived from salmon DNA — fish allergy flag)
- Drug interaction (e.g., iron/magnesium separation)
- Contraindication with another common product in the same routine (e.g., retinol layering)
- Timing conflict with another supplement or medication

**Rule:** Always offer the empathetic alternative if there is one. "If you have X, you can't use this" is incomplete. "If you have X, here is what to do instead / here is how to check" is complete.

## Decision Guide: Which Type to Write First? (Types 1–3)

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| TOF video just went live, product is new to your audience | Write Type 1 and Type 2 now. Run as A/B test. |
| Product has been in your feed for a while, lots of creator saturation | Write Type 3 first — fills the how-to-use gap immediately. |
| Product has a complex protocol (timing, layering, dose) | Type 3 is the priority — that is where the pharmacist value is highest. |
| Product has a simple protocol but strong pain points | Type 1 and Type 2 are the priority — pain point stack converts faster. |
| You want to run all three | Write Type 3 first (can run anytime), then Type 1 and Type 2 as A/B test. |

## Quality Checklist — All Four Types

Before any BOF suite script is considered complete, verify:

**Type 1 — BOF Short-Form:**

## Type 4 — Scam-Warning + Label-Reading ("This Is Fake")

**Audit Evidence Boundary:** Path A is permitted only when real counterfeit, seller-mismatch, or unverifiable-listing evidence has been captured. Path B is the criteria-led alternative when the product has three or more source-verified differentiators; it must not falsely label ordinary competing products as counterfeit. If neither evidence condition is met, skip Type 4.

**Hook Framework:** `scam-warning` (Hook 29)
**Funnel Stage:** TOF/BOF hybrid — cold-audience entry point that closes immediately
**Runtime Target:** 60–75 seconds
**Core Angle (two execution paths — see below):**
- **Path A (Consumer Protection):** Scam hook → "gone crazy viral" framing → verification tutorial (blue checkmark + sales volume) → supplement regulation line → brief benefit → CTA
- **Path B (Label-Reading Criteria):** Scam hook → pivot to label-reading → 3 criteria most competitors fail → product passes all three → CTA

**Status:** Validated across 4 Riva videos (August 10, 2026). Structure is consistent and reliable. Needs 2+ additional healthcare creators for cross-creator universal rule status.

---

### Why This Hook Works

The scam-warning hook borrows the highest-converting scroll-stop mechanic on TikTok: the viewer expects to be warned away from something. Instead, the pharmacist uses the warning to establish the criteria a good product must meet — and then reveals that the recommended product passes all of them. The viewer who was about to scroll past now has a reason to stay: they want to know if the product they already bought is real or fake.

The triple repetition ("This is fake. This is a scam. And this is fake.") synchronized with pointing at three different fake listing screenshots creates a visual rhythm that is extremely difficult to scroll past. Each point lands on a new image — the viewer's eye is constantly moving.

---

### Visual Hook

White coat. **FOUR** fake/competing product listing screenshots pop up in the four corners around your head (edited in post-production). Point at each one as it appears. Both text boxes visible:
- **Box 1:** "Watch out for SCAMS ⚠️" (color varies — white, yellow, or red background all confirmed)
- **Box 2:** "From a Pharmacist" (white text on **RED** background — this is consistent across all 4 reference videos, do not change)

Both text boxes disappear the moment the real product appears (at the pivot).

**4 fakes is the standard.** If you cannot find 4 real fake/competing listings, use 3 minimum. Do not fabricate listings.

Each beat lands on a different product image as you point at it. Deliver flat and fast — no inflection, no pause. The rhythm is the hook. 4 beats for 4 fakes.

### Fill-In Rules

**The three criteria must:**
1. Be real ingredients with clinical data or mechanism support — verified in the intel doc
2. Be explained in plain language — no unexplained clinical terms
3. Each have a corresponding checkmark in the education overlay stack

**The viral reason section must:**
- Hold up the real product immediately when the fakes disappear
- Name the hero ingredient and explain why it works in plain language
- Reference a study or clinical data if one exists for the hero ingredient

**The scam warning must:**
- Come AFTER the education section — the viewer must want the product before the scam warning lands
- Frame the pharmacist as a protector ("I want to make sure you're getting the one that works") not an evaluator
- Show the pharmacist license screenshot here — authority is most credible in the protection context

- Include the "I'll always link the correct one in my videos" trust anchor — NON-NEGOTIABLE
- Include the blue checkmark instruction — NON-NEGOTIABLE
- Include the sales volume number — reinforces that the real product has a track record

### Decision Guide Update (Type 4)

**Which path to use:**

| Situation | Path | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Real knockoff listings exist on TikTok Shop for this product | Path A | Consumer protection framing is most credible when the threat is real |
| Product has 3+ verifiable label differentiators that most competitors fail | Path B | Pharmacist-exclusive angle — stronger conversion when differentiators exist |
| Both conditions are true | Path B | Label-reading is the stronger pharmacist angle — leads with expertise, not just consumer protection |
| Neither condition is confirmed | Skip Type 4 | Use Type 1/2/3 instead |

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Product has gone viral and knockoff listings exist on TikTok Shop | Type 4 is the priority — scam-warning hook captures both cold and warm audiences |
| Product category is saturated with low-quality competitors | Type 4 — use the label-reading structure to differentiate |
| Product has 3+ verifiable label differentiators that most competitors fail | Type 4 is viable — do not use if you cannot find 3 real differentiators |
| Product is new to TikTok Shop with no knockoff problem yet | Skip Type 4 — use Type 1/2/3 instead |
