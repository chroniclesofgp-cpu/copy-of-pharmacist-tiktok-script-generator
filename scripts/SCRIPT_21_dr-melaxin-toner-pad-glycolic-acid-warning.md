**[PROBLEM — The Hidden Ingredient]**
Most people buying a brightening toner pad think they are getting kojic acid, tranexamic acid, maybe some niacinamide. And yes — this pad has all of those. But here is what most people do not know: it also contains glycolic acid.

Glycolic acid is an AHA — an alpha hydroxy acid. It is an exfoliant. And exfoliants increase your skin's sensitivity to UV light. That is not a side effect — that is how they work. They remove the outer layer of dead skin cells so the brightening actives can penetrate deeper. But that also means the fresh skin underneath is more exposed to the sun.

---

**[GAP — The Backfire Mechanism]**
So here is what happens. You use your brightening toner pad in the morning. You go outside. UV hits that freshly exfoliated skin. Your melanocytes — the cells that produce pigment — respond to that UV exposure by producing more melanin. More melanin means darker spots.

You are using a dark spot treatment that is actively triggering more dark spots — because you skipped one step.

*[On-screen overlay: "Glycolic Acid → Removes Dead Cells → Fresh Skin Exposed → UV → More Melanin → Darker Spots"]*

---

**[EDUCATION — The Fix]**
The fix is simple, but it is non-negotiable. If you are using this pad in the morning, you must follow it with SPF. Not a moisturizer with SPF. An actual dedicated SPF — mineral or chemical, at least SPF 30. That is what blocks the UV from triggering the melanin response that undoes everything the pad is doing.

If you do not want to add SPF to your morning routine, use this pad at night. At night there is no UV exposure, the glycolic acid does its exfoliation work while you sleep, and in the morning your skin has already started the brightening process without any of the backfire risk.

## Filming Notes

- **This is a safety-first video.** Lead with the warning, not the product. The product reveal comes after the mechanism is fully explained — this is the correct structure for a suppressed-knowledge hook.
- **Do not say "makes dark spots worse" without immediately explaining why.** The mechanism (glycolic acid → UV sensitivity → more melanin) must follow within 5 seconds of the hook or the claim feels irresponsible.
- **The SPF message is the pharmacist authority moment.** Deliver it with confidence — this is the kind of thing a pharmacist knows that a skincare influencer does not.
- **Product hold-up:** Show the pad container. If possible, pull a pad out and show the texture — the physical demonstration reinforces that this is a real product review, not a generic skincare lecture.
- **Night use alternative:** If your audience skews toward people who already have a morning routine they do not want to change, the "use it at night instead" option is a strong empathy moment — you are giving them a solution, not a criticism.
