**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:32]**

When you shave you're underarms you are disrupting your skin barrier every single time. Shaving removes the top layer of skin cells along with the hair. That barrier disruption is what causes the irritation, the bumps, and over time, the darkening. Your skin is in a constant state of low-grade inflammation and repair. Now here is the problem with most brightening deodorants: they use AHAs — alpha-hydroxy acids — to exfoliate. AHA on freshly shaved skin is like putting an acid on an open wound. It stings, it irritates, and it can make the inflammation worse — which can actually deepen the darkening over time.

**[REHOOK — 0:32–0:37]

So what does your skin actually need after shaving? 

**[MECHANISM — 0:37–1:05]**Ceramides are the lipids — the fats — that make up your skin barrier. When you shave, you strip some of those ceramides away.

A deodorant with ceramides in the formula can actively replace what shaving strips away — so your skin is repairing at the same time as it's brightening

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 1:15–1:28]**The one I recommend for sensitive skin and frequent shavers is the Medicube Body Brightening Deodorant. it has ceramide NP in the formula to replace the barrier protecting your skin. So while the kojic acid in this formula is working upstream — blocking the enzyme that produces melanin before new pigmentation forms — the ceramide is simultaneously repairing the barrier damage that shaving caused. That is two jobs happening at the same time: brightening and healing. Most deodorants do one. This one does both. And because there is no AHA, you can apply it immediately after shaving without any stinging or irritation.Kojic acid for brightening, ceramide NP for barrier repair, — no aluminu. It is a Korean skincare formula applied to a body care product, and the ingredient logic is sound.
