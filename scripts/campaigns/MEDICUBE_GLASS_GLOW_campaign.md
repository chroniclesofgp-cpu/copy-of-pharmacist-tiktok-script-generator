## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
Nobody talks about this — but this ingredient in your skincare set is actually a registered pharmaceutical drug in South Korea and Europe. Here is what it does — and why a pharmacist recognizes it immediately.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:15]**
I'm a pharmacist. I have been looking at pharmaceutical ingredient mechanisms for over a decade. And when I saw PDRN on this label, I recognized it — because it is used in clinical medicine for wound healing, tissue repair, and anti-inflammatory treatment. It is not just a skincare ingredient. It is a drug.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:30]**
Most skincare ingredients are cosmetic actives — they work on the surface of the skin or in the upper layers. PDRN is different. It has a pharmacological mechanism — a specific receptor it binds to, a specific cellular pathway it activates. And that mechanism is the reason it works for skin repair at a level most cosmetic ingredients cannot reach.

**[MECHANISM — 0:30–1:00]**
PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide. It is derived from salmon DNA. Here is the mechanism. PDRN binds to adenosine A2A receptors on your skin cells. When those receptors are activated, two things happen simultaneously. First — it inhibits NF-κB, which is the master switch for inflammation in your cells. Turning that switch off reduces the chronic low-grade inflammation that accelerates skin aging. Second — it stimulates VEGF, which promotes the formation of new blood vessels in the dermis. Better blood supply means better oxygen and nutrient delivery to your skin cells. And it upregulates CTGF — connective tissue growth factor — which directly stimulates collagen synthesis by your fibroblasts. That is anti-inflammation, improved circulation, and collagen production through one receptor. That is a pharmaceutical mechanism. Not a cosmetic claim.

**[GAP — 1:00–1:10]**
The honest caveat: the clinical studies on PDRN involve injectable concentrations — higher than what is in a cosmetic product. The mechanism is the same. The magnitude of effect is more modest in a topical formula. But the mechanism is real, and it is the reason this set has 514,000 sold and a 4.7-star rating.

The set is the Medicube Glass Glow Skincare Set. 8 products — cleanser, AHA/BHA exfoliant pads, PDRN toner, exosome spicule shot, PDRN serum, collagen cream, gel mask, and overnight wrapping mask. The PDRN is in multiple products in this set. Every layer is building on the one before it.

## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
Exosomes in skincare sound impressive — but most of them cannot actually get into your skin. This product figured out how to solve that. And it is the most pharmacologically interesting step in this entire set.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. The delivery problem in skincare is something I think about constantly — because an ingredient that cannot penetrate the skin barrier is just sitting on the surface doing nothing. And exosomes have a penetration problem.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:30]**
Exosomes are nano-sized vesicles — tiny packages — that cells use to communicate with each other. They carry proteins, lipids, and genetic material. When you apply them to skin, they have the potential to deliver bioactive cargo to your skin cells — stimulating regeneration, reducing inflammation, promoting collagen production. The problem is the stratum corneum — the outer layer of your skin. It is designed to keep things out. Exosomes are large enough that without a delivery mechanism, most of them cannot cross that barrier. They sit on the surface and get washed off.

**[MECHANISM — 0:30–0:58]**
This product — the Exosome Spicule Shot — solves that problem with calcium spicules. Spicules are microscopic needle-like structures derived from sea sponge. When you apply them to your skin and massage them in, they physically create micro-channels in the stratum corneum. Those channels are temporary — they close within hours. But while they are open, the exosomes in this formula can penetrate through them to reach the living skin cells below. The calcium in the spicules also activates fibroblasts directly — calcium-dependent signaling triggers collagen production. And the Vitamin D in the REBORNIC technology activates VDR receptors in keratinocytes — the same pathway that promotes skin barrier repair. So the spicule is doing three jobs: creating the delivery channel, activating fibroblasts, and triggering barrier repair. The exosomes are the cargo. The spicule is the delivery vehicle.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:58–1:10]**
This is step four in the Medicube Glass Glow Set — the Zero Exosome Spicule Shot. You apply it after the AHA/BHA pads and the PDRN toner, when the skin surface is already prepped. The micro-channels it creates then allow the PDRN serum and collagen cream applied afterward to penetrate more deeply as well. The order in this set is not random. Each step is setting up the next one.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:10–1:22]**
The redness and tingling you feel when you apply this — that is the micro-channel formation. It is not an allergic reaction. It should resolve within 30 to 60 minutes. If you have active rosacea, broken skin, or very reactive skin, skip this step and use the rest of the set. Also — the set contains fragrance in several products. Sensitive skin should patch test the full set before committing.
