## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
This cream says it has NAD+ in it. Here is what that actually means — and what you should actually care about.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:15]**
I'm a pharmacist. I look at ingredient labels the way most people look at menus — I want to know exactly what is in there and why. And when I saw this one, there was a more interesting story underneath the marketing.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:30]**
NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — is a coenzyme your cells use for energy production and DNA repair. As you age, your NAD+ levels decline. That decline is linked to skin aging, reduced cellular repair, and loss of elasticity. The idea of putting NAD+ in a cream makes sense in theory. Here is the challenge.

**[MECHANISM — 0:30–0:58]**
NAD+ is a large, charged molecule. The rule in dermatology is the 500 Dalton rule — only molecules under 500 Daltons can reliably penetrate the skin barrier. NAD+ is approximately 663 Daltons. It does not readily cross the stratum corneum — the outer layer of skin — to reach the cells where it would need to work. So the NAD+ listed on this label is real. But the more important story is what else is in this formula. Niacinamide — also listed — is a direct precursor to NAD+. Your skin cells take up niacinamide and convert it through the salvage pathway into NAD+. So instead of trying to deliver NAD+ from the outside — which is difficult — this formula delivers the building block your cells use to make their own. That is actually the smarter approach.

**[GAP — 0:58–1:08]**
The brand markets this as a NAD+ cream. The honest version is: this is a niacinamide cream that supports your skin's ability to produce its own NAD+. That is not a lesser claim. It is a more accurate one. And the rest of the formula is genuinely strong.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 1:08–1:22]**
Beyond the niacinamide, this cream has 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid — a stable Vitamin C derivative — combined with ferulic acid, which doubles the antioxidant efficacy of Vitamin C. It has ceramide NP for barrier repair, adenosine for collagen synthesis, and seven forms of hyaluronic acid for multi-depth hydration. That is a serious anti-aging formula. The NAD+ marketing is just the least accurate part of the story.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:22–1:35]**
One honest caveat — this formula contains fragrance, which can be an irritant for sensitive skin. If you have reactive skin, patch test first. Also — the 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid is more stable than regular Vitamin C, but it still degrades with heat and light. Store this away from direct sunlight.

## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
The reason the Vitamin C in this cream works better than most Vitamin C serums — it is not the ingredient. It is the form. And the ferulic acid next to it is doubling its efficacy.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. I have been counseling patients on antioxidant skincare for years. And the Vitamin C conversation is one I have constantly — because most people are using the wrong form.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:28]**
Regular Vitamin C — L-ascorbic acid — is the most studied form. But it is also the most unstable. It oxidizes rapidly when exposed to air and light, turning yellow and then brown. By the time many Vitamin C serums reach your skin, a significant portion of the active ingredient has already degraded. You are applying an oxidized product. And oxidized Vitamin C does not just stop working — it can actually generate free radicals, which is the opposite of what you want from an antioxidant.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:52]**
This cream uses 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid. It is a Vitamin C derivative where an ethyl group has been attached to the molecule — that structural change makes it significantly more stable. It does not oxidize as readily. It penetrates the skin barrier more effectively than L-ascorbic acid. And once inside the skin, it is converted back to active Vitamin C by skin enzymes. Now here is the ferulic acid part. Ferulic acid is a phenolic antioxidant that has been shown in published research to double the photoprotective efficacy of Vitamin C derivatives when the two are combined. It also inhibits the enzymes that break down collagen and elastin. So the ferulic acid is doing two jobs: amplifying the Vitamin C and independently protecting your collagen.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:52–1:05]**
The cream is JiYu NAD+ Brightening Cream. The 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid plus ferulic acid combination is the same pairing that SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic pioneered — except this formula adds niacinamide, ceramide NP, adenosine, and seven forms of hyaluronic acid on top of it. That is a complete anti-aging stack in one product.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:05–1:18]**
One honest note — even 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid degrades over time with heat and light exposure. Store this away from direct sunlight and do not leave it in a hot bathroom. Also — this formula contains fragrance. If you have sensitive skin, patch test before committing.
