## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
BHA and AHA are both exfoliants — but they work completely differently. And if you have pores or acne, using the wrong one is why you are not seeing results.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. I explain drug mechanisms for a living. And the AHA versus BHA distinction is one of the clearest examples of how chemistry determines what an ingredient can and cannot do in your skin.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:28]**
Most people think of exfoliants as interchangeable — they all remove dead skin cells, so they should all work the same way. They do not. The difference comes down to one chemical property: whether the molecule is water-soluble or oil-soluble. And that single property determines where in your skin each exfoliant can actually reach.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:55]**
AHAs — glycolic acid, lactic acid — are water-soluble. They dissolve in water. Your skin surface has water in it, so AHAs work well there — they loosen the bonds between dead skin cells and improve surface texture. But your pores are lined with sebum — oil. A water-soluble molecule cannot dissolve into an oil environment. So AHAs cannot penetrate the pore lining. They work on the surface and stop there. BHA — salicylic acid — is lipophilic. Oil-soluble. It can dissolve into sebum. That means it can penetrate the follicle lining, dissolve the sebum plug inside the pore, and exfoliate the pore wall from within. That is why BHA clears blackheads and reduces pore appearance when AHAs cannot. And salicylic acid is also anti-inflammatory — it inhibits prostaglandin synthesis, which reduces the redness and swelling of active acne. AHAs have no anti-inflammatory mechanism. So for active acne, BHA is pharmacologically the correct choice. Not AHA.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:55–1:07]**
The Zero Pore Pad in the Medicube Mix & Match Set has salicylic acid plus lactic acid — BHA for pore penetration and AHA for surface exfoliation in the same pad. The Kojic Acid Turmeric Pad adds glycolic acid plus gluconolactone for brightening maintenance. The Red Succinic Acid Pad is the post-breakout repair step. Three pads. Three mechanisms. Used on different days based on what your skin needs.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:07–1:18]**
Do not use all three in one session — over-exfoliation. The Kojic Acid Pad with glycolic acid requires SPF in the morning. And the Zero Pore Pad contains denatured alcohol — it is formulated for oily and acne-prone skin. If you have dry or sensitive skin, the Red Succinic Acid Pad is your daily driver.

## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
This ingredient in the red pad — nobody explains what it actually does. And it is doing something for post-breakout skin that most people have never heard of.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. I recognize succinic acid from biochemistry — not from skincare marketing. And when I saw it on this label, I knew exactly what it was doing. Most creators do not explain this one.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:28]**
After a breakout clears, most people have two problems. The first is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation — the dark mark left behind. The second is that the skin at the breakout site is in a state of cellular fatigue. The inflammation has stressed the local cells, disrupted the barrier, and left the skin in a low-energy, slow-repair state. Most post-acne products address the pigmentation. Very few address the cellular energy problem. Succinic acid addresses both.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:55]**
Succinic acid is a dicarboxylic acid. Your body produces it naturally — it is an intermediate in the citric acid cycle, which is the metabolic pathway your cells use to produce ATP, the energy currency of the cell. When you apply succinic acid topically, it stimulates mitochondrial activity in skin cells — essentially giving fatigued cells an energy boost that accelerates repair and regeneration. It has also been shown to induce positive effects on dermal senescent fibroblasts — cells that have essentially stopped functioning properly due to age or stress — helping them resume normal collagen production. On top of that, succinic acid has gentle exfoliation properties, anti-inflammatory effects, and sebum-balancing activity. The betaine salicylate in this pad adds a gentler BHA mechanism — same pore-penetrating lipophilic action as salicylic acid, but at a higher pH that reduces irritation. So the red pad is: cellular energy restoration, gentle exfoliation, anti-inflammation, and sebum balance. That is the post-breakout repair story.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:55–1:07]**
The Red Succinic Acid Panthenol Pad is the second pad in the Medicube Mix & Match Set. You use it in the days after a breakout clears — when the skin needs repair, not more aggressive exfoliation. The panthenol — provitamin B5 — adds barrier soothing and hydration. The niacinamide blocks melanin transfer to prevent the dark mark from forming.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:07–1:18]**
This is the gentlest pad in the set — suitable for daily use even on sensitive skin. Do not combine it with the Zero Pore Pad or Kojic Acid Pad in the same session. And if you are using niacinamide in other products in your routine, that is fine — niacinamide does not have a stacking limit.
