## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
This toner pad has an ingredient in it that most people have never heard of — and it is doing something that most serums three times the price cannot. Nobody is explaining this.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:15]**
I'm a pharmacist. When I look at a skincare ingredient list, I am looking for the actives that have real clinical mechanisms behind them. And when I saw maltobionic acid on this label, I recognized it immediately — because it is one of the most pharmacologically interesting exfoliants in cosmetic chemistry.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:30]**
Most people think about skincare in terms of what you add. Collagen creams. Peptide serums. Vitamin C. And those things matter. But there is a parallel process happening in your skin that most products ignore — the destruction side. Your skin produces enzymes called matrix metalloproteinases — MMPs. Their job is to break down and remodel the collagen and elastin in your dermis. When you are young, that process is balanced — breakdown and synthesis happen at roughly the same rate. As you age, the breakdown accelerates. And UV exposure, pollution, and inflammation all accelerate it further. Most anti-aging products focus entirely on adding collagen. This ingredient stops the breakdown.

**[MECHANISM — 0:30–1:00]**
Maltobionic acid is a polyhydroxy bionic acid — a next-generation exfoliant. Here is what makes it different from AHAs. AHAs like glycolic acid exfoliate by loosening the bonds between dead skin cells — that is their primary job. Maltobionic acid does that too, but it also inhibits matrix metalloproteinases — the enzymes I just described that degrade your collagen. It also reduces UV-induced lipid peroxidation — that is the oxidative damage that UV light causes to the fats in your skin cell membranes. And it functions as a humectant, retaining more water than lactobionic acid. Clinical studies show improvement in fine lines, wrinkles, elasticity, firmness, texture, and clarity. And because of its larger molecular size compared to glycolic acid, it penetrates more slowly — which means it is non-irritating and suitable for sensitive skin. This is the most underexplained ingredient in this formula.

## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
If you have dark spots and you are only using one ingredient to treat them, here is why it is not working as well as it should — and why these toner pads are doing it differently.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. Hyperpigmentation is one of the most common concerns I see. And the reason most people are not getting the results they want is that they are only targeting one step in a two-step process.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:28]**
Here is how dark spots form. Your skin has cells called melanocytes — they produce melanin, the pigment that gives skin its color. When your skin is exposed to UV light, inflammation, or hormonal changes, those melanocytes go into overdrive. They produce excess melanin. That melanin then gets packaged into structures called melanosomes and transferred to the surrounding skin cells — that is what you see as a dark spot. Most brightening products target step one: melanin production. But if you only block production and do not address the transfer, the existing melanin still gets delivered. You need to interrupt both steps.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:52]**
Alpha-arbutin targets step one. It is a hydroquinone derivative — but unlike hydroquinone, which permanently inactivates the tyrosinase enzyme, alpha-arbutin competitively inhibits it. That means it blocks the enzyme's active site without destroying it — which gives you a better safety profile and less risk of paradoxical darkening that can happen with aggressive tyrosinase inhibitors. It is also significantly more stable and less irritating than kojic acid, which degrades quickly and can cause contact dermatitis in some people. Niacinamide targets step two. It inhibits the transfer of melanosomes from the melanocytes to the surrounding cells — so even if some melanin is produced, it cannot get delivered to the surface. Two separate steps. One product.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 0:52–1:05]**
The pads are JiYu Korean Skincare Toner Pads. Alpha-arbutin plus niacinamide for the dual-pathway brightening. Maltobionic acid for exfoliation and MMP inhibition. Centella Asiatica for anti-inflammatory support. Snail secretion filtrate and EGF for regeneration. This is a complete brightening and anti-aging pad.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:05–1:18]**
Honest caveats: multiple actives means introduce gradually if you have sensitive skin. Snail secretion filtrate can cross-react with dust mite allergies — patch test if that applies to you. And results from brightening ingredients take four to eight weeks — this is not an overnight product.
