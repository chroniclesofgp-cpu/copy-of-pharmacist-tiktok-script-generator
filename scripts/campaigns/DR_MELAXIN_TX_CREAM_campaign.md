Tranexamic acid inhibits plasminogen activator — it interrupts the UV-melanin signaling cascade before melanin production is even triggered. It is blocking the signal, not the enzyme. A 2014 study in the Journal of Research in Medical Sciences — PMID 25422661 — found that topical TXA is effective and safe for melasma, comparable to hydroquinone with fewer side effects. No rebound hyperpigmentation. No safety concerns at cosmetic concentrations.

A 2014 study found TXA comparable to hydroquinone for melasma with fewer side effects. And TXA is stable — it does not oxidize. It works at a neutral pH — no irritation. And it is safe for sensitive skin and during pregnancy at cosmetic concentrations.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:25]**
Hyperpigmentation is a multi-step process. UV light triggers a signaling cascade that tells your melanocytes to produce melanin. The melanin is then packaged into structures called melanosomes. Those melanosomes are transferred from the melanocytes to the surrounding keratinocytes — the surface skin cells — where they accumulate and create the visible dark spot. Most brightening products target one step. This combination targets two.

**[MECHANISM — 0:25–0:55]**

Even if some melanin is produced — because no single ingredient blocks 100% of synthesis — niacinamide stops it from reaching the surface. You are blocking the problem at the source and catching what gets through at the next gate. A 2002 study in the British Journal of Dermatology — PMID 12100180 — found that niacinamide significantly decreased hyperpigmentation and increased skin lightness after four weeks by inhibiting melanosome transfer. The TXA and niacinamide combination is the dual-checkpoint approach that dermatologists recommend for stubborn melasma and PIH.

I'm a pharmacist. Glutathione is a tripeptide — glycine, cysteine, and glutamate — that your body produces naturally. It is the most abundant intracellular antioxidant in the human body. And in the pharmacy world, oral glutathione has become one of the most discussed supplements for skin brightening — particularly in Southeast Asia, where it has been used for decades.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:28]**
Most people who want the brightening benefit of glutathione are buying it as an oral supplement. That works — oral glutathione increases systemic glutathione levels, which can shift melanin synthesis. But it is expensive, requires consistent daily dosing, and the bioavailability of oral glutathione is debated. Topical glutathione is a more direct approach — you are delivering it to the skin cells where it needs to work.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:55]**
Here is what glutathione does for skin brightening. Your melanocytes produce two types of melanin: eumelanin — which is dark brown to black — and pheomelanin — which is lighter, yellow to reddish. The ratio of eumelanin to pheomelanin determines how dark your skin tone appears. Glutathione inhibits tyrosinase and shifts melanin synthesis away from eumelanin toward pheomelanin. Less dark melanin, more light melanin — the result is a brightening and evening of skin tone. Glutathione also has antioxidant activity — it neutralizes reactive oxygen species that would otherwise trigger melanin production. So it is addressing the oxidative stress component of hyperpigmentation simultaneously. In this cream, glutathione is working alongside tranexamic acid — which blocks the UV-melanin signal — and niacinamide — which blocks melanin transfer. Three separate mechanisms. Three separate checkpoints.
