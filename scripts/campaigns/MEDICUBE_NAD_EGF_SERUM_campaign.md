## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
Most NAD+ skincare products have a problem that nobody talks about. This one actually solved it. And the solution is two ingredients most creators have never heard of.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:15]**
I'm a pharmacist. NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — is a coenzyme your cells use for energy production, DNA repair, and sirtuin activation. Sirtuins are proteins that regulate cellular aging. As you age, your NAD+ levels decline, and that decline is linked to reduced cellular repair, loss of skin elasticity, and accelerated aging. The idea of replenishing NAD+ through skincare is legitimate. The problem is delivery.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:32]**
Here is what most NAD+ skincare brands do not tell you. There is an enzyme in your skin called CD38 — a NAD+ glycohydrolase. Its job is to degrade NAD+. When you apply topical NAD+, CD38 breaks it down before it can reach the cells where it needs to work. Most NAD+ skincare products put NAD+ in the formula and stop there. They do not address the CD38 problem. So the NAD+ gets applied, CD38 degrades it, and the benefit you were promised does not fully materialize.

**[MECHANISM — 0:32–1:00]**
This serum addresses the CD38 problem directly. It contains quercetin — a flavonoid — and enoxolone, which is 18β-glycyrrhetinic acid derived from licorice root. Both of these compounds inhibit CD38 activity. When CD38 is inhibited, NAD+ degradation slows. More NAD+ survives long enough to reach the skin cells where it activates sirtuins, supports mitochondrial function, and promotes DNA repair. A 2024 study published in the journal Cells — PMID 39513906 — validated exactly this mechanism. Quercetin and enoxolone significantly boosted the anti-aging effects of exogenous NAD+ in skin cells through CD38 inhibition, mediated by improved sirtuin activation, autophagy, and mitochondrial functionality. That is the first published validation of this specific combination. And this serum has both ingredients.

**[GAP — 1:00–1:10]**
The niacinamide in this formula adds a second NAD+ pathway — your skin cells convert niacinamide into NAD+ through the salvage pathway. So this serum is delivering NAD+ from the outside while protecting it from CD38, and simultaneously providing the building block your cells use to make their own NAD+ from the inside.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 1:10–1:22]**
The serum is the Medicube NAD+ EGF Firming Serum. Beyond the NAD+ system, it has a full growth factor complex — EGF, IGF-1, bFGF, VEGF — and eight peptides including Copper Tripeptide-1 and Matrixyl. **[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:25]**
The reason anti-aging skincare often underdelivers is that it targets one pathway. One ingredient for collagen. One ingredient for hydration. Skin aging is not a single-pathway problem — it is a multi-system decline. Collagen production slows. Cell turnover slows. Blood vessel density in the dermis decreases. Fibroblast activity declines. A formula that addresses all of those simultaneously is doing something fundamentally different from a single-active serum.

**[MECHANISM — 0:25–0:58]**
EGF — epidermal growth factor. Binds to EGFR receptors on keratinocytes and fibroblasts, activates tyrosine kinase signaling, promotes cell proliferation and migration. This is the growth factor used clinically in wound healing for diabetic ulcers. For cosmetic anti-aging, the evidence is promising — a 2021 systematic review found efficacy in skin wound regeneration and positive results in anti-aging trials, though most trials are uncontrolled. IGF-1 — insulin-like growth factor 1. Promotes fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis through the PI3K/Akt pathway. Works synergistically with EGF. bFGF — basic fibroblast growth factor. Stimulates fibroblast proliferation and collagen I and III synthesis. Also promotes angiogenesis — new blood vessel formation in the dermis, which improves oxygen and nutrient delivery. VEGF — vascular endothelial growth factor. The primary driver of angiogenesis. Promotes new capillary formation in the dermis. Better vascular supply means better delivery of everything your skin needs for repair. Together: cell proliferation from EGF, collagen synthesis from IGF-1 and bFGF, and improved dermis vascularization from VEGF. That is a complete anti-aging growth factor system.

## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
If you are using this serum and not seeing results — here is the step most people skip. And it is the step that determines whether the NAD+ actually reaches your cells.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:14]**
I'm a pharmacist. Ingredient delivery is as important as ingredient selection. And with NAD+ skincare specifically, the application protocol matters more than most people realize.

**[PROBLEM — 0:14–0:28]**
The most common mistake: applying the serum on top of a thick moisturizer or occlusive product. NAD+ and growth factors need to reach the living skin cells below the stratum corneum. If you apply them on top of a heavy occlusive layer, you are reducing their ability to penetrate. The second mistake: inconsistent use. The CD38 inhibitor system in this serum — quercetin and enoxolone — needs consistent daily application to maintain suppressed CD38 activity. Skipping days allows CD38 to recover, and the NAD+ protection drops.

**[MECHANISM — 0:28–0:52]**
The right protocol. Step one: cleanse. Remove the barrier of sebum, dead cells, and product residue that would block penetration. Step two: if you have a toner — apply it first. A water-based toner like the PDRN toner primes the skin without creating an occlusive barrier. Step three: apply the NAD+ EGF Serum to slightly damp skin. Damp skin has a temporarily disrupted stratum corneum — penetration is enhanced. Step four: wait 60 to 90 seconds before applying moisturizer. This gives the actives time to begin absorbing before you seal them in. Step five: apply your moisturizer or cream on top. The occlusive layer now seals the actives in rather than blocking them out. Use this serum at night — growth factors and NAD+ work with your skin's natural overnight repair cycle.
