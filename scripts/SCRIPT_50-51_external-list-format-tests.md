# SCRIPT 50 — Medicube Body Brightening Deodorant
## Do / Don’t Contrast List Test

| Field | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Funnel stage** | MOF → BOF |
| **Target runtime** | 40–50 seconds |
| **Test variable** | Balanced 2×2 green Do / red Don’t grid: concise cause-based education versus the existing comparison and expert-verdict formats |
| **CTA ID** | U-02 — Conditional cart FOMO |
| **Product reveal** | 0:16–0:20, after the full 2×2 grid establishes the decision criteria |

## Triple Hook

| Layer | Execution |
|---|---|
| **Visual hook** | A green **DO** column occupies screen-left and a red **DON’T** column occupies screen-right. Add one concise item to each column at a time, preserving the equal 2×2 grid. |
| **Spoken hook** | “If your underarms get darker after shaving, do these two things—and stop doing these two.” |
| **Text-hook options** | 1. `DARK UNDERARMS? DO THIS — NOT THIS`  2. `2 UNDERARM DOS + DON’TS AFTER SHAVING`  3. `STOP MAKING UNDERARM DARKENING WORSE` |

## Script

**[0:00–0:04 — GREEN / RED SPLIT GRID; hook]**

**Spoken:** “If your underarms get darker after shaving, do these two things—and stop doing these two.”

**Overlay:** `DARK UNDERARMS? DO THIS — NOT THIS` — upper third, 0:00–0:04. Credential badge remains physically visible.

**[0:04–0:10 — Grid item 1]**

**Spoken:** “Do treat the appearance of uneven tone with ingredients like kojic acid. Don’t scrub or over-exfoliate trying to make darkness disappear—too much friction and irritation can make dark spots look worse.”

**Overlay:** Green left: `DO: TONE-CARE INGREDIENTS` → Red right: `DON’T: SCRUB / OVER-EXFOLIATE`.

**[0:10–0:16 — Grid item 2]**

**Spoken:** “Do treat your underarms with care after shaving—look for skin-conditioning ingredients like ceramide NP. Don’t put harsh exfoliating acids on freshly shaved skin, when it may already be sensitive.”

**Overlay:** Green left: `DO: POST-SHAVE CARE + CERAMIDE NP` → Red right: `DON’T: HARSH ACIDS AFTER SHAVING`.

**[0:16–0:29 — Product reveal + why it fits]**

**Spoken:** “That is why I like this Medicube deodorant for a routine like this. Its official listing says kojic acid helps improve the appearance of dullness and uneven tone, while niacinamide and vitamin C help promote brighter, smoother-looking skin. The ingredient list also includes ceramide NP.” **[Bring Medicube into frame, label facing camera.]**

**Overlay:** `MEDICUBE BODY BRIGHTENING DEODORANT` → checkmarks: `Kojic acid` → `Niacinamide + vitamin C` → `Ceramide NP`.

**[0:29–0:40 — Sensitive-underarm fit + secondary benefit]**

**Spoken:** “It is also low-irritation tested and designed with sensitive underarms in mind. And it is aluminum-free and clinically tested to help control odor for up to 24 hours. Just know it is a deodorant—not an antiperspirant—so it is not meant to stop sweat.”

**Overlay:** Sequential green checkmarks: `Low-irritation tested` → `Aluminum-free` → `24-hour odor control` → `Not an antiperspirant`.

**[0:40–0:44 — Expectations + caveat]**

**Spoken:** “Give tone care time—think weeks, not overnight—and patch test first if you are sensitive.”

**Overlay:** `EXPECT GRADUAL RESULTS` and then `PATCH TEST IF SENSITIVE`.

**[0:44–0:50 — CTA]**

**Spoken:** “If you want to check it out, I’ll leave it in the orange link down below. But if you can’t see it anymore, that means they’re all gone.” **[Physical downward point.]**

**Overlay:** `MEDICUBE BODY BRIGHTENING DEODORANT` with orange arrow down. Cart screenshot only if current listing and cart are visible at filming.

## Caption

Dark underarms after shaving are not fixed by scrubbing harder. Gentle post-shave care and tone-care ingredients can make a more thoughtful routine. @medicube_official_shop Results vary; this is general education, not personal medical advice.

# SCRIPT 51 — Physician’s Choice Digestive Enzymes
## Best-Last Digestive-Support List Test

| Field | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Funnel stage** | TOF → MOF |
| **Target runtime** | 55–65 seconds |
| **Test variable** | Best-last #5 → #1 countdown with an unresolved #1 payoff, versus the existing long-form enzyme education formats |
| **CTA ID** | U-02 — Conditional cart FOMO |
| **Product reveal** | #1, approximately 0:40–0:46 |

## Triple Hook

| Layer | Execution |
|---|---|
| **Visual hook** | Hold up five fingers beside a bold descending counter. On frame 0, a large `#5 → #1` vertical scale appears screen-left; each criterion crosses off as you progress. |
| **Spoken hook** | “I’m a pharmacist, and these are the five things I look for before I recommend a digestive enzyme—the last one is why I prefer this one.” |
| **Text-hook options** | 1. `5 THINGS I CHECK BEFORE I RECOMMEND DIGESTIVE ENZYMES`  2. `A PHARMACIST’S DIGESTIVE-ENZYME CHECKLIST`  3. `#1 IS WHAT MOST ENZYME LABELS MISS` |

## Script

**[0:00–0:06 — Countdown promise]**

**Spoken:** “I’m a pharmacist, and these are the five things I look for before I recommend a digestive enzyme—the last one is why I prefer this one.”

**Overlay:** `5 THINGS I CHECK BEFORE I RECOMMEND DIGESTIVE ENZYMES` — upper third, 0:00–0:06. Left-side countdown scale begins at `#5`.

**[0:06–0:14 — #5]**

**Spoken:** “Number five: it needs to match more than one type of meal. If dairy bothers you, you need lactase. If beans and broccoli get you, you need alpha-galactosidase—the same enzyme that’s in Beano.”

**Overlay:** `#5 MATCH THE FOOD` → icons: dairy / beans / broccoli; then `LACTASE + ALPHA-GALACTOSIDASE`.

**[0:14–0:22 — #4]**

**Spoken:** “Number four: it needs broad coverage. Protein, carbs, fats, plant fiber—not one single enzyme that only covers one piece of the meal.”

**Overlay:** `#4 BROAD COVERAGE` → `Protein • Carbs • Fats • Fiber`.

**[0:22–0:30 — #3]**

**Spoken:** “Number three: the directions have to make sense. Enzymes work with food, so this one is taken with your largest meal—not as a rescue pill after you already feel miserable.”

**Overlay:** `#3 TAKE WITH YOUR LARGEST MEAL` → plate icon and a green check. Avoid a clock claim.

**[0:30–0:38 — #2; pharmacist safety flag]**

**Spoken:** “Number two: I check what else you take. This formula has bromelain, so if you take a blood thinner or daily aspirin, ask your pharmacist before you start it.”

**Overlay:** `#2 CHECK INTERACTIONS FIRST` → `Blood thinner or daily aspirin? Ask your pharmacist.`

**[0:38–0:50 — #1 + product reveal]**

**Spoken:** “But number one is the label has to do more than just digest food. This Physician’s Choice formula has 16 enzymes, plus prebiotics and a named probiotic strain called BS50. That strain has its own published clinical trial in healthy adults for bloating and burping symptoms.” **[Hold up product at “Physician’s Choice.”]**

**Overlay:** `#1 MORE THAN ENZYMES` → checkmarks stack: `16 enzymes` → `Prebiotics` → `BS50® probiotic`. Flash [PMID 36269141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36269141/) title screenshot during “published clinical trial.”

**[0:50–0:57 — realistic expectation]**

**Spoken:** “It’s not a weight-loss supplement, and it won’t fix every cause of stomach symptoms. But if mealtime bloating is what you’re trying to support, that is the checklist I want to see.”

**Overlay:** `NOT A WEIGHT-LOSS SUPPLEMENT` → `MEALTIME SUPPORT, NOT A DIAGNOSIS`.

**[0:57–1:04 — CTA]**

**Spoken:** “If you want to check it out, I’ll leave it in the orange link down below. But if you can’t see it anymore, that means they’re all gone.” **[Physical downward point.]**

**Overlay:** `PHYSICIAN’S CHOICE DIGESTIVE ENZYMES` with orange arrow down. Keep #1 badge visible on screen during CTA.
