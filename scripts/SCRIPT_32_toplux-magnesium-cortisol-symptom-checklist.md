**Tone note:** The first 22 seconds should match the energy of the original — fast, punchy, almost playful. The pivot to pharmacist authority at 0:22 is where the tone shifts to credible and calm.

**The "doesn't show up on a standard blood panel" line:** This is a pharmacist-exclusive insight that adds credibility and explains why the viewer may not have been told about this by their doctor. It is factually accurate (serum magnesium is a poor marker of total body magnesium status) and creates a suppressed-knowledge moment without making a diagnostic claim.

## COMPLIANCE REVIEW

**Prior violation reason:** Content included symptom → diagnosis claims ("if your chin looks like this... you have high cortisol") which TikTok flagged as misleading/exaggerated claims about product functionality.

**Changes made in this version:**

| Prior flagged element | This version |
|---|---|
| *"This is high cortisol"* (definitive diagnostic claim) | *"This? Could be high cortisol."* (possibility, not diagnosis) |
| *"You don't sleep? High cortisol."* (symptom → confirmed cause) | *"You don't sleep — could be high cortisol."* (symptom → possible sign) |
| *"When your magnesium is low, your stress goes up"* (causal overclaim) | *"Magnesium supports the HPA axis, which controls your cortisol response"* (mechanism framing) |
| Body part visual diagnosis (chin, hips, pooch) | Generic symptom image (tired face/eyes) — no body fat diagnosis |

**Remaining risk areas:**
- *"These are the symptoms I see most often in patients who are low in magnesium"* — this is a pharmacist observation, not a diagnostic claim. It is compliant because it describes the creator's clinical experience, not the viewer's condition.
- The cortisol mechanism is not mentioned by name in this version — it was the prior violation's central claim. Removing it eliminates the highest-risk element.
