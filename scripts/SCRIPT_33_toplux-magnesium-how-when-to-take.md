**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**So they shoved magnesium down your throat and told you it was going to fix everything — but then they didn't tell you how to take it. Well, I'm a pharmacist, and I'm going to fix that.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:13]**I've been counseling patients on supplements for over a decade. And the number one question I get after someone buys magnesium is: when and do I take it with food.

**[ANSWER — TIMING — 0:13–0:30]**The best time to take magnesium is at night — 30 to 60 minutes before bed. magnesium glycinate, which is the sleep form, works by activating glycine receptors in the brain that lower your core body temperature. That temperature drop is one of the signals your body uses to initiate sleep. If you take it in the morning, you are wasting the most important benefit of the glycinate form. Night is when it does its job.

**[ANSWER — FOOD — 0:30–0:42]**Take it with food or a small snack — not on an empty stomach. Magnesium oxide and magnesium citrate, which are both in this complex, can cause loose stools or stomach cramping if taken without food. The food slows the absorption slightly, which actually reduces the GI side effects without meaningfully reducing the benefit. If you have ever tried magnesium and stopped because it upset your stomach, this is probably why.

**[PHARMACIST FLAG — INTERACTIONS — 0:42–0:55]**One thing to note that commonly gets missed: if you are taking an iron supplement, do not take it at the same time as your magnesium. They compete for absorption — meaning you are reducing the benefit of both. Separate them by at least two hours. Take your iron in the morning, your magnesium at night.

Non-GMO, vegan, and it is one of the few complexes that actually includes orotate, which is the form used in European cardiology research for cardiac energy metabolism. That is not something you find in most supplements.
