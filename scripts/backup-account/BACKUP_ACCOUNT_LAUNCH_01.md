# Backup Account Launch Video 1

**Series:** Ask a Pharmacist  
**Funnel stage:** Audience-building / education-first  
**Target runtime:** 35–45 seconds  
**Product / link:** None  
**Working title:** The first thing I need to know before I answer an OTC question

## Triple Hook

| Hook type | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Start in white coat, holding four ordinary medicine / vitamin / supplement containers with their labels turned away. Bring them into frame immediately, then set them down as the question lands. |
| **Spoken** | “Before I answer what you can take for sleep, pain, allergies, or a cold, I need to know one thing: what else are you taking?” |
| **Text** | **The 1 question before any OTC answer** |

### Text-Hook Options

1. **Question:** “What else are you taking?”
2. **Provocative statement:** “The bottle in your hand is not the whole story.”
3. **Stakes framing:** “One missing detail can change the OTC answer.”

**Use for filming:** Option 1. Show at 0:00; animate out at 3 seconds.

---

## Spoken Script

**0:00–0:05 — Hook**  
“Before I answer what you can take for sleep, pain, allergies, or a cold, I need to know one thing: **what else are you taking?**”

**0:05–0:10 — Authority**  
“I've been a pharmacist for over a decade, and that question is not me being difficult—it is how I get the whole picture before I try to help.”

**0:10–0:20 — Problem**  
“Most people only show the box in their hand. But prescriptions, over-the-counter products, vitamins, supplements, and even something you only use once in a while can all matter to the conversation.”

**0:20–0:31 — Practical education**  
“And here is the part most people do not realize: two different-looking boxes can sometimes overlap, or a supplement can be important context your pharmacist needs before giving you a useful answer.”

**0:31–0:39 — Action + safety boundary**  
“So before your next pharmacy-counter question, keep a list—or take clear photos—of everything you take and bring it with you. A real answer needs the whole picture, not just one bottle.”

**0:39–0:44 — Follow CTA**  
“Follow for the pharmacy-counter questions people should have been able to ask.”

---

## Filming and Overlay Plan

| Timing | Visual / on-screen direction |
|---|---|
| **0:00** | Title-card flash: **ASK A PHARMACIST**. Then immediate text hook: **“The 1 question before any OTC answer.”** Hold four unlabeled / turned-away containers in frame. |
| **0:03** | Text hook fades out. Add small lower-third: **Licensed Pharmacist • 10+ years**. |
| **0:10** | Pop-up sequence, one every 2 seconds: **Prescription** → **OTC** → **Vitamin** → **Supplement** → **“Sometimes-used products count too.”** |
| **0:20** | Split visual: two generic, different-looking boxes with a center overlay: **“Different box ≠ different ingredients.”** Do not name specific medicines. |
| **0:31** | Hold phone with a blurred / generic medication-list note. Overlay: **“Bring the whole list.”** |
| **0:39** | Bottom-screen follow graphic: **“Pharmacy-counter questions, explained.”** Point downward to the graphic while giving the follow CTA. |

**Props:** White coat, four generic containers with labels facing away, phone showing a blank / mocked medication-list note.  
**Performance:** Direct and calm; no “hey guys” introduction. Treat the opening question as if the viewer is at your pharmacy counter.  
**Visual strategy:** Minimal education-first variation of Strategy E—fast label cards instead of product or study walls.

# Backup Account Launch Video 2

**Series:** Pharmacy Counter Basics  
**Funnel stage:** Audience-building / education-first  
**Target runtime:** 40–50 seconds  
**Product / link:** None  
**Working title:** Five things I wish every patient told their pharmacist

## Triple Hook

| Hook type | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Start in white coat with a clipboard showing five large, blank checkboxes. Check the first box as the hook lands. |
| **Spoken** | “There are five things I wish every patient told their pharmacist before asking, ‘What can I take for this?’” |
| **Text** | **5 things your pharmacist needs to know** |

### Text-Hook Options

1. **Question:** “What should you tell a pharmacist first?”
2. **Provocative statement:** “The question is not the whole story.”
3. **Stakes framing:** “These 5 details can change the conversation.”

**Use for filming:** Option 3. Show at 0:00; animate out at 3 seconds.

---

## Spoken Script

**0:00–0:05 — Hook**  
“There are five things I wish every patient told their pharmacist before asking, ‘What can I take for this?’”

**0:05–0:10 — Authority**  
“I've been a pharmacist for over a decade, and these little details are how I get past a quick guess and into a useful conversation.”

**0:10–0:29 — Five-part checklist**  
“First: every prescription you take—even the one you only use sometimes.  
Second: anything over the counter you already tried today.  
Third: vitamins, supplements, or herbal products.  
Fourth: if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or managing a health condition.  
And fifth: what changed—because a new symptom, a new medicine, or a new routine is important context.”

**0:29–0:40 — Practical action + safety boundary**  
“You do not need to memorize all of that. Keep a simple note in your phone, or bring the bottles with you. The more complete the picture, the more useful your pharmacist can be.”

**0:40–0:46 — Follow CTA**  
“Follow for the pharmacy-counter questions people should have been able to ask.”

---

## Filming and Overlay Plan

| Timing | Visual / on-screen direction |
|---|---|
| **0:00** | Title-card flash: **PHARMACY COUNTER BASICS**. Large hook: **“5 things your pharmacist needs to know.”** Hold clipboard with five checkboxes. |
| **0:03** | Large hook fades. Add lower-third: **Licensed Pharmacist • 10+ years**. |
| **0:10–0:29** | Check one clipboard box per item. Use a new simple icon / text overlay every 3–4 seconds: **Prescription** → **OTC today** → **Vitamins + supplements** → **Pregnancy / breastfeeding / health conditions** → **What changed?** |
| **0:29** | Hold phone showing a blank note titled **“My medication list.”** Overlay: **“You do not have to memorize it.”** |
| **0:40** | Bottom-screen follow graphic: **“Pharmacy-counter questions, explained.”** Point downward while giving the follow CTA. |

**Props:** White coat, clipboard, marker, phone with a generic blank note.  
**Performance:** Brisk but not rushed; pause for half a beat between each checklist item so viewers can save the video.  
**Visual strategy:** Checklist-driven Strategy E variation; each item gets one clean visual interrupt.

# Backup Account Launch Video 3

**Series:** Read the Label With Me  
**Funnel stage:** Audience-building / education-first  
**Target runtime:** 35–45 seconds  
**Product / link:** None  
**Working title:** The first line I check before I buy a cold medicine

## Triple Hook

| Hook type | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | Start in white coat holding a generic cold-medicine box with the front covered by your hand. On “this line,” turn it around and point to a mocked / blurred **Active ingredients** panel. |
| **Spoken** | “Before you buy a cold medicine, find this line first: **active ingredients.**” |
| **Text** | **Find this before you buy cold medicine** |

### Text-Hook Options

1. **Question:** “What is the first line to check on a cold-medicine label?”
2. **Provocative statement:** “The front of the box is not the answer.”
3. **Stakes framing:** “Do not buy cold medicine before checking this line.”

**Use for filming:** Option 2. Show at 0:00; animate out at 3 seconds.

---

## Spoken Script

**0:00–0:05 — Hook**  
“Before you buy a cold medicine, find this line first: **active ingredients.**”

**0:05–0:10 — Authority**  
“I'm a pharmacist, and I have been checking this line with patients for over a decade because the front of the box is designed to get your attention—the label tells you what you are actually considering.”

**0:10–0:22 — Problem**  
“Words like daytime, nighttime, maximum, or severe can tell you the marketing angle. But the active-ingredients panel is where you start the real pharmacy question: what is inside, and is any of it already in something you took?”

**0:22–0:33 — Practical education**  
“You do not need to become a label expert. Just find that line, take a clear picture, and compare it with the other things you have used today before you ask your pharmacist for help.”

**0:33–0:41 — Follow CTA**  
“Follow for the label details that make pharmacy-counter questions easier.”

---

## Filming and Overlay Plan

| Timing | Visual / on-screen direction |
|---|---|
| **0:00** | Title-card flash: **READ THE LABEL**. Large hook: **“The front of the box is not the answer.”** Cover a generic / mock cold-medicine box front with your hand. |
| **0:03** | Turn the box around; large text fades. Add lower-third: **Licensed Pharmacist • 10+ years**. |
| **0:05** | Zoom or punch in on a mocked / blurred panel with only **ACTIVE INGREDIENTS** clearly readable. Do not use a brand name. |
| **0:10** | On-screen words pop up one at a time: **Daytime** → **Nighttime** → **Maximum** → **Severe**. Then crossfade to: **“Marketing words.”** |
| **0:18** | Return to the active-ingredients panel. Overlay: **“What is inside?”** then **“Already in something else?”** |
| **0:25** | Hold up phone camera aimed at the generic label. Overlay: **“Take a clear photo.”** |
| **0:33** | Bottom-screen follow graphic: **“Read the label with me.”** Point downward during the CTA. |

**Props:** Generic or self-made mock cold-medicine label; avoid a brand name, product link, or visible product recommendation.  
**Performance:** Confident and helpful; the “front of the box” line should feel like an insider correction, not a scare tactic.  
**Visual strategy:** Label-reading Strategy E variation—high contrast label close-ups and text annotations every 3–5 seconds.

---

## Caption

The first line I check is not the biggest promise on the front of the box. It is the active-ingredients panel.
