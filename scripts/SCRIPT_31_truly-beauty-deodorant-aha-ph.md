## SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:07]**
Nobody talks about this — but the reason AHA deodorants actually control odor is completely different from what the brand says. And once you understand the mechanism, you will never look at deodorant the same way.

**[AUTHORITY — 0:07–0:15]**
I'm a pharmacist. I have been counseling patients on skincare and topical products for over a decade. And when I looked at the ingredient label on this one, there was a mechanism here that I recognized immediately — and that the brand is not explaining.

**[PROBLEM — 0:15–0:30]**
Most people think deodorant works by masking odor — either with fragrance or with aluminum that blocks sweat. But the odor itself is not coming from sweat. Sweat is odorless. The odor comes from bacteria — specifically Corynebacterium and Staphylococcus — that live on your skin and break down sweat into the compounds that smell. Those bacteria thrive in a specific environment. And here is where the AHA comes in.

**[REHOOK — 0:30–0:35]**
Because the AHA in this formula is not just exfoliating. It is changing the environment those bacteria need to survive.

**[MECHANISM — 0:35–1:02]**
AHAs — alpha-hydroxy acids — are acids. When you apply them to your skin, they lower the pH of the skin surface. Normal underarm skin sits at a pH of around 6 to 7. Odor-causing bacteria — Corynebacterium and Staphylococcus — thrive in that neutral-to-slightly-alkaline range. When the AHA brings the pH down to the 3.5 to 4.5 range, that environment becomes hostile to those bacteria. They cannot grow as effectively. So the lactic acid and mandelic acid in this formula are doing two jobs simultaneously: exfoliating the dead pigmented skin cells that cause visible darkening, and acidifying the skin surface to inhibit the bacteria that cause odor. The brand markets this as a brightening serum deodorant. The odor control mechanism is real — it is just not the story they are telling.

**[GAP — 1:02–1:12]**
Standard aluminum antiperspirants stop odor by blocking sweat entirely — they plug the sweat glands. This formula does not block sweat. It changes the environment so the bacteria that produce odor cannot thrive. That is a fundamentally different approach. And for people who want to move away from aluminum, this is the mechanism that replaces it.

**[PRODUCT REVEAL — 1:12–1:27]**
The product is Truly Beauty Soft Serve Serum Deodorant. It has a 5% AHA blend — lactic acid and mandelic acid. Mandelic acid specifically is the gentler of the two — it has a larger molecular size, so it penetrates more slowly and is better tolerated on darker skin tones, where AHA can sometimes cause post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation if it is too aggressive. It also has niacinamide and vitamin C, which work on the melanin transfer pathway — so you are getting exfoliation, pigment transfer inhibition, and odor control in one formula.

**[BRAND CLOSE + OBJECTION HANDLE — 1:27–1:42]**
One important caveat — do not apply this immediately after shaving. AHA on freshly shaved skin can sting and cause irritation. Wait a few hours, or apply at night after your skin has had time to recover. Also — if you have sensitive skin, this is not the right pick. The AHA concentration is effective but it is not designed for reactive skin. And this is not an antiperspirant — it will not stop sweating. It manages odor through the pH mechanism I just described.

**[CTA — 1:42–1:52]**
I will link it below. If you are trying to move away from aluminum and want something that actually treats your skin while it works, this is worth a look. *(point down)*
